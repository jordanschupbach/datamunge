    Historical MuJoCo sorting benchmark

    This is the historical MuJoCo 3.4.0 experiment and its retained results. The standard datamunge packages now use the in-house engine. To rerun this reference, build nix build .#datamunge-mujoco and supply its lib/pkgconfig directory via DATAMUNGE_CAD_PKG_CONFIG_PATH when executing this block. The current native experiment is in playground.

    Robot sorting with articulated contact physics and native RL

    This benchmark compares *Q(λ), Double DQN, dueling Double DQN and clipped PPO* using datamunge's native C++ learning backend. It selects a method and checkpoint on independent validation streams, freezes that choice, then evaluates it on held-out streams and renders a *20-second example* of the selected controller. The former proxy-trained controller is evaluated as a historical transfer reference and remains eligible for deployment if it wins independent validation. A neural controller is not guaranteed to win.

    The task and observation

    Fruit enters a continuously moving conveyor under an independent *Poisson process*, with random position, orientation, size, color and six shape prototypes per class. The demonstration and evaluation rate is *0.12 fruit/s*. The belt runs at *0.22 m/s*; fruit passing X=0.30 m unhandled is missed. Apples belong in the red bin and bananas in the yellow bin. Outcomes require settling with bin floor support. Completed fruit clears after two seconds; arrivals at the eight-body capacity count as misses. Fruit still moving, held or falling at an exact measurement boundary remains pending.

    Every pipeline chooses among the same *five variable-duration options*: wait, intercept the oldest fruit at either of two gates, or intercept the second-oldest fruit at either gate. Feasible-action masks exclude absent targets, blocked approaches and expired interception deadlines. Fixed IK options request approach, descent, finger closure, transfer and release; *MuJoCo integrates the actual motion*. A grasp requires sustained force from both fingers. Fruit remains a free dynamic body during transport and can slip or fall. Releases preserve velocity. Success requires bin-floor contact, low linear/angular speed and a continuous 0.2-second settling interval. Empty-queue waits yield every 0.125 seconds while the actual wrist turns toward the infeed.

    Physics audit and numerical checks

    The previous simulation interpolated robot joints, attached fruit before finger closure, carried it kinematically and zeroed release velocity. Its fast training proxy further approximated conveyor transport and settling. Those mechanisms could reward behavior that would fail under contact dynamics; earlier performance figures are historical proxy-task results.

    This experiment uses *MuJoCo 3.4.0*, with the pinned Seeed URDF's joint origins, axes, limits, link masses, centers of mass and inertia tensors. Official collision meshes become convex hulls; each concave finger pad is split into smaller hulls to retain its opening. Apples use ellipsoids and bananas compound tapered capsules. Gravity, joint coupling, self-collision except adjacent links, fruit rotation, friction, contact forces and actuator effort limits are integrated. The moving belt acts through tangential contact constraints; fruit velocity is not assigned to belt speed. Rendering and the wrist camera use measured poses.

    The task clock is 240 Hz and integration runs at *960 Hz* with full implicit-in-velocity integration, elliptic friction cones and an iterative contact solver. Position references drive force-limited rate servos. Requested rates are capped at 1.5 rad/s and 0.08 m/s, but *actual rates can exceed them* under contact and backdriving. The report records actual peak rates, penetration and effort-limit usage instead of treating requested rates as measured hard limits.

    Unit checks cover free fall, momentum, rotation, moving-belt traction, bin support, spawn/reset invariance and agreement between dynamics and rendered forward kinematics. The 960 and 1920 Hz development runs have the same aggregate task outcomes; contact trajectories and maximum penetration are not monotonic in the step size. MuJoCo can enlarge the contact time constant at coarse timesteps for stability, so this is a practical sensitivity check rather than a fixed-equation convergence proof. Learned-policy streams below are separate from this development check. A torque-free check also shows numerical damping: about 0.08% energy loss over two seconds at 5.4 rad/s initial spin, but roughly 50% at an extreme 269 rad/s. Stable rapid-tumbling trajectories are therefore not claimed to be accurate.

    This is a *nominal rigid-body model, not a hardware-calibrated digital twin*. Friction, damping, reflected motor inertia, finger mass and servo gains need measurement. Fruit compliance, damage, motor electronics, latency, camera noise and manufacturing tolerances remain approximations. The published hardware flyer and pinned URDF also differ in some limits. Mesh fidelity alone cannot resolve that revision uncertainty. See the physics audit for model assumptions, references and calibration requirements.

    The tabular methods use the existing *1,680-state abstraction*. The neural methods instead receive *25 bounded features*: queue/release counts, arm and finger pose, tool position, the first two targets' presence/position/lateral position/age, and the five feasibility flags. Neither representation includes true fruit labels, future arrivals or privileged prototype identities. Tracking and IK still use simulator geometry. This compares complete representation and learning pipelines, rather than isolating network architecture alone. The task learns scheduling and interception, rather than joint torques or visual detection. These features omit instantaneous velocities and detailed contact state, so the scheduler has a partial observation of the physical system. Fixed feedback skills use measured poses; this is not presented as a fully observed physical MDP.

    The shared reward is *5 correct − 8 wrong − 6 missed*, minus 0.02 per elapsed second and 0.25 per failed attempt. Waiting with eligible fruit costs another 0.5 per second. *All RL training and evaluation use the articulated contact backend*, with no analytic transport or ideal attachment. Training and checkpoint screening use a class-conditional synthetic prediction-error channel, calibrated on independent wrist-camera validation crops. Final selection and held-out tests use actual RGB inference. This remaining perception approximation is explicit; training is not end-to-end image-based control.

    Why these learning methods

    *Q(λ)* supplies a compact baseline with replacing Watkins eligibility traces (decay 0.8). *Double DQN* separates online action selection from target-network valuation, using uniform replay, Huber loss and hard target updates. van Hasselt, Guez and Silver (2016). *Dueling Double DQN* adds separate value and centered advantage heads, which can share state-value information across interception choices. Wang et al. (2016). *PPO* learns a masked categorical actor and value function with a clipped probability ratio, entropy bonus and generalized advantage estimates. Schulman et al. (2017). These methods fit the current discrete option interface. Continuous-control methods in examples/nn/6_deep_reinforcement_learning would require a different motor-action benchmark.

    The neural models share two 32-unit hidden layers: ReLU for Q networks, tanh for PPO. Native tensors, autograd and Adam implement all learning. DQN uses learning rate 0.0005, replay capacity 32,768, batch 64, warm-up 512, an update every eight composite decisions and a target copy every 128 updates. PPO uses learning rate 0.0003, batches of 64, up to 256 rollout decisions, four shuffled epochs, clipping radius 0.2, GAE decay 0.95, value coefficient 0.5 and entropy coefficient 0.01. PPO also flushes at each window boundary. Q(λ) uses step size 0.08; Q methods anneal epsilon from 0.35 to 0.01. All use a gradient-norm limit of 10 where applicable.

    The discount is *0.995 raised to actual elapsed seconds*. Up to eight trailing forced-action steps can be composed with exact discounted rewards; every physics step and arrival still runs. Terminal states stop bootstrapping, while time-limit boundaries bootstrap from the actual final observation and cut traces across resets. Saved NPZ files contain complete inference models; optimizer/replay state is not saved for training resumption.

    Comparison and selection protocol

    Each fresh method trains *256 independent windows of at least 120 simulated seconds*, with the *same reset-seed schedule* and arrival-rate curriculum uniform on 0.08–0.30 fruit/s. A final option may overshoot the budget; the history records actual simulated time and update counts. Initialization is deterministic and method-specific. This first comparison uses *one training seed per method* and fixed settings, not a broad hyperparameter search or a statistical claim of universal algorithm superiority.

    Checkpoints at 128 and 256 windows are screened on *12 common full-physics streams* with synthetic perception. The top two checkpoints per fresh method are evaluated on *six separate common actual-RGB validation streams*. The method/checkpoint with the largest *5 correct − 8 wrong − 6 missed* validation score wins. Checkpoint score ties prefer the earlier checkpoint; method ties prefer fewer wrong placements, then more correct placements, then the earlier listed method. Evaluation takes the highest-valued feasible action (highest actor logit for PPO), with no exploration. All counts are taken at the exact 120-second boundary.

    The old proxy-trained controller is tested using the same current vision, motors, options, arrivals and score. It previously trained 32,768 windows and selected window 256; its larger training/search budget and training physics are explicitly different. Its larger historical budget is shown separately from the matched fresh comparison. The selection is frozen *before* testing all five candidates on *16 new common two-minute actual-RGB streams*. Screening seeds start at 63,000,000,000, validation at 64,000,000,000 and test at 65,000,000,000; these do not overlap with training, vision datasets or the fixed video seed. Held-out results never change the selected controller. Wrong placements and pending fruit are retained in the raw outcomes, alongside correct sorts and misses.

    The fixed validation rule selects *dueling Double DQN, window 256*. On the sixteen held-out streams it records *111 correct, 8 wrong, 73 missed and 15 pending* from 207 arrivals. The historical controller records 103 correct, 7 wrong, 84 missed and 13 pending on those same streams. These are results for the new contact task; the earlier attached-payload scores are not directly comparable. Physical grasp and transfer failures are now represented explicitly.

    Frozen perception and realistic rendering

    The *256×256 rigid wrist camera*, with nominal intrinsics and extrinsics, supplies rendered RGB. Registered background subtraction finds visible components, and tracked geometry associates the intended target. MobileNet receives only the unannotated padded RGB crop; its prediction alone chooses red or yellow. Empty crops remain unknown, with no true-class fallback. Video bounding boxes and labels are overlays, never inputs. The previous classifier initializes a new *12-epoch adaptation* to moving views from the contact-driven task: 256 training crops from prototypes 0–3, 64 validation crops from prototype 4 and 64 test crops from prototype 5. These holdouts apply to this adaptation; initial weights retain their previous simulation-training history. The frozen adaptation checkpoint was collected during physical-model development before the arrival-clearance guard; its source fingerprint is retained with the audit. All reported controller training and evaluation use the final guard. Validation cross entropy selects the checkpoint. Validation errors, with Laplace smoothing, set the training prediction channel; test accuracy is reported only after selection. The resulting classifier is *frozen across all RL pipelines*.

    The exterior retains imported Seeed meshes, joint-following cables, procedural fruit skins, factory scenery and distinct metal, rubber and painted materials. CUDA can render the overview with 2×2 antialiasing, soft shadows and approximate studio reflections. The wrist inset and classifier keep the CPU sensor renderer. Geometry tracking, fixed IK skills, nominal contact parameters and automatic bin clearing remain benchmark assumptions. This is simulated scheduling and interception with contact dynamics, not hardware-ready end-to-end control.

    Complete executable comparison and demonstration

    just playground trains the four native pipelines, compares them, freezes the winner, evaluates and renders it. It adapts the prior wrist classifier to measured pickup views and evaluates the old controller as a transfer baseline, copying the bundled initial assets on a clean run. Native asset fetching verifies/downloads the robot CAD as needed. DATAMUNGE_STREAM_LEARN_ONLY=1 just playground stops after comparison. just playground-preview reloads the completed comparison; just playground-gpu uses the CUDA renderer for this frozen replay. Replay validates the protocol, backend sources, classifier and selected checkpoint hashes and fails on stale inputs rather than silently using an older run. When changing the comparison settings in this program, bump its protocol string; that string versions the training/selection settings while backend-source hashes track core changes.
