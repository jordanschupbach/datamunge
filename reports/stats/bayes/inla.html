    INLA: Conditional Gaussians and Hyperparameter Mixing

    Abstract

    Observations may depend on latent effects whose prior scales are themselves unknown. Inference must account for uncertainty in both the effects and those scales, often without a tractable posterior integral. Integrated nested Laplace approximation replaces difficult conditional posteriors with local approximations and integrates low-dimensional hyperparameters numerically. We study this library's dense Gaussian strategy through an exact three-observation normal model, a skew Bernoulli posterior, and an explicit Gaussian mixture over uncertain precision. Grid refinement and a Palmer penguins mixed model expose numerical and prior sensitivity. A separate multiple-testing appendix preserves the original p-value example with its rejection counts corrected; it is not part of Bayesian posterior inference.

    The method

    Specify the latent Gaussian model and the approximation

    Let \(x\in\mathbb R^p\) be the latent field, \(\theta\in\mathbb R^d\) its hyperparameters, and \(D\) the observation design. The model is

    \[\eta=Dx,\qquad x\mid\theta\sim N(0,Q(\theta)^{-1}),\qquad p(x,\theta\mid y)\propto p(\theta)|Q(\theta)|^{1/2} \exp(-x^TQ(\theta)x/2)\prod_i p(y_i\mid\eta_i,\theta).\]

    The likelihood callback supplies its log density, score \(s_i=\partial\log p_i/\partial\eta_i\), and nonnegative curvature \(c_i=-\partial^2\log p_i/\partial\eta_i^2\). At fixed \(\theta\), the gradient and negative Hessian in the latent field are

    \[g=D^Ts-Qx,\qquad H=Q+D^T\operatorname{diag}(c_i)D,\qquad x_{\mathrm{next}}=x+\alpha H^{-1}g.\]

    Positive-definite \(Q\) and nonnegative curvature make \(H\) positive definite, so the conditional log posterior is strictly concave. A stationary point, if attained, is its unique mode. The solver uses Cholesky solves and step halving. It stops on a small proposed Newton step relative to \(1+\|x\|_\infty\); exhaustion or a failed line search is rejected. The final score norm is exposed, but numerical stopping alone does not quantify the Gaussian approximation error.

    At the conditional mode \(m(\theta)\), use \(x\mid y,\theta\approx N(m,H^{-1})\). This is exact for a normal likelihood with linear predictor and known hyperparameters. For a non-Gaussian likelihood, the mode usually differs from the actual conditional posterior mean.

    The Laplace ratio and the outer integral

    The normalized Gaussian prior contributes \(|Q|^{1/2}(2\pi)^{-p/2}\). Integrating the local Gaussian contributes \((2\pi)^{p/2}|H|^{-1/2}\), so those \(2\pi\) factors cancel. Thus the approximate log hyperposterior integrand is

    \[\ell(\theta)=\log p(\theta)+\frac12\log|Q(\theta)| +\sum_i\log p(y_i\mid D_im,\theta)-\frac12m^TQ(\theta)m -\frac12\log|H(\theta)|.\]

    A seeded differential-evolution search finds a candidate mode inside a declared box; it is not a global-optimality certificate. Finite differences estimate hyperparameter curvature there. Its inverse determines a rotated, equally spaced grid in curvature-SD coordinates. If the hyper-Hessian is unusable, the recorded fallback uses diagonal covariance equal to one sixteenth of each box width squared. Small covariance eigenvalues are floored for numerical grid construction.

    For one hyperparameter, cells are clipped to the box and evaluated at their clipped midpoints, with their actual widths. In higher dimensions, rotated cells retain a constant volume and outside-box centers are skipped: boundary quadrature is coarser and needs separate refinement. All retained conditional fits must converge. The reported skipped-point count can include both outside support and failed evaluations; inspect the model and sensitivity rather than interpreting it as a numerical error bound.

    Combine moments without confusing them with a single Gaussian

    With quadrature cell measures \(\Delta_k\), let \(w_k=\exp(\ell_k)\Delta_k/\sum_l\exp(\ell_l)\Delta_l\). Then

    \[\bar m_j=\sum_kw_km_{kj},\qquad V_j=\underbrace{\sum_kw_k[H_k^{-1}]_{jj}}_{\text{within}} +\underbrace{\sum_kw_k(m_{kj}-\bar m_j)^2}_{\text{between}},\qquad \widetilde p(x_j\mid y)=\sum_kw_k\phi(x_j;m_{kj},[H_k^{-1}]_{jj}).\]

    The between term is nonnegative. This proves total variance exceeds its own weighted within term, not that it exceeds the variance of a different fit evaluated only at the hyperparameter mode. Empirical Bayes (EB) retains that single conditional fit, sets theta uncertainty and between variance to zero, and omits the outer integral. Its reported integrated evidence is therefore NaN. With a normalized likelihood, proper normalized priors and intentional integration support, the grid's \(\log\sum_k\exp(\ell_k)\Delta_k\) approximates log evidence. An arbitrary constant improper prior makes its scale arbitrary.

    This implementation follows the *Gaussian latent-marginal strategy*: it does not perform INLA's simplified/full marginal Laplace skew correction, sparse GMRF algebra, adaptive integration or exact posterior sampling. Conditional Newton work is \(O(np^2+p^3)\) per iteration; retaining \(K\) covariance matrices costs \(O(Kp^2)\). Tensor-grid cost grows as \(K=m^d\), so Grid caps \(d\) at four and candidates at one million. These are numerical limits, not promises of practical performance at those extremes.

    Small worked examples

    Three normal observations: every conditional calculation is exact

    Take \(y=(1,2,3)\), \(y_i\mid x\sim N(x,1)\), and \(x\sim N(0,1)\). The likelihood as a function of \(x\) is proportional to \(N(2,1/3)\). Completing the square gives posterior precision \(1+3=4\), mean \((1+2+3)/4=1.5\), and variance \(1/4\). Starting at zero, the Newton score is six, so the first step is \(6/4=1.5\); the next proposed step is zero.

    The normalized evidence is also hand-checkable. The marginal covariance of \(y\) is \(I+\mathbf1\mathbf1^T\), with determinant four and inverse \(I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^T/4\), giving \(\log p(y)=-3\log(2\pi)/2-\log(4)/2-5/2\). The normalized likelihood-shaped curve below is an aid to reading the completed square; it is not itself a posterior.

    Four Bernoulli outcomes: mode and mean separate

    Now take \(y=(1,1,1,0)\), a logit link, and \(x\sim N(0,4)\). The score is \(3-4\operatorname{logistic}(x)-x/4\), and the negative Hessian is \(4p(1-p)+1/4\). At zero they equal one and 1.25, so the first Newton proposal is 0.8. The converged mode and local variance describe a Gaussian approximation, not the exact posterior mean and spread.

    inla_scalar_reference instead integrates the actual scalar likelihood times its normal prior on a dense finite midpoint grid. It has quadrature and tail truncation error, so its window, resolution and edge mass are explicit. We compare small and larger samples with the same exact success fraction three quarters. Improving agreement along this sequence is evidence for these inputs, not a universal monotonic error theorem. A small final Newton score cannot detect the skew error.

    Unknown precision: integrate conditional normals rather than only their mode

    Return to \(y=(1,2,3)\) with unit observation noise, but now use \(x\mid\theta\sim N(0,e^{-\theta})\) and \(\theta\sim N(0,1)\). The fitted theta coordinate is *log precision*. Its prior is already a density on that scale; substituting a prior density for precision itself would require a Jacobian. We restrict the numerical integration box to [-6,6], where the standard-normal prior has negligible omitted mass, and use a five-curvature-SD grid span.

    At each theta the exact conditional variance is \(v_k=(3+e^{\theta_k})^{-1}\) and mean is \(m_k=6v_k\). Thus conditional Gaussian approximation error vanishes in this example. The remaining difference from the full posterior comes from mode search and numerical hyperparameter integration. EB freezes \(\theta\) at its selected mode. The integrated marginal can be skew even though every component is normal; replacing it with a normal having the final mean and SD would lose that shape.

    Observed data: Palmer penguins

    The bundled 344-row dataset includes sex, body mass, species and island. Retain rows complete in sex, body_mass_g and island, giving 333 observations. Define male as one and use centered body mass in kilograms, \(\text{mass}=\text{body_mass_g}/1000-4.2\). Fit male ~ mass + (1 | island), retaining the original report's random-intercept structure while making units and priors explicit. This is a descriptive fit to all complete rows; there is no held-out prediction-performance claim.

    Fixed coefficients receive independent \(N(0,2.5^2)\) priors on these centered units. The island random-intercept SD \(\lambda\) has a normalized uniform prior on [0.01,3]. The latter is a density on SD itself, not on variance or log SD. General random-slope models use independent uniform priors on packed covariance-factor entries, not independent uniform variance/correlation priors. With three islands, prior support for an SD matters and will be checked separately.

    Species composition differs sharply by island; the formula omits species. Consequently an island coefficient can absorb species composition and other omitted structure. It should not be read as a causal island effect. Neither descriptive fitting accuracy nor a local Gaussian interval resolves that model limitation.

    The interval endpoints come from the actual retained Gaussian mixture CDF, not a symmetric mean-plus-or-minus-two-SD shortcut. They remain intervals of an approximation. variance_components_frame() transforms each grid point before computing the mean and SD of physical random-effect SDs. By contrast, legacy random_effect_std_devs() evaluates the transformation at mean theta; those quantities can differ for multivariate factors. Gaussian residual_std_dev() similarly returns exp(mean log SD), which is not the mean residual SD.

    predict() computes the inverse link at the posterior mean latent field. For logit and log links this is generally different from integrating the response mean over the posterior. Unseen/null groups set their random effects to zero; that fallback does not integrate the distribution of a new island. The smooth curves therefore illustrate the fitted mean field rather than calibrated posterior predictive probabilities.

    Separate grid sensitivity from prior sensitivity

    First change only the number of grid points, holding the [0.01,3] SD prior fixed. Then fix 81 grid points and vary the prior's upper bound through 1.5, 3 and 5. This second experiment changes the model, not just numerical resolution. Do not choose a prior bound by maximizing these observed-data summaries or evidence values. With only three groups, movement in the random-effect SD is scientifically relevant even when the fixed mass slope moves little. A zero outer-grid edge mass cannot establish that a deliberately finite prior bound is unimportant.

    Independent appendix: adjust the original ten p-values

    The supplied p-values are \((.001,.004,.009,.03,.12,.18,.35,.51,.72,.98)\). They are illustrative numbers, not tests extracted from the penguin posterior, and small p-values alone do not identify which null hypotheses are true. For ordered values \(p_{(i)}\), Bonferroni uses \(\min(1,mp_i)\); Holm takes the running maximum of \((m-j+1)p_{(j)}\) through rank \(i\), capped at one. BH takes the backward running minimum of \(mp_{(j)}/j\) from rank \(i\), also capped at one. Both procedures restore the caller's original order.

    At level .05, Bonferroni and Holm reject *two*, while BH rejects *three*. The fourth raw value .03 is not rejected by any of them: its adjusted values are .30, .21 and .075. Bonferroni/Holm control family-wise error under valid marginal p-values without a dependence restriction. BH controls false discovery rate under independence or suitable positive dependence. FDR is an expected false-discovery proportion with the zero-rejection case defined as zero, not a guarantee for each realized list. These procedures do not convert an approximate Bayesian interval into a multiplicity-adjusted frequentist test.

    Conclusion

    The normal-normal control verifies the inner mode, covariance and evidence calculation against exact algebra. The Bernoulli control separates Newton convergence from local Gaussian accuracy. Unknown precision demonstrates genuine hyperparameter mixing, with the full conditional grid and within/between variance visible. Grid refinement checks the numerical integration, while the penguin prior experiment changes the model and exposes sensitivity that numerical convergence cannot remove.

    These are approximate Gaussian-mixture marginals from a dense implementation, not exact posteriors or the full R-INLA marginal correction machinery. Posterior SDs, mixture quantiles, transformed physical SD moments and inverse-link plug-in predictions are distinct quantities. Keeping those distinctions explicit makes the penguin example interpretable, including the limits imposed by three islands and omitted species. The p-value appendix is a separate frequentist calculation with its own error-rate assumptions and the original report's rejection-count errors corrected.
