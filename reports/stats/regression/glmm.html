    Generalized Linear Mixed Models: Working Fits and Partial Pooling

    Abstract

    Binary outcomes and event counts often arrive in groups whose observations share unmeasured influences. We want response probabilities or count means that account for that dependence while sharing information across groups. A generalized linear mixed model adds Gaussian group effects on a link scale. This report derives a working-model PQL approximation, calculates two tiny updates by hand, profiles a four-count working variance, and checks recovery on simulated groups with known effects. A penguin analysis then separates species adjustment, prediction within observed islands and prediction on an omitted island. Numerical convergence, working-Gaussian criteria and original response likelihoods have different meanings throughout; setting a new group's effect to zero also differs from averaging over its unknown effect. On the prespecified 100-penguin holdout, adding species increases accuracy from 81% to 89% and lowers Brier loss from 0.1446 to 0.0744. The full-data island SD changes from 1.180 to a zero-variance boundary after species adjustment.

    The method

    Conditional observations and latent groups

    For row \(i\) in group \(g\), a conditional GLMM specifies

    \[b_g\sim N_q(0,D),\qquad \eta_i=x_i^\top\beta+z_i^\top b_g,\qquad E(Y_i\mid b_g)=\mu_i=g^{-1}(\eta_i).\]

    dm::stats::GLMM supports Bernoulli zero/one observations with a logit link, and nonnegative integer Poisson counts with a log link. Conditional dispersion is fixed at one: \(V(\mu)=\mu(1-\mu)\) or \(\mu\). A random intercept multiplies a Poisson mean by \(e^{b_g}\) and shifts binomial log odds by \(b_g\). These are link-scale effects, not additive changes in counts or probabilities.

    A formula such as y ~ x + (1 + x | group) uses fixed intercept/slope and a correlated random intercept/slope. There is one grouping factor, and random slopes must be plain numeric column names. This implementation has no trial-count weights, exposure offsets, additional conditional dispersion, crossed groups or arbitrary links. Rows incomplete for the fixed design, random design or group are omitted jointly; kept_rows() preserves original indices. Retained numeric values must be finite. Null fixed predictors produce NaN predictions; null required random slopes also produce NaN for a known group.

    What the working fit approximates

    An exact marginal likelihood would integrate the conditional density over every group effect. For non-Gaussian observations, replacing that integral by a sequence of Gaussian working fits is a statistical approximation, even if every working fit is solved accurately. This differs from ordinary GLM IRLS, which can converge to the actual response-likelihood maximum without making a Gaussian approximation to a latent-effect integral.

    At the current conditional fit, linearizing the link gives a pseudo-response \(u\) and diagonal working precision \(W\):

    \[u_i=\eta_i+(y_i-\mu_i)g'(\mu_i),\qquad w_i=\frac1{V(\mu_i)g'(\mu_i)^2},\qquad u\approx X\beta+Zb+e,\quad e\sim N(0,W^{-1}).\]

    For logit, \(w_i=\mu_i(1-\mu_i)\); for Poisson log, \(w_i=\mu_i\). The library initializes random effects at zero and, when there is a fixed intercept, sets it to the link of the overall response mean. Training/working means are guarded at \(10^{-8}\), and at \(1-10^{-8}\) for binomial. These guards prevent zero working precision; they do not fix separation or certify a finite statistical optimum.

    Writing \(D=\Lambda\Lambda^\top\), each group has working covariance \(M_g=W_g^{-1}+Z_g D Z_g^\top\). Conditional on \(D\), Gaussian algebra gives

    \[\widehat\beta=\left(\sum_gX_g^\top M_g^{-1}X_g\right)^{-1} \sum_gX_g^\top M_g^{-1}u_g,\qquad \widehat b_g=D Z_g^\top M_g^{-1}(u_g-X_g\widehat\beta).\]

    For each frozen \(u,W\), this implementation estimates \(\Lambda\) by Gaussian working ML, minimizing

    \[Q(\Lambda;u,W)=n\log(2\pi)+\sum_g\log|M_g| +\sum_g(u_g-X_g\widehat\beta)^\top M_g^{-1}(u_g-X_g\widehat\beta).\]

    This is the implemented PQL variant: it uses profiled ML for working covariance, rather than the default working REML in R's MASS::glmmPQL. Bounded differential evolution is followed by a bounded local polish; the exact zero-variance candidate is also checked for random-intercept models. A user can instead condition on a known random-intercept SD. That option is used below to make the first updates hand-checkable.

    working_data(k) reconstructs the inputs to update \(k+1\), starting at zero. iteration_history() records completed updates. Its stopping flag requires changes in fixed coefficients, group effects and covariance-factor entries to satisfy the relative infinity-norm tolerance. A stationary fixed intercept alone is insufficient. At the iteration limit, a finite last iterate is returned with converged() false.

    Partial pooling and computation

    With only a random intercept of variance \(\tau^2\), define \(s_g=\sum_{i\in g}w_i\) and \(\bar r_g=\sum_{i\in g}w_i(u_i-x_i^\top\widehat\beta)/s_g\). The BLUP simplifies to

    \[\widehat b_g=\frac{\tau^2s_g}{1+\tau^2s_g}\bar r_g.\]

    This shows why the number of rows alone does not determine pooling: logit observations near probabilities zero or one have little working information, whereas larger Poisson means give larger log-scale information. When \(\tau=0\), group effects vanish and the working fit reduces to ordinary GLM IRLS. Random-effect covariance boundaries deserve attention, especially with very few groups; covariance_at_boundary() is a numerical heuristic for singular-factor or search-box boundaries, not a variance-component test.

    The implementation uses Woodbury identities with \(U_g=Z_g\Lambda\):

    \[M_g^{-1}=W_g-W_gU_g(I+U_g^\top W_gU_g)^{-1}U_g^\top W_g, \qquad \log|M_g|=-\sum_{i\in g}\log w_i+\log|I+U_g^\top W_gU_g|.\]

    The formulas also work when \(D\) is singular because they never invert \(D\). For \(n\) observations, \(G\) groups, \(p\) fixed and \(q\) random coordinates, one objective evaluation costs \(O(n(p^2+pq+q^2)+Gq^3+p^3)\). Fitting multiplies that work by all candidate evaluations across outer updates. Numerical normal equations for fixed effects still limit accuracy with badly scaled or collinear designs.

    Prediction targets and criteria

    predict() uses a known group's fitted effect. An unseen, null or absent group falls back to \(b=0\); predict_fixed() explicitly requests that zero-effect prediction. It is not, in general, a new-group marginal mean. For a Poisson random intercept,

    \[\mu_{b=0}(x)=e^{x^\top\widehat\beta},\qquad E_b[\mu(x,b)]=e^{x^\top\widehat\beta+\widehat\tau^2/2}.\]

    For logistic models the corresponding logistic-normal integral has no elementary closed form, and the current prediction API does not evaluate it. No prediction intervals are provided. Group BLUPs and their plug-in predictions condition on fitted variance components; working-model fixed-effect Wald SEs do not propagate variance-component uncertainty.

    working_criterion() is \(Q\) on the final pseudo-data. The historical deviance() accessor returns that same quantity; historical aic() and bic() merely add parameter penalties to it. They are not marginal GLMM likelihood criteria. In particular, values of \(Q\) from different outer iterations or models use different pseudo-data and should not rank original-response models. The report uses observed-response test losses instead. PQL can be biased for binary data, especially with small clusters, and many rows in just three groups do not provide a precise group-variance estimate.

    Simple examples

    Four counts, two groups and a known variance

    Take group A counts \((1,3)\), group B counts \((5,7)\), and fix \(\tau=1\). Initially \(\beta=\log4\), \(b_A=b_B=0\), \(w_i=4\), and \(u_i=\log4+(y_i-4)/4\). Both groups have \(s_g=8\), while their working residual means are \(-1/2\) and \(+1/2\). Symmetry keeps the first fixed intercept at \(\log4\), so

    \[\widehat b_A^{(1)}=-\frac89\frac12=-\frac49,\qquad \widehat b_B^{(1)}=\frac49.\]

    Checking the theory

    A working-variance profile with an exact minimum

    Return to the four counts and their first working state, now estimating \(\tau\). Each group's working mean has residual variance \(1/8\), and the two means lie \(1/2\) below or above \(\log4\). After profiling the common mean, the part of \(Q\) that depends on \(t=\tau^2\) is

    \[Q(t)=C+2\log(t+1/8)+\frac{1/2}{t+1/8},\qquad Q'(t)=\frac2{t+1/8}-\frac{1/2}{(t+1/8)^2},\qquad \widehat t=\frac18.\]

    Thus the first estimated SD is \(\sqrt{1/8}\), the shrinkage factor is one half, and the first effects are \(-1/4,+1/4\). This is an ML result for a single frozen Gaussian working dataset, not an exact Poisson GLMM variance estimate.

    Penguins: a grouped binary response

    Data, units and group limitations

    dm::datasets::penguins() contains 344 penguins, with species, island, sex and four morphological measurements. Sex is missing for 11 rows. We omit incomplete sex/body-mass rows, encode male as one and use centred body mass in kilograms, mass = body_mass_g/1000 - 4.2. The binary response is natural; no thresholded or rounded measurement is invented to obtain it. The intercept refers to a 4.2 kg penguin.

    Only three islands are available. They are a small, geographically specific set, rather than a large random sample from an island population. Gentoo occurs on Biscoe only, Chinstrap on Dream only, while Adelie occurs on all three. Island effects from a model that omits species can therefore absorb species composition. We first summarize that composition, then compare the descriptive mass-only model to a species-adjusted fit.

    A prespecified holdout within the observed islands

    We split once with train_test_split(...,0.3,42,"island"). Island stratification retains training observations from each island, so these test predictions may legitimately use island effects learned from other penguins. Test labels never enter those effects. Two formulas are prespecified: mass only, and mass plus species. Brier loss averages \((\widehat p-y)^2\); log loss averages negative Bernoulli log probabilities. Lower is better for both. Accuracy uses the fixed threshold one half. A small single split is an illustration, not a calibrated uncertainty assessment or a model-selection guarantee.

    Omit a whole island: a different prediction task

    Now fit on two islands and assess the third, using the mass-only formula in every fold. Species adjustment is deliberately excluded here: omitting Biscoe or Dream would also remove an entire species from training, so its fixed effect could not be estimated. The omitted island is unseen. prediction_frame() therefore uses the zero-random-effect baseline, not a BLUP learned from its test labels and not a logistic-normal marginal mean. These three folds are too few to estimate a reliable distribution of performance over new islands. Their species mixes and covariate distributions also differ, so scores should be interpreted fold by fold rather than as a direct ranking against the within-island split.

    Conclusion

    The tiny examples expose partial pooling and demonstrate that fixed coefficients can stop moving before group effects do. The first frozen Poisson variance profile has an exact minimum, while simulation checks recovery against actual generating effects. On penguins, island variation is sensitive to species composition and is supported by only three groups. Predictions within observed islands and on omitted islands use different information. PQL remains an approximation, its working criteria are not marginal GLMM likelihoods, and a zero-effect prediction is a conditional baseline rather than a population average.
