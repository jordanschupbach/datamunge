    Elastic Net: Thresholding, Grouping and Cross-Validation

    Abstract

    Predicting a continuous response from correlated measurements can produce unstable least-squares coefficients. We want to control coefficient size and possible sparsity while measuring prediction error without fitting preprocessing on validation rows. Elastic net combines L1 thresholding with quadratic shrinkage. Four-row examples expose its exact coordinate solution, sensitivity to measurement units and grouping of duplicated predictors. A small fold-refit check verifies the validation pipeline. Iris then supplies coefficient paths, complete CV diagnostics and a predeclared 120/30-row training/test comparison of ridge, lasso, mixtures and unpenalized regression. Numerical convergence, training fit, CV selection and held-out performance are reported separately. The primary fit does not improve held-out RMSE over unpenalized regression on this split; neither sparsity nor shrinkage alone certifies a useful predictor.

    The method

    Specify the objective in the coordinates that are penalized

    Let the expanded formula-design columns be x_j. With an intercept, fit training means mu_j and response mean y_bar; without an intercept, both origins are zero. Optional scaling uses s_j=sqrt(sum_i(x_ij−mu_j)^2/n), with an exactly zero divisor replaced by one. Define Z_ij=(x_ij−mu_j)/s_j and u_i=y_i−y_bar. The model minimizes

    \[J(\theta)=\frac{1}{2n}\|u-Z\theta\|_2^2+ \lambda\left[\alpha\|\theta\|_1+\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\|\theta\|_2^2\right], \quad\beta_j=\theta_j/s_j,\quad b_0=\bar y-\sum_j\mu_j\beta_j.\]

    Here lambda>=0 controls strength and alpha in [0,1] controls the L1 mixture. Alpha=0 is Ridge, alpha=1 is Lasso; the wrappers use the same engine. The intercept is unpenalized, and the response is centred when appropriate but never scaled. Formula interactions are expanded first and then centred/scaled as their own design columns. Reported coefficients are in original design units, so their penalty is not generally lambda times their unscaled norm. coefficient_table() shows both coordinate systems. Population RMS uses n, not n−1; without an intercept it is RMS about zero.

    This is the direct penalized objective used in the glmnet coordinate-descent formulation. It does not apply the additional coefficient rescaling of the original 2005 elastic-net paper. Scikit-learn names the same parameters differently: its alpha is this lambda, and its l1_ratio is this alpha. Standardize explicitly before comparing solvers.

    Derive the exact coordinate minimizer

    Hold every coefficient except theta_j fixed and put its old contribution back into the residual. The one-dimensional objective has curvature q_j, not automatically one:

    \[\rho_j=\frac1n Z_j^T\!\left(u-\sum_{\ell\ne j}Z_\ell\theta_\ell\right),\quad q_j=\frac1n\|Z_j\|_2^2,\qquad \theta_j\leftarrow\frac{S(\rho_j,\lambda\alpha)}{q_j+\lambda(1-\alpha)},\quad S(a,t)=\operatorname{sign}(a)\max(|a|-t,0).\]

    Differentiating on the positive and negative half-lines gives the two nonzero cases; the subgradient interval at zero supplies the threshold region. For a zero column and zero denominator, choose coefficient zero. Even when standardization is disabled, this actual q_j is essential. Every exact coordinate update weakly decreases the convex objective, subject to floating-point arithmetic. Positive lambda*(1−alpha) makes the slope objective strictly convex; lasso and unpenalized least squares can have nonunique slopes when the design is dependent.

    For ridge, stationarity also gives

    \[(Z^TZ+n\lambda I)\theta=Z^Tu,\qquad \kappa=\frac{d_{\max}+n\lambda}{d_{\min}+n\lambda}.\]

    A positive lambda makes this matrix invertible, but an arbitrarily small positive lambda does not guarantee a small condition number. Ridge does not impose a sparsity threshold; nevertheless, a coefficient can be exactly zero for an irrelevant orthogonal or constant column. The old report's “never zero” and unconditional conditioning claims are therefore too strong.

    Check stationarity as well as coefficient movement

    Let c_j=Z_j^T(u−Z theta)/n−lambda*(1−alpha)*theta_j. Convex optimality requires

    \[c_j=\lambda\alpha\operatorname{sign}(\theta_j)\quad(\theta_j\ne0),\qquad |c_j|\le\lambda\alpha\quad(\theta_j=0).\]

    diagnostics() reports the maximum violation of those conditions, objective and coefficient change. Stopping requires max_change<=tol and violation<=tol times max(1,max_j abs(Z_j^T u/n)). max_iter bounds full cyclic sweeps; a finite partial fit is returned and flagged if that limit is reached. Full-data path and individual fold results retain their own convergence evidence. This is a stationarity check, not a duality-gap certificate or a prediction guarantee. One sweep costs O(n*p); a length-L path with F folds costs O((F+1)*L*iterations*n*p), with warm starts.

    Keep validation preprocessing local to each training fold

    A finite negative lambda requests automatic CV. The common descending geometric grid uses full-training correlations: lambda_max=max(abs(Z_j^T u/n))/alpha for alpha>0, or a ridge-only heuristic using divisor .001. At zero correlation the maximum falls back to one. The default minimum/maximum ratio is .01 when n<p and .0001 otherwise; lambda_min_ratio can override it. Pure ridge has no finite all-zero cutoff. A search boundary means the stated grid should be reconsidered, not that a global optimum was found. Alpha is never tuned automatically by this API.

    Balanced folds use a local SplitMix64 seed. Each fold computes its own training predictor offsets/scales and response offset, fits the common lambdas, and predicts its held-out rows with those frozen quantities. The candidate grid and Formula schema are defined from the full training set, rather than separately per fold. CV pools squared error over all held-out rows, weighting rows equally even for unequal folds:

    \[\operatorname{CV}(\lambda)=\frac1n\sum_{f=1}^F\sum_{i\in V_f} \left(y_i-\widehat b_{0,-f}-x_i^T\widehat\beta_{-f}(\lambda)\right)^2.\]

    The first minimum on the descending path wins exact ties. The corresponding full- training path fit supplies the final model. Candidates with finite but unfinished optimization remain visible, including their status; no finite CV points are hidden from the plot. The old report's divergent-fold workaround is removed: using a unit coordinate denominator on arbitrary fold columns and reusing full-data preprocessing were solver/pipeline errors, rather than reasons to conceal large errors.

    Formula nulls drop rows before fitting; kept_row_indices() records the mapping. Retained design/response values and arithmetic must be finite. Predictions use the frozen Formula schema and raw-unit coefficients; query batches never fit new offsets. A null-predictor query row receives NaN, while scoring omits null formula rows. No p-values, confidence intervals or causal conclusions follow from selected slopes.

    Checking the theory

    Duplicated predictors expose grouping and nonuniqueness

    Let x=z and y=2*x, with x=(-1,-1,1,1), lambda=1. Any lasso solution with nonnegative slopes summing to one is optimal. Cyclic order can put all weight on the first column; reversing the columns changes the reported allocation but not predictions. With alpha=.5, strict convexity and exchange symmetry force equal slopes. Substituting b_x=b_z=b gives 2−2*b=.5+.5*b, hence b=.6. Ridge gives 2/3 on each duplicate.

    Conclusion

    The small examples connect the penalized objective to exact soft-thresholded coordinate solutions, raw-unit coefficients and equal sharing of duplicate predictors under a positive L2 contribution. Finite sweep budgets expose how numerical stationarity is checked. Explicit fold refits confirm that validation predictions use training-only preprocessing. Coefficient sparsity, coefficient uniqueness and predictive performance remain separate properties.

    The Iris comparison distinguishes training fit, CV selection and a reserved-row prediction check. These measurements describe one formula, declared grids and one split. They do not establish causal measurements, coefficient significance, reliable variable selection across resamples or a universally optimal alpha/lambda. Search boundaries and unfinished fits remain visible rather than being hidden from figures.
