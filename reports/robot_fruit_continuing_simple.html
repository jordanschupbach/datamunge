    Continuing robot-arm fruit sorting

    Continuing robot-arm fruit sorting

    The robot now works in a *continuing environment*. Fruit appears according to a Poisson process while the belt keeps moving and the arm keeps its joint pose between picks. The environment has no episode horizon; the video below is a single, uninterrupted *60-second observation window* of a frozen trained policy. The earlier arrival, episodic RL and varied-fruit classification examples remain in the episodic report.

    Arrivals, deadlines and physical outcomes

    The demonstration uses *0.12 fruit per second*, or 7.2 arrivals per minute in expectation. Exponential inter-arrival times are independent of the arm's availability, chosen actions and appearance randomness. There is no minimum spacing, busy-arm pause, source queue or admission delay. Poisson bursts can produce several fruit at once. Every fruit receives a random belt position, orientation, size, skin and one of six prototypes per class.

    The friction-driven belt runs continuously at *0.22 metres per second*. The arm can intercept at X=0.04 or X=0.16 m. Unhandled fruit passing X=0.30 m counts as missed. Misses also include failed releases and capacity overflow. Fruit still travelling, held or falling at the end of a measurement window remains *in progress*, and is reported separately. A correct sort requires a dynamic fruit to settle with physical floor support inside the correct bin: apples in red and bananas in yellow.

    The bins automatically clear completed fruit after two seconds so the world can run indefinitely with bounded memory. The active-body limit is 32; an arrival at capacity is counted as an overflow miss, rather than postponed. Births are inserted within one 1/240-second tick of their exact scheduled time.

    Learning to keep up

    A hierarchical controller learns five duration-varying choices: wait, intercept the leading fruit at either gate, or intercept the second-leading fruit at either gate. It observes queue size, leading position, spacing, arm side and pending releases. It cannot see future arrivals or use true fruit labels to choose a bin. Fixed IK options approach, time the descent, close the gripper, lift, traverse and release, with bounded joint/finger rates and fixture guards. This trains scheduling and interception decisions; it does not learn motor torques or replace the existing idealized grasp and arm-collision assumptions.

    The option reward is five times correct placements, minus five times wrong placements, minus three times misses, minus 0.02 per elapsed second and 0.25 per failed attempt. Q-learning discounts the next-state value by 0.995^elapsed_seconds, so a slow option has its actual time cost. Training uses *8,192 independent continuing windows of at least 120 simulated seconds*, with randomized arrival rates from 0.10 to 0.22 fruit/s. Windows are truncated for sampling; their final values bootstrap rather than receive a terminal reward.

    The fast training model preserves arrival scheduling, servo durations, fixture guards and rewards, while approximating belt transport and bin settling and omitting fruit-to-fruit contacts. Its perception-error channel is calibrated using classifier validation data. *It is a training proxy, not the final physics simulation.* Power-of-two checkpoints are compared on twelve separate full-physics validation streams before the final policy is selected.

    Adapting wrist vision to moving pickups

    The rigid wrist camera renders the actual multi-fruit scene. Registered RGB background subtraction finds visible components; a projected tracked target position associates the intended component. This association and the motor controller use privileged geometry. The box itself comes from RGB differences, and MobileNet receives the unannotated padded RGB crop. Its prediction alone selects red or yellow. Empty crops remain unknown and never use a true-label fallback. Green video boxes show the current target's visible component.

    The previous MobileNet checkpoint is fine-tuned for forty epochs on moving pickup views: 512 images from prototypes 0–3, 128 validation images from prototype 4, and 128 test images from prototype 5. Classes/prototypes are balanced and stream seeds are disjoint. Brightness and grayscale augmentation remain enabled. Validation loss selects the vision checkpoint; test images never supply gradients or select a checkpoint.

    The final comparison uses sixteen independent two-minute streams with actual RGB inference and full 240 Hz physics. The learned scheduler and both fixed oldest-first baselines see identical Poisson arrival streams. Counts are taken at the exact window boundary, including unresolved work. The video seed is fixed before training, and its minute includes all idle periods, misses, classification mistakes and unfinished work—there is no success montage.

    Run just playground to train and build everything. Once learning has completed, DATAMUNGE_STREAM_REPLAY=1 just playground reloads its frozen checkpoints and regenerates the evaluation video. The simulator itself can keep stepping forever; only recording stops at one minute.

    Replaying the completed training run and its frozen checkpoints.

    The scheduler trained for *276.1 simulated hours* across *8,192 continuing windows*. Independent full-physics validation selected checkpoint *512*. Moving-view MobileNet classified *121/128* held-out-prototype crops correctly.

    Across *16 independent two-minute streams with actual wrist RGB*, the learned scheduler correctly sorted *155*, versus *149* for early-oldest and *97* for late-oldest. Missed fruit: *66*, *73*, and *133*, respectively. All methods use the same Poisson arrival streams.

    The uninterrupted *60-second* video uses seed 2026113001 at *0.12 fruit/s*. At 60 seconds: *4 arrivals*, *3 correct*, *0 wrong*, *1 missed*, *0 still in progress*. The belt keeps running; there are no cuts, speed-ups or between-pick resets.
