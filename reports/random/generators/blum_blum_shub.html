    Blum–Blum–Shub: Modular States, Low Bits and Packed Words

    Abstract

    Produce a reproducible bit stream by repeatedly squaring a state modulo a composite number. The low bit of each new state is the output, and sixty-four such bits form one native word. We retain the original seed-three example, expose its actual state transitions, and enumerate every admissible seed for the small modulus 77. This fixed-width teaching engine makes the arithmetic checkable; its known small factors, short demonstrated cycles and at-most-64-bit modulus do not supply cryptographic security.

    The method

    For distinct primes \(p\equiv q\equiv3\pmod4\), set N=pq and choose a seed s coprime to N. The engine starts at a quadratic residue and emits a bit after each further square:

    \[x_0=s^2\bmod N,\qquad x_{i+1}=x_i^2\bmod N,\qquad b_i=x_{i+1}\bmod2.\]

    The constructor reduces s modulo N before squaring. Unsigned 128-bit intermediates preserve the product and square before modulo reduction; merely wrapping a 64-bit multiplication would implement a different recurrence. next_bit(), next_u64() and the trace helper use one canonical transition.

    The mathematical BBS construction studies unpredictability under computational assumptions and suitable parameter generation. Here the caller supplies the parameters, the default factors are public and small, and state inspection is deliberate. The constructor checks factor congruences, unequal factors, product range and seed coprimality. It does not test primality; primality remains a caller precondition for the number-theoretic conclusions below.

    Bit order and the original hexadecimal result

    The raw word is \(W=\sum_{i=0}^{63}b_i2^i\). Thus bits 0,0,1,1 occupy the low nibble as \(0+0+4+8=12\), hexadecimal c, reproducing the original report. Repeating that four-bit cycle sixteen times gives the full word cccccccccccccccc.

    If a modular-state cycle has length L, sampling its state every 64 steps has period \(L/\gcd(L,64)\): this is the least positive word count t with \(64t\equiv0\pmod L\). The emitted word sequence can have a smaller period if its output map merges states. Here L=4, so each word returns to the same modular state and every raw word is identical. A mathematically defined generator need not provide useful simulation output for every admissible seed and small parameter choice.

    Checking the theory over every unit seed modulo 77

    The quadratic-residue groups modulo p and q have odd orders (p-1)/2 and (q-1)/2. Squaring is invertible on an odd-order group: exponent two has an inverse modulo the group order. The Chinese remainder correspondence therefore makes squaring a permutation of the unit quadratic residues modulo N. Every admissible initial state is purely periodic, with no transient prefix.

    For 7 and 11, these groups have orders three and five, so their product is a cyclic group of order fifteen. A state of multiplicative order r returns under repeated squaring when \(2^L\equiv1\pmod r\). The possible r are 1,3,5,15, containing respectively 1,2,4,8 states. Their state periods are 1,2,4,4, taking the identity's period as one. Each residue has four unit square roots, so the sixty unit seeds must split into 4 seeds of period one, 8 of period two, and 48 of period four.

    Bounded inspection and parameter preconditions

    An orbit budget counts transitions, including any closing transition. Three steps from initial state nine have not yet shown a repeat; four steps close the cycle. An open record means that the budget ended, with period still unknown. Seed one is coprime and accepted, but produces the fixed state one.

    The final control deliberately supplies composite p=15 with q=7. It satisfies the constructor's structural checks, but violates the primality precondition. Its state path 4,16,46,16 has a transient of length one. This explains why the pure-period theorem requires prime factors; the engine does not establish that precondition on the caller's behalf.

    Native tests compare individual bits, traces and packed words using an independent overflow-free add/double modular multiplication oracle, including a modulus near UINT64_MAX. A separate Python arbitrary-integer check verifies the recorded squares and bit packing. Further controls cover invalid seeds, unsupported congruences, product overflow, zero budgets, failed trace allocation and copied normal caches. Neither those correctness checks nor a seed-period enumeration establish unpredictability for real-world use.

    Conclusion

    The original hexadecimal c is preserved and expanded into its actual four-state cycle and full repeated word. Exact seed-period counts connect the implemented recurrence with the quadratic-residue group. Budget and composite-factor controls separate observed closure from a theorem's parameter assumptions.

    The engine stores constant fixed-width state and uses 64 modular squares per raw word. Recording B bits uses O(B) work and storage. Bounded orbit inspection uses expected O(B) work/storage and stops early at a repeat; its records own their data. Generated arithmetic examples suffice here, so no observed dataset is needed.

    The public small defaults, deterministic seed and fixed-width arithmetic define a teaching implementation. Cryptographic parameter generation, large-integer security and statistical quality of arbitrary user parameters are outside its contract.

The fixed-width state inspection and small-cycle analysis above describe this implementation separately from the paper's security setting.
