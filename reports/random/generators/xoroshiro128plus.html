    xoroshiro128+: Balanced Bits Can Still Be Predictable

    Abstract

    xoroshiro128+ combines a 128-bit XOR/rotate transition with addition of two 64-bit words. We derive the transition's inverse and show why its lowest output bit remains linear despite the addition scrambler. A recurrence fitted on an initial segment predicts a separate suffix, revealing a weakness that bit frequencies and a small goodness-of-fit battery can miss. The generator then drives an ordinary bootstrap of mean fuel economy in dm::datasets::mtcars(), checked against the exact conditional standard error. Published jump operations organise reproducible simulation streams without turning disjoint state ranges into a claim of independence.

    The data: fuel economy in a selected set of cars

    The built-in mtcars dataset contains 32 cars and 11 numeric variables. Fuel economy mpg is in miles per US gallon and weight wt is in thousands of pounds. The cars have different cylinder counts, weights and engines; they are a selected historical set, not a random sample of today's fleet. Our resampling calculation describes the empirical distribution of these records under an exchangeable-record model. It does not estimate a causal weight effect or automatically generalise to other cars.

    The method: addition hides some linear structure, but not the lowest bit

    For old state words \((a,b)\), put \(q=a\oplus b\). Version 1.0 uses the 24/16/37 parameters, rather than the earlier 55/14/36 variant. With all words truncated to 64 bits, output and simultaneous transition are

    \[y=a+b\pmod{2^{64}},\qquad T(a,b)=\left(\operatorname{rotl}(a,24)\oplus q\oplus(q\ll16),\ \operatorname{rotl}(q,37)\right).\]

    XOR, fixed shifts and rotations are linear over \(\mathbf F_2\), so this is a binary linear state transition. Given a next state \((u,v)\), its inverse follows directly:

    \[q=\operatorname{rotr}(v,37),\qquad a=\operatorname{rotr}\left(u\oplus q\oplus(q\ll16),24\right),\qquad b=q\oplus a.\]

    This proves reversibility. The stronger claim that all nonzero states lie on one cycle of length \(2^{128}-1\) comes from the published primitive characteristic polynomial, not from the inverse or a short empirical run. The zero state is absorbing and rejected by the direct-state constructor. A scalar seed fills two words from successive SplitMix64 outputs. Those outputs are distinct because SplitMix's counter step and scrambling are invertible, so the pair cannot be all zero.

    Addition is nonlinear over XOR because of carries, but there is no carry into bit zero. Thus the lowest output bit is exactly

    \[z_t=(a_t)_0\oplus(b_t)_0=\ell(T^t s_0),\qquad z_t=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{L}c_j z_{t-j},\quad L\le128.\]

    The recurrence bound follows from applying the degree-128 characteristic identity for \(T\) to the state and then the linear functional \(\ell\). A sequence can have almost half ones and still obey such a short recurrence. The authors explicitly identify low linear complexity in the lowest bits; uniform01() instead uses the top 53 bits of a raw output.

    State snapshots exclude any second normal variate cached by the distribution interface. Jumps clear that cache, while copying a complete engine preserves both the raw state and the cache. Disjoint state ranges require stream lengths smaller than the jump spacing and no wrap around the full period; they are not a proof of statistical independence.

    A recurrence trained on one segment and checked on another

    For an ideal independent fair-bit stream, any predictor fixed from the training segment and based on previous observations makes an error with conditional probability one half on each next bit. A zero-error suffix of 512 bits would have probability \(2^{-512}\) for that fixed diagnostic. This is a structural demonstration of the low-bit limitation, not a general ranking of generators. Larger samples and different diagnostics can expose other weaknesses; high bits are not made cryptographically secure by passing.

    A small quality battery addresses different questions

    The seven-test battery measures marginal uniformity, serial pairs, lag-one correlation, raw monobit balance and low-byte frequencies. It contains no linear-complexity test. Forty fresh scalar-seeded engines provide a second-level KS check of each test's p-values, with 100000 observations per first-level test. Approximate reference distributions, discrete statistics, multiple testing and related deterministic seed choices limit interpretation. All seven results are retained, without selecting a favourable seed.

    Applying the engine: resampling mean fuel economy

    For observations \(x_1,\ldots,x_n\), let \(s^2\) be their sample variance with denominator \(n-1\). An ordinary bootstrap draws \(n\) records independently with replacement from the empirical distribution. Its single-draw variance and exact conditional mean variance are

    \[\operatorname{Var}_*(X^*)=\frac{n-1}{n}s^2,\qquad \operatorname{Var}_*(\bar X^*)=\frac{n-1}{n^2}s^2,\qquad \operatorname{SE}_*(\bar X^*)=s\frac{\sqrt{n-1}}{n}.\]

    The caller-owned generator controls the random draws; the seed stored in BootstrapOptions is ignored by this overload. Indices use unbiased multiply-and-reject bounded sampling, not a raw word modulo \(n\). For the 32-record sample, this uses the top five bits with no rejection, so the demonstrated raw lowest-bit recurrence does not choose the indices. This claim is specific to \(n=32\); for other bounds the multiplication and rejection rule must be considered directly.

    The simulated SE is 1.03719 mpg, about 1.1% below the exact 1.04865 mpg. For approximately normal replicates, the rough Monte Carlo SE of this standard-deviation estimate is \(1.04865/\sqrt{2(10000-1)}\), about 0.00742 mpg. The difference is about 1.5 such Monte Carlo standard errors. Independent Python reproduces all 10000 means, and R agrees on the summary and percentile endpoints.

    Increasing the replication count reduces Monte Carlo noise in these summaries. It cannot correct the original cars' selection or establish an independent-observation model. Agreement with the exact conditional variance checks the resampling calculation, not general statistical quality of the engine on every application.

    Conclusion

    The 24/16/37 transition is reversible, and published jump polynomials match the authors' reference stream. Addition leaves a linear lowest bit whose short recurrence predicts new outputs even though balance alone looks ordinary. A modest quality battery and a held-out recurrence check answer different questions. In the car bootstrap the bounded-index API uses upper bits, and an exact conditional variance provides an application-specific oracle while preserving the limits of the resampling model.
