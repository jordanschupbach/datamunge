    Mersenne Twister: Reproducible Words and Recoverable State

    Abstract

    A simulation needs a long, repeatable stream of numeric inputs from a compact seed, with a precise contract for restarting and transforming that stream. MT19937-64 produces 64-bit words from a 312-word state array. We work through a single twist and a tiny tempering example before checking seeded streams across many refills. Inverting tempering recovers a continuation from one aligned block of full outputs, showing why reproducibility and a long period do not imply unpredictability. Single-stream and forty-seed statistical checks then describe selected finite samples, without treating unremarkable p-values as a certification of randomness.

    The method

    A seed is a starting condition, not fresh entropy

    dm::random::MersenneTwister64(seed) owns its state and implements *MT19937-64*. It matches the scalar-seeded raw stream of std::mt19937_64, not the 32-bit std::mt19937 engine or an unspecified language's default random generator. The 64-bit version has distinct parameters and output. In particular the original 32-bit paper's 623-dimensional statement must not simply be attached to this engine.

    For \(n=312\) words, scalar initialisation uses unsigned wraparound:

    \[x_0=s,\qquad x_i=6364136223846793005\, [x_{i-1}\oplus(x_{i-1}\gg62)]+i\pmod{2^{64}},\quad 1\le i<312.\]

    For seed zero, the first three words are zero, one and 6364136223846793007: seeding zero does not initialise an all-zero array. The first output refills the array before reading it. block_index() is 312 initially, then one after that first output. Copies preserve both the raw array/index and the inherited normal sampler's cache. state_words() alone is a borrowed raw view, not a full checkpoint; a later refill changes that view.

    There are 19968 stored word bits, but the recurrence's effective degree is \(312\times64-31=19937\). Its published nonzero-state period is \(2^{19937}-1\). This is a property of the chosen recurrence and its characteristic polynomial, not a conclusion from the short runs below. A scalar seed supplies at most \(2^{64}\) possible initial streams, not 19937 bits of entropy. Different seeds do not prove independent streams. No fresh randomness arrives after seeding.

    Join two words and apply the twist

    Let \(U=\mathtt{FFFFFFFF80000000}\), \(L=\mathtt{000000007FFFFFFF}\), and \(A=\mathtt{B5026F5AA96619E9}\), all hexadecimal 64-bit constants. In recurrence order, one new word is

    \[v=(x_i\mathbin{\&}U)\mathbin{|}(x_{i+1}\mathbin{\&}L),\qquad x_{i+312}=x_{i+156}\oplus(v\gg1)\oplus \begin{cases}A,&v\text{ odd},\\0,&v\text{ even}.\end{cases}\]

    The join has disjoint bit fields, so its OR also equals XOR. The odd correction is the low bit of v multiplied by the fixed binary vector A; thus the twist is linear over \(\mathbf F_2\). This does not make the scalar-seeding recurrence, which includes integer multiplication and addition, binary-linear.

    The implementation fills its ring *in increasing index order*. Steps 0–155 read lag words from the old block; steps 156–311 use already replaced lag words. The final step also takes the lower bits of the newly written word zero. A loop that computes all 312 updates simultaneously from a frozen old array implements a different stream. Refill costs O(312); between refills a draw reads and tempers one word, giving O(1) amortised cost and fixed storage. The array itself is 2496 bytes, with additional index, distribution cache and object metadata.

    Tempering is an invertible output map

    Starting from one raw word, apply the four operations below in order, truncating left shifts to 64 bits. The native inspection function returns the input and all four intermediate words.

    \[\begin{aligned} z_1&=x\oplus[(x\gg29)\mathbin{\&}\mathtt{5555555555555555}],\\ z_2&=z_1\oplus[(z_1\ll17)\mathbin{\&}\mathtt{71D67FFFEDA60000}],\\ z_3&=z_2\oplus[(z_2\ll37)\mathbin{\&}\mathtt{FFF7EEE000000000}],\\ y&=z_3\oplus(z_3\gg43). \end{aligned}\]

    For any one stage write \(y=(I+N)x\), where N is its masked-shift map over \(\mathbf F_2\). Every application shifts surviving information farther in one direction, so \(N^q=0\) once \(q\,\text{shift}\ge64\). Therefore \((I+N)^{-1}=I+N+\cdots+N^{q-1}\): multiplying the two expressions cancels every interior term, leaving \(I+N^q=I\). Undo the four stages in reverse order. This proves a unique preimage for every 64-bit output, and explains the native mt19937_64_untemper implementation. Tempering changes distribution properties of the output coordinates without increasing period, state entropy or secrecy.

    The common uniform01() sampler takes the upper 53 bits and multiplies by \(2^{-53}\). Its possible values are exactly \(j/2^{53}\), from zero through \(1-2^{-53}\); it is a discrete grid rather than a literal continuous uniform. It discards eleven bits, so the simple full-word inversion shown here does not apply directly to those floating samples. Integer uniform_u64 uses unbiased multiply-and-reject range mapping; normal uses cached Box–Muller pairs. Consequently a seed alone does not specify a mixed sampling transcript: the call order, parameters and cached state matter. Raw integer streams are exact; results involving log, sin and cos can vary with the floating-point math implementation.

    Checking the implementation and its limits

    Seeded reference streams, refill boundaries and sampling order

    Compare eight declared seeds, including zero, the high bit and the all-ones value, for 10000 consecutive outputs each. This exercises repeated refills in 80000 exact 64-bit word comparisons, covering 5120000 bit positions. The standard's 10000th default-seeded word is 9981545732273789042; its scalar default seed is 5489. Our constructor requires an explicit seed. Endpoint words and the boundary transcript are hexadecimal, with zero-based output indices. Index 311 exhausts the first block; index 312 triggers the next refill.

    An aligned output block is enough to reproduce the continuation

    Observe all 312 full outputs beginning at a refill. Invert each to recover the stored raw block, set the next index to 312, and apply the same ordered recurrence. The next block is then determined. Induction gives the same result for every later block. No seed search is involved. The native from_output_block factory implements this calculation and starts with an empty normal cache; it does not recover a previously cached normal value or infer the starting alignment.

    We collect the first block from a known local engine, then freeze that recovered state before generating 4096 unseen words. The two rasters show only the first 64 held-out words, while the error count checks all 4096. This is an exact predictability demonstration on our own sequence, distinct from probabilistic quality tests. Arbitrary input arrays are accepted as deterministic states; they need not be reachable by scalar seeding. An all-zero supplied block gives a degenerate zero continuation and is outside the nonzero-state period claim.

    A finite sample can look uniform and remain predictable

    The existing seven-test battery checks 100-cell uniform counts, uniform KS, non-overlapping 10-by-10 serial cells, lag-one correlation, low/high monobit counts and low-byte frequencies. Each stage uses fresh words in a fixed order; the two monobit counts share their n words. The full battery advances the generator by 7n raw outputs. Set n=20000 before inspecting the results.

    For a bit with ideal independent fair draws, the estimated one-frequency has standard deviation \(1/(2\sqrt n)\). The plotted three-SD guides are *pointwise*, not simultaneous 64-bit acceptance bands. The histogram uses another fresh engine with the same seed; these reused prefixes are reproducible, not independent replications. The null reference is a modelling benchmark for the diagnostics, not a claim that a deterministic seeded sequence literally consists of independent random variables.

    The chi-square statistics use their multinomial degrees of freedom. Correlation and monobit p-values use approximate normal tails; the KS routine uses a corrected asymptotic reference. The floating uniform's very fine grid, dependence and finite sample size qualify those calibrations. No rule here declares a generator good because every p-value exceeds 0.05: even ideal calibrated tests sometimes give small p-values, and these tests are not mutually independent.

    Repeat the battery across forty declared seeds

    A second-level check looks at the first-level p-values across seeds 1 through 40, with n=20000 per stage. Under an ideal independent, exactly calibrated reference, first-level p-values would be uniform; one expects about two below 0.05 per test, with standard deviation \(\sqrt{40(0.05)(0.95)}\), about 1.38. The figure's horizontal count-two guide is an expectation, not a threshold. The native table also reports upper-tail counts, mean p and a KS check of each p-value distribution. Finite-sample approximations, discrete statistics and consecutive-seed dependence mean the second-level p-values are diagnostic rather than exact significance levels. Seven second-level comparisons add another multiplicity issue. We retain all seven outcomes rather than selecting a favourable test or seed.

This visible excess is a reason to retain the whole table and, if using the diagnostic for a decision, plan a larger follow-up rather than change the seed range after inspection.

    What the examples establish

    The twist example checks exact masking and conditional XOR. The tempering example connects intermediate words to a finite inverse-series proof. Seeded conformance checks exercise the ordered refill and the standard's long transcript rather than relying on one matching output. The continuation example shows that 312 aligned full words reveal enough state to predict later words exactly, despite balanced bits and a huge recurrence period.

    Statistical plots and forty-seed checks expose only selected low-dimensional features of finite samples. They do not prove the stated period, all-dimensional equidistribution, stream independence or cryptographic security. MT19937-64 is appropriate here as a reproducible simulation mechanism; a secret seed does not turn its invertible output mapping into protection for adversarial use. No external dataset is needed for this algorithm report: all inputs, reference words and observed sequences are declared, and correctness has an exact oracle. No timing ranking or practical throughput claim is inferred from the complexity calculation.
