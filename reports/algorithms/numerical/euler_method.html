    Forward Euler: Actual Tangents, Error and Stability Limits

    Abstract

    Given an initial state and its rate of change, forward Euler advances along the current derivative once per fixed step. We begin with two hand-checkable decay steps, retain the original coarse trajectory and error-refinement experiment, and distinguish first-order accuracy from stability. The same core execution returns actual states, derivatives and work counts. Scalar decay, an oscillator, time-dependent forcing and finite numerical boundaries expose what this simple method can and cannot establish. Successful execution supplies no adaptive error bound.

    Two actual tangent steps

    For y'=-y with y(0)=1 and h=1/2, the first derivative is -1, giving y[1]=1+(1/2)(-1)=1/2. At this approximate state, the next derivative is -1/2, giving y[2]=1/2+(1/2)(-1/2)=1/4. The exact value at t=1 is exp(-1), about 0.367879. The second step uses the derivative of the ODE field at the approximate state, not a tangent to the exact curve at that time. Both derivatives below come from the actual solver call.

    Derive the update and the first-order error claim

    For a first-order system y'=f(t,y), take N fixed steps with nominal h=(t1-t0)/N. Forward Euler uses

    \[y_{n+1}=y_n+h f(t_n,y_n).\]

    If the exact solution is twice continuously differentiable, Taylor's formula over a step beginning at the exact state gives the one-step defect y(t+h)-y(t)-h f(t,y(t))=O(h2). Some texts divide this defect by h when defining local truncation error, obtaining O(h); the normalisation must be stated. Here "one-step defect" means the unnormalised O(h2) quantity.

    Suppose f is Lipschitz in y with constant L on the relevant region, and the solution's second derivative is bounded by M. With e[n] the global state error and positive h, its norm obeys

    \[\|e_{n+1}\|\le(1+hL)\|e_n\|+\frac M2h^2.\]

    Starting with exact initial data and summing this geometric bound gives |e[N]| <= M h ((1+hL)^N-1)/(2L) for L>0, bounded by M h (exp(L T)-1)/(2L) on a fixed interval of length T. The L=0 limit is M T h/2. These assumptions give first-order global error, not accuracy for an arbitrary black-box callback or an arbitrarily large step. Finite arithmetic introduces additional rounding effects.

    Preserve the original coarse decay and error family

    The original experiment integrates y'=-y from zero to four with eight steps. Its endpoint is (1-1/2)^8=1/256, far below exp(-4). For the refinement experiment over [0,1], with h=1/N,

    \[y_N=(1-h)^{1/h},\quad \log y_N=-1-\frac h2+O(h^2),\quad \frac{\exp(-1)-y_N}{h}\longrightarrow\frac1{2e}.\]

    The program measures this error directly and prints its ratio to the preceding refinement. The displayed log-log error points come from actual trajectories, not a prewritten power-law curve. For this decay and 0<h<1, 1-h<exp(-h) explains undershooting at sample times; that sign is not a general property of Euler errors on other equations.

    Stability is different from accuracy

    On the scalar test equation y'lambda y, Euler multiplies the state by R(z)=1+z, with z=h lambda. Absolute stability means 1+z<=1, the closed disk centered at -1 with radius one in the complex plane. Strict decay of the numerical homogeneous solution requires 1+z<1. For real decay y'-a y with a>0 and positive h, put q=h a. Then 0<q<2 gives decay; 0<q<1 stays positive, 1<q<2 alternates sign, q=2 alternates without damping, and q>2 grows. q=1 reaches zero in one step. Stability alone supplies no desired accuracy tolerance.

    Oscillations expose the imaginary-axis limitation

    For the harmonic oscillator x'v, v'-x, exact energy E=(x2+v2)/2 is constant. Forward Euler instead maps (x,v) to (x+h v,v-h x), so

    \[x_{n+1}^2+v_{n+1}^2=(1+h^2)(x_n^2+v_n^2),\qquad E_n/E_0=(1+h^2)^n.\]

    Every nonzero real h therefore pumps energy into this oscillator. Its eigenvalues are imaginary, and 1+i h>1 places them outside the Euler stability disk. Reducing h reduces the growth over a fixed finite interval, but does not turn the method into an energy-preserving integrator. The following plot uses the actual two-component trace; the energy formula is an independent closed discrete check.

    Actual time-dependent callbacks and dimension-dependent work

    For y'=t, y(0)=0, the exact solution is t^2/2. On [0,1] with N steps, Euler is the left-endpoint quadrature h sum(i=0,...,N-1) i h=(N-1)/(2N). For eight steps the endpoint is 7/16, whereas the exact endpoint is 1/2. The callback below saves the times and states it actually receives. The trace is checked against those observations and contains no extra query at the final time, since no further step begins there.

    Each step invokes the vector callback once regardless of dimension d, and performs d scalar state updates. Intrinsic output has N+1 state samples. Optional derivative capture has C=(N+1)(d+1)+N(d+1)=(2N+1)(d+1) logical entries under the slot convention described below. Constant forcing supplies independently known endpoints for dimension/work controls; the numerical integration loop belongs entirely to the core.

    Input contracts and finite time grids

    Euler requires a nonempty finite initial state, a nonempty callback, finite endpoints and a positive integer step count. Every returned derivative must have exactly the state dimension and contain only finite values. Products h*f[i] and updated double states must remain finite. Invalid shapes and arithmetic failures throw explicitly; user callback exceptions propagate unchanged. A successful integration can still have a large truncation error or an unstable solution.

    Reverse integration uses negative h. Equal endpoints are supported: h=0, N callbacks and N+1 repeated-time unchanged states are retained. For a nonzero interval, the whole rounded time grid must advance strictly before any callback is invoked. Nominal h is converted from an extended-precision endpoint difference divided by N; interior times use extended t0+i*h, and the last time is exactly the requested endpoint. Adjacent rounded times need not differ by exactly h; all state updates use the same nominal h. This policy avoids a hidden overflow in a large endpoint subtraction, and rejects a step too small to advance at the represented time scale. Refining indefinitely cannot bypass floating-point resolution or state-update rounding.

    Ordinary result/work calls return their intrinsic O((N+1)d) trajectory and keep no optional derivative history or capture quota. Trace capture counts each of N+1 sample slots with d state entries, and each of N derivative slots with d derivative entries. Since C=(2N+1)(d+1) is known upfront, the full quota is checked before history copies or any callback. Default one million; zero disables that quota. Other outcome metadata, runtime state/callback storage, vector headers, capacities and allocator bytes are excluded. C is not a peak-memory estimate. Returned samples and derivatives own their storage. Exceptions return no partial result, although a later callback failure can follow earlier callback side effects.

    The test suite verifies closed discrete scalar/vector solutions, nonautonomous quadrature, oscillator evolution and energy, actual stateful callback order, ownership, exact quotas, strict domains and floating limits. All 132 frozen legacy state trajectories, totaling 16,268 samples, retain exactly the same state values and callback/sample counts. Two time grids change by at most 1.4211e-14 through the documented rounding policy; all other grids are exact matches. The old malformed callback probe triggered an out-of-bounds access, and its nonpositive step/NaN/rounded-grid probes were accepted; those unsafe cases now fail. Explicit ODE oracles are the natural inputs for these checks, so no external dataset or independent solver setup is required by the report.
