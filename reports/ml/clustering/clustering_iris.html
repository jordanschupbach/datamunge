    Clustering Iris: Centroids, Merge Trees and Density

    Abstract

    Given only measurements, we want groups of similar observations without prescribing species labels as a training target. Similarity alone does not specify a unique partition: distance to a centre, the cost of a merge and local density answer different questions. Four collinear points and two short density chains make those mechanisms checkable by hand. We then cluster all four Iris measurements, compare K-means cluster counts and restarts, re-cut a hierarchical tree, and vary DBSCAN's radius. Species labels describe the fitted results afterwards. Explicit noise coverage and permutation-invariant agreement prevent purity from being mistaken for predictive accuracy or evidence for a uniquely correct cluster count.

    The method

    Specify a geometry and a question

    An observation is a numeric vector \(x_i\in\mathbb R^p\). Every fit below uses Euclidean distance in the supplied feature units. Neither the clustering classes nor these diagnostics standardize features automatically. Changing a single column's units changes the geometry; equal units do not imply equal variance or scientific importance. Uniform scaling by a multiplies squared-distance objectives by a squared, and preserving DBSCAN neighbourhoods then requires scaling epsilon by the absolute value of a.

    The concept diagram below is drawn by dm::graphics::Diagram. Its editable C++ source is the complete block below; regeneration uses the same datamunge toolchain as the numerical examples. Exports consume the saved local SVG.

    Lloyd updates and careful seeding

    For k nonempty groups, K-means minimizes the within-cluster sum of squares (WCSS), also called inertia. Holding centres fixed gives nearest-centre assignments; holding assignments fixed gives means:

    \[J=\sum_i\|x_i-\mu_{z_i}\|^2,\qquad z_i\leftarrow\arg\min_c\|x_i-\mu_c\|^2,\qquad \mu_c\leftarrow\frac1{|C_c|}\sum_{i\in C_c}x_i.\]

    The derivative of a group's squared-error sum is \(2|C_c|\mu_c-2\sum_{i\in C_c}x_i\), so the mean minimizes that subproblem. Each exact assignment and nonempty mean update cannot increase J. This is alternating minimization, with no guarantee of a global minimum. The implementation reseeds an empty centre at a farthest point. It stops when every centre's Euclidean shift is below tolerance, or at the iteration cap, then reassigns labels to the final centres. At a capped, unconverged fit those centres need not equal the final assigned-group means.

    K-means++ chooses the first centre uniformly and each later point with probability \(D_i^2/\sum_jD_j^2\), where D is distance to the nearest existing centre. The native implementation uses that single-draw seeding, then keeps the lowest-inertia run across n_init restarts. We use positive iteration/restart budgets and a fixed seed; iterations_used() counts only the winning run. Extending the restart count at the same seed keeps the earlier candidates, so the best objective cannot worsen. Increasing k makes the globally optimal objective nonincreasing, but independently initialized finite local fits have no such general guarantee. An elbow is descriptive, not a validated estimator of a biological species count.

    Linkage and the units of a merge height

    Agglomeration starts from singleton groups. Single linkage takes the minimum cross-group distance; complete takes the maximum; average takes the mean over all cross-group point pairs. Ward instead minimizes the increase in WCSS:

    \[\Delta(A,B)=\frac{|A||B|}{|A|+|B|}\|\mu_A-\mu_B\|^2,\qquad h_{\mathrm{Ward}}(A,B)=\sqrt{2\Delta(A,B)}.\]

    The identity follows by splitting each squared distance about its old and merged means; the centred cross terms vanish. The native Lance-Williams update keeps squared Ward distances internally and reports their square roots. Thus its height is not the raw SSE increase. Ward requires Euclidean distance. Every fit builds all n−1 merges; cut(k) replays a prefix of that same tree, so changing the cut needs no refit. Tied merges may produce different valid trees in another implementation.

    Core connectivity, borders and noise

    DBSCAN uses a closed radius neighbourhood that includes the point itself:

    \[N_\varepsilon(i)=\{j:\|x_i-x_j\|\leq\varepsilon\},\qquad i\text{ is core}\iff |N_\varepsilon(i)|\geq m.\]

    Core-to-core chains form components. Noncore neighbours of a core join as border points but cannot extend a chain; everything else is noise, labelled -1. A point initially marked noise may later be reclaimed as a border. If a border touches two components its assignment can depend on traversal order. Epsilon and min_samples, as well as feature scaling and distance choice, define the result. More epsilon can create core components before it joins them, so cluster count need not be monotone. A two-cluster result at one radius does not establish two intrinsic populations.

    Agreement needs both a partition and coverage

    With a contingency table \(n_{cj}\) between fitted clusters and reference classes, assigned purity is \(\sum_{c\geq0}\max_j n_{cj}/n_{\mathrm{assigned}}\). It ignores noise and reaches one for singleton clusters, so it rewards fragmentation and can hide rejected observations. Always report coverage, assigned/n, beside it. For adjusted Rand, let A and B be sums of unordered within-cluster and within-class pair counts, and S the sum within contingency cells:

    \[\mathrm{ARI}=\frac{S-AB/\binom n2}{(A+B)/2-AB/\binom n2},\quad S=\sum_{cj}\binom{n_{cj}}2,\quad A=\sum_c\binom{n_c}2,\quad B=\sum_j\binom{n_j}2.\]

    ARI is invariant to label renaming, equals one for identical partitions, and can be negative. Its chance correction uses a fixed-marginal permutation model, not a significance test. Here ARI includes all observations, treating every noise label as one category; it does not credit noise rows as assigned. Degenerate identical partitions receive one. Purity is NaN when all rows are noise. Neither statistic is held-out classification accuracy, and looking at these scores to choose settings would use the species labels for selection. We present predeclared settings and sensitivity ranges.

    For n rows, p features, k centres, r restarts and T iterations, Lloyd fitting costs O(r T n k p). This implementation's dense hierarchical search uses O(n3+n2 p) time and O(n2) memory. DBSCAN precomputes all pair distances, requiring O(n2 p) time and O(n^2) memory; it does not use a spatial index. External count summaries use O(n log n) time and O(n) storage. These examples concern small data, not large-scale benchmarks.

    Iris measurements

    All 150 flowers have four complete numeric measurements in centimetres: sepal length, sepal width, petal length and petal width. Each species has fifty rows. All four raw features enter every fit below; two petal coordinates are used only for display. Species is excluded from every fit and returned only for descriptive agreement. Settings and sensitivity grids are declared in code rather than selected by a species score. Using all rows makes this an exploratory, in-sample analysis.

    Epsilon is a hard threshold. Some Iris pair distances lie at .3 within floating-point error. Direct coordinate-difference distances (used here) and dot-product-based distance evaluation can put them on opposite sides of that threshold. Our independent check against scikit-learn agrees on all seven partitions when supplied the same explicit distance matrix; allowing it to compute distances independently changes the partition at .3. Use a common distance matrix to isolate algorithm differences from numerical threshold effects, and examine nearby radii when an edge is borderline.

    Conclusion

    At k=3, K-means gives WCSS 78.8514, assigned purity .8933 and ARI .7302. Average linkage has ARI .7592, compared with Ward .7312, complete .6423 and single .5638 for these three-cluster fits. At epsilon=.6, DBSCAN gives two groups and nine noise rows: coverage .94 and assigned purity .6738. These are post-fit descriptions, not labels used to select an algorithm. The twenty-run K-means prefix happens to find its best objective in the first run; that observation is specific to seed 42.

    Centroid error, linkage cost and density connectivity define different partitions. The small examples verify means and WCSS, Ward's square-root height convention, core and border roles, and the limits of purity. Iris supplies an exploratory comparison in one declared raw-feature geometry. Its measured WCSS, merge tree, radius sensitivity and species agreement characterize those fits; they do not infer a unique intrinsic cluster count, validate a predictive classifier or make every noise point an outlier in biological terms. Feature scaling and domain purpose remain part of the question.
