    Isomap: Neighbour Graphs, Geodesics and Shortcuts

    Abstract

    High-dimensional observations can lie on a curved surface where straight-line distance crosses folds rather than following the surface. We want low-dimensional coordinates that preserve travel distances along that surface, while recognizing when the sampled points do not determine those distances reliably. Isomap replaces global straight-line distances by shortest paths through local neighbours, then applies classical scaling. We work through a four-point line, a square cycle and two disconnected triangles before checking a Swiss roll with known intrinsic coordinates and Fisher's iris measurements. The square has perfect positive-inertia fit and zero residual variance yet distorted edge lengths. On a 1000-point roll, six neighbours give a 7.45% intrinsic distance error, while forty give 67.53%. Iris needs 25 neighbours for connectivity under the stated graph rule; that connection threshold is not evidence that the flowers form one sampled manifold.

    The method

    Local measurements define a graph

    Let \(x_i\in\mathbb R^p\), \(i=1,\ldots,n\), be the retained feature rows. dm::stats::Isomap uses raw Euclidean or Manhattan distances. Feature units and scaling therefore define the model; standardizing a column changes neighbourhoods and paths, not just the axis labels. This report uses Euclidean distances throughout.

    For each point retain exactly \(k\) other neighbours. Equal distances are ordered by increasing retained-row index, making the choice reproducible. The undirected graph uses the union rule: an edge exists if either endpoint selects the other. It is not the mutual-neighbour graph, so a vertex can have degree greater than \(k\). An edge has its original feature distance as weight. Duplicate observations retain their zero-length edges; a zero weight does not mean an edge is absent.

    isomap_graph() exposes the directed selections, union adjacency, original distances, component labels and retained row indices without requiring connectivity. Formula-free numeric features are selected explicitly, nulls are omitted jointly and retained nonfinite values are rejected. Labels and synthetic truth columns are never included unless explicitly named as features. The current API requires \(2\le k<n\).

    Shortest paths estimate geodesics

    Starting with edge weights, zeros on the diagonal and infinity for absent edges, Floyd–Warshall applies

    \[D_{ij}^{(m)}=\min\{D_{ij}^{(m-1)},D_{im}^{(m-1)}+D_{mj}^{(m-1)}\}.\]

    Induction on the allowed intermediate vertices proves that the final \(D_G\) contains all-pairs shortest-path distances. A disconnected graph has infinite cross-component distances, so no single finite classical-scaling matrix exists. The constructor rejects it; it does not invent inter-component edges. Inspect the graph or fit components separately before deciding whether a larger neighbourhood makes scientific sense. Some other implementations, including scikit-learn, repair disconnected graphs, so their results need not have this same meaning.

    For Euclidean input edges, the triangle inequality gives \(d_E(i,j)\le D_G(i,j)\). There is no universal ordering between \(D_G\) and the true manifold distance: sparse paths can detour, while chord edges and cross-fold shortcuts can underestimate it. The original consistency argument requires suitable sampling density, small local neighbourhoods, controlled curvature/branch separation and an intrinsically Euclidean geometry. It does not say that a connected graph or an attractive plot certifies recovery. A circle or a sheet with holes need not have a globally distance-preserving flat map.

    With fixed distance ties, increasing \(k\) only adds edges. Component counts and shortest paths can therefore only decrease. That monotonicity does not make embedding quality monotone. At \(k=n-1\), every pair is an edge and \(D_G=d_E\); Isomap reduces to ordinary Euclidean classical MDS, which has the same centred score geometry as unscaled PCA.

    Classical scaling and what its spectrum means

    Let \(J=I-\mathbf1\mathbf1^\top/n\). Squaring distances elementwise and centring gives

    \[B=-\tfrac12 J D_G^{\circ2}J=V\Lambda V^\top,\qquad Y_d=V_{1:d}\operatorname{diag}\{\sqrt{\max(\lambda_1,0)},\ldots, \sqrt{\max(\lambda_d,0)}\}.\]

    If \(D_G\) is Euclidean, \(B\) is positive semidefinite and sufficiently many axes recover its distances. A general graph metric can produce negative eigenvalues. Orthogonal invariance of the Frobenius norm shows that dropping all negative modes and retaining the largest positive modes minimizes \(\|B-YY^\top\|_F\) among rank-at-most-\(d\) positive-semidefinite Gram matrices. This is a strain optimum, not a least-squares distance-stress optimum. The full signed spectrum is available from spectrum(). Eigenvector signs and rotations within repeated eigenspaces are arbitrary; axis-by-axis correlations are therefore a poor general comparison between implementations.

    The historical goodness_of_fit() is only \(\sum_{a\le d}\max(\lambda_a,0)/\sum_a\max(\lambda_a,0)\). It omits negative modes. dimension_diagnostics() separately reports negative absolute-inertia mass, normalized Gram error and actual graph-distance errors. For unordered pairs \(i<j\), write \(\widehat D_{ij}=\|y_i-y_j\|_2\):

    \[E_G=\sqrt{\frac{\sum_{i<j}(\widehat D_{ij}-D_{G,ij})^2} {\sum_{i<j}D_{G,ij}^2}},\qquad R_V=1-\operatorname{corr}(D_{G,ij},\widehat D_{ij})^2.\]

    Residual variance follows the original Isomap diagnostic. Correlation ignores affine changes between the two distance vectors, so zero residual variance does not establish an isometry. A constant distance vector has undefined correlation and is reported as NaN. All of these scores describe the fitted graph; none can detect a wrong graph using only its own shortest-path targets. reference_diagnostics() can compare with independent reference coordinates when known, as on the Swiss roll below.

    Computation and scope

    Brute-force input distances cost \(O(n^2p)\), and deterministic partial sorting costs \(O(n^2\log k)\). The dense shortest-path and symmetric eigendecomposition stages each cost \(O(n^3)\). The implementation uses contiguous Floyd–Warshall matrices and a Householder/implicit-QL eigensolver, with \(O(n^2+np+nk)\) storage. This is a dense in-sample method; it has no out-of-sample transform or built-in validation procedure. A small retained fraction or a high graph-fit score does not replace geometric checks.

    Simple examples

    Four points on a line

    Use \((x,y)=(0,0),(1,0),(2,0),(4,0)\) and \(k=2\). Directed neighbour lists are \((1,2),(0,2),(1,0),(2,1)\), indexed from zero. In particular, the union graph has edge \((1,3)\) even though row 1 did not select row 3. The only missing unordered edge is \((0,3)\), whose shortest length is \(2+2=4\) through row 2. Thus every graph distance is \(|x_i-x_j|\).

    \[\bar x=\tfrac74,\qquad x_c=(-\tfrac74,-\tfrac34,\tfrac14,\tfrac94)^\top,\qquad B=x_cx_c^\top,\quad \lambda_1=x_c^\top x_c=\tfrac{35}4.\]

    A square cycle with a negative eigenvalue

    Now take \((0,0),(1,0),(1,1),(0,1)\), again with \(k=2\). Every vertex selects its two adjacent corners, so edges have length one and opposite corners have graph distance two. Each squared-distance row has mean \(3/2\), giving

    \[B=\tfrac14\begin{pmatrix}3&1&-5&1\\1&3&1&-5\\-5&1&3&1\\1&-5&1&3\end{pmatrix}, \qquad \operatorname{eig}(B)=(2,2,0,-1).\]

    Two positive axes explain 100% of positive inertia. Yet the resulting square has adjacent distances \(\sqrt2\), while opposite distances remain two. Its relative graph-distance error is \((\sqrt2-1)/\sqrt3\), and its relative strain is \(1/3\). Negative absolute-inertia mass is \(1/(2+2+1)=1/5\). Because the six pair distances have only two distinct levels before and after embedding, their Pearson correlation is one. Residual variance is therefore zero despite the edge distortion. All three diagnostics must be read according to their definitions.

    Two triangles are not one connected geometry

    Take two unit right triangles ten units apart. At \(k=2\), each vertex selects the other two vertices of its own triangle: there are six union edges and two components. The cross-component block of \(D_G\) is infinite, so the constructor rejects a global embedding. At \(k=3\), new long edges force the components together. That change makes a computation possible; it does not establish that the gap represents a sampled surface. The graph inspector allows both situations to be examined without catching a failed fit.

    Checking the theory on a Swiss roll

    Known intrinsic coordinates give an independent target

    dm::datasets::swiss_roll(1000,0,1) supplies a noiseless rolled sheet and withheld coordinates. Points are \((t\cos t,h,t\sin t)\), where \(t\) ranges from \(3\pi/2\) to \(9\pi/2\), and \(h\) from zero to 21. The true flat coordinate along the spiral is arc length, not \(t\):

    \[s(t)=\int_0^t\sqrt{1+u^2}\,du =\tfrac12\{t\sqrt{1+t^2}+\operatorname{asinh}t\},\qquad ds^2+dh^2=(1+t^2)dt^2+dh^2.\]

    The intrinsic domain is a rectangle in \((s,h)\), so its geodesic distance is ordinary Euclidean distance in those coordinates. Only x,y,z enter the fit. We evaluate complete pair distances in \((s,h)\), making the check invariant to reflection or rotation of the embedding. This avoids claiming recovery merely from one embedding axis correlating with one latent parameter.

    Iris measurements

    Data and scaling

    dm::datasets::iris() contains 150 flowers, four numeric measurements in centimetres and a species label. We use all four measurements without scaling. Their shared physical unit does not make their variances equal, but it makes the raw Euclidean baseline explicit. Species is withheld from graph construction, fitting and diagnostics; it colours the final map only. No classification accuracy or prediction on new flowers is estimated.

    Connectivity is a property to inspect, not an automatic tuning rule

    The next graph-only sweep does not fit invalid global maps. At small \(k\), separate components are a genuine output. With the stated tie/union rule, iris first becomes connected at \(k=25\). That is a fairly broad neighbourhood among 150 observations. Joining separated groups is not proof that their shortest paths approximate travel on a single biological manifold; component-wise analysis can be more defensible than forcing a connection just to obtain one picture.

    An independent NumPy/SciPy calculation reproduces the stated iris graph, shortest paths, spectrum and coordinate Gram matrix. Stock scikit-learn agrees on two continuous Swiss-roll checks, but differs on iris: rounding and neighbour tie choices can change the selected graph. Comparisons must match the graph before comparing coordinates.

    Conclusion

    Isomap learns a global metric from local edges before it learns coordinates. The worked examples separate shortest-path correctness, connectivity and Euclidean representability. Even perfect positive-inertia fit and residual variance can hide distance distortion. The Swiss roll supplies an independent target and exposes both neighbourhood and sampling sensitivity. Iris illustrates a different limitation: obtaining connectivity by broadening neighbourhoods does not validate a single connected manifold. Graph inspection, signed spectra and clearly named distance targets make these distinctions reviewable.
