    Independent Component Analysis: Beyond Decorrelation

    Abstract

    Several measured signals may each contain a different mixture of the same hidden sources. Recovering those sources requires assumptions beyond making the measurements uncorrelated. Independent component analysis uses an instantaneous linear mixing model and non-Gaussian structure to estimate a separating transform. We first construct an exact uncorrelated-but-dependent four-point example and calculate a FastICA update by hand. Known waveforms, independent random sources, a Gaussian control and mixture geometry then test what the algorithm recovers and what numerical convergence cannot establish. Iris supplies an observed-data illustration with no known latent-source truth. Population whitening, per-component stopping, frozen transforms and one-to-one recovery matching remain explicit throughout. All twelve non-Gaussian random-source fits meet the angle rule, with matched mean correlations from .98949 to .99986; Gaussian controls show why neither a high score nor numerical stopping identifies a unique source basis.

    The method

    Specify the source model and its ambiguities

    Let observations satisfy \(x=\mu+As\), with \(p\) measured coordinates, \(k\le p\) mutually independent source variables and a full-column-rank mixing matrix \(A\). We estimate \(s\) from the observations without knowing \(A\). This is an instantaneous linear model; it does not represent reverberation, time delays, nonlinear mixtures or an arbitrary collection of measured variables.

    Sign, scale and ordering cannot be inferred from the mixture alone. For an invertible diagonal \(D\) and permutation \(P\),

    \[As=(AP^TD^{-1})(DPs).\]

    With independent non-Gaussian sources, standard ICA identification results recover these directions up to those ambiguities; at most one source can be Gaussian. A multi-source Gaussian subspace permits additional orthogonal rotations with the same distribution. Numerical contrast optimization on a finite sample does not prove independence or select a physically meaningful interpretation.

    Independence means the joint probability law factorizes. Zero covariance constrains only one second moment. A nonlinear dependence can remain even when every pairwise correlation is zero. The central limit theorem motivates searching for non-Gaussian projections, but it does not order every finite weighted sum as “more Gaussian than each constituent” under every contrast. We use specific contrast approximations, which can have local or spurious stationary directions and sampling error.

    Population whitening removes covariance, not all dependence

    For the \(n\) complete fitting rows, let \(X_c\) be the centered row matrix. Compute its population-divisor covariance and retain the leading \(k\) positive eigenvalues:

    \[C=\frac1nX_c^TX_c=E\Lambda E^T,\qquad K=E_k\Lambda_k^{-1/2},\qquad Z=X_cK,\qquad \frac1nZ^TZ=I_k.\]

    Every orthogonal rotation of \(Z\) retains that identity covariance. Thus covariance alone cannot choose the independent orientation. PCA coordinates are not automatically white: whitening additionally divides each retained score by its standard deviation. PCA truncation discards information before ICA and depends on the original feature units. Native ICA centers but does not standardize the input feature columns.

    Derive the deflationary fixed-point update

    For one unit direction \(w\) in white coordinates, approximate non-Gaussianity using a smooth contrast \(G\). Under the constraint \(w^Tw=1\), stationarity requires \(E[z\,g(w^Tz)]-\beta w=0\), with \(g=G'\). A Newton-style approximation replaces the expectation involving \(zz^Tg'\) by \(E[g']I\). Rescaling the resulting direction leads to the FastICA update below. This is a fixed-point method, not a line search that guarantees a monotone empirical contrast at every iteration.

    \[u=\frac1n\sum_i z_i g(w^Tz_i)-\left[\frac1n\sum_i g'(w^Tz_i)\right]w, \quad u\leftarrow u-\sum_{j<c}(u^Tw_j)w_j,\quad w^+=\frac{u}{\|u\|_2},\quad e=1-|{w^+}^Tw|.\]

    Deflation extracts directions sequentially. Two projection passes keep new directions orthogonal to the previous ones. The three implemented derivative pairs are

    \[\begin{aligned} \text{logcosh}:&\quad g(u)=\tanh u,&g'(u)&=1-\tanh^2u,\\ \text{exp}:&\quad g(u)=u e^{-u^2/2},&g'(u)&=(1-u^2)e^{-u^2/2},\\ \text{cube}:&\quad g(u)=u^3,&g'(u)&=3u^2. \end{aligned}\]

    These are contrasts for a non-Gaussianity approximation, not direct independence measurements. Empirical excess kurtosis is \(E[y^4]/E[y^2]^2-3\) for centered sources. A nonzero value rules out Gaussian marginal shape, but zero kurtosis does not imply a Gaussian law or independence. Bounded tanh is less sensitive to large projections than the cubic contrast; no contrast is universally best.

    Fitted maps, diagnostics and numerical contracts

    Let \(R\) contain the extracted orthonormal directions as rows. The fitted source, unmixing and mixing maps are

    \[S=ZR^T=X_cB^T,\qquad B=RK^T,\qquad A_{\rm fit}=E_k\Lambda_k^{1/2}R^T=B^+,\qquad A_{\rm fit}B=E_kE_k^T.\]

    The columns of \(A_{\rm fit}\) are feature-space mixing patterns. The rows of \(B\) are unmixing covectors; they are not interchangeable geometric arrows. With fewer components, reconstruction is the retained PCA projection regardless of the ICA rotation. A tiny full-rank reconstruction error therefore checks algebra, not source separation or statistical independence.

    ICA drops rows with null selected features jointly and exposes their original indices through kept_row_indices(). Retained values must be finite, feature names unique and numeric, the iteration budget positive, and \(0<\mathrm{tol}<1\) finite. A common feature-scale factor stabilizes covariance formation. The relative rank cutoff is \(\max(n,p)\epsilon\lambda_{\max}\); asking for too many nondegenerate dimensions is an error. A zero deflated update raises an exception instead of NaNs.

    converged() means every direction had \(e<\mathrm{tol}\) within its budget. It is not an accuracy or independence certificate. component_diagnostics() retains individual stopping flags, angle changes, population variances and kurtoses; convergence_history() records every completed direction update before final signs. Even an exhausted fit retains orthonormal directions and a valid reconstruction map. The first training value with largest absolute magnitude fixes each component's sign positive; components remain in extraction order. Seed reproducibility assumes the same standard library's normal-distribution implementation, not every platform.

    transform() always uses the fitted means and unmixing map, so an observation gives the same answer alone or in a batch. Query null/nonfinite values are rejected rather than omitted. inverse_transform() adds back the training mean. A known-source benchmark uses ica_source_matches() to maximize the total absolute correlation with a one-to-one Hungarian assignment. It also supplies sign/scale/offset alignment for plotting. Truth is used only for scoring, never for fitting; unknown observed data have no such recovery score.

    Covariance formation costs \(O(np^2)\), with a bounded-sweep \(O(p^3)\) eigensolve. For \(T\) total updates across \(k\) components, fixed-point work is \(O(T(nk+k^2))\). Retained arrays and history use \(O(n(p+k)+p^2+k^2+Tk)\) storage. Source matching adds \(O(nk^2+k^3)\) work.

    Simple examples

    Four equally likely points: decorrelated but dependent

    Take all four combinations of \((s_1,s_2)\in\{-1,1\}^2\), each with probability one quarter. The sources are independent, centered and have variance one. Rotate them: \(u=(s_1+s_2)/\sqrt2\), \(v=(s_1-s_2)/\sqrt2\). The rotated variables also have variance one and covariance zero, but exactly one is zero in every row. Thus \(P(u=0)=P(v=0)=1/2\), while \(P(u=0,v=0)=0\ne1/4\). Equivalently, \(u^2+v^2=2\) and their squared values are perfectly negatively correlated. The native Pearson call below supplies only its coefficient; its inference assumptions are not applied to this discrete four-point construction.

    A cubic update that can be checked by hand

    For the independent corners and a unit direction \(w=(a,b)\), \(E[s_1^4]=E[s_2^4]=1\) and mixed odd moments vanish. With the cubic contrast, \(E[s(s^Tw)^3]-3w=(-2a^3,-2b^3)\). Starting at (.8,.6), the raw update is (−1.024,−.432). After normalization, its direction ratio is \((.6/.8)^3=.421875\). This exact example explains the locally cubic direction convergence of the kurtosis update near a source axis; finite data and other contrasts need their own analysis.

    The same program fits ICA blindly to four mixtures with matrix rows (1,.6),(.3,1.2). The native local-step call uses the already white source corners to verify the hand calculation; the separate fitted model sees only the mixture columns. Its diagnostics check whitening and reconstruction without treating either as a proof that all possible source models have been identified.

    Independent-source recovery and a Gaussian control

    The independent benchmark draws separate Laplace, uniform and Rademacher source coordinates, each with population variance one before finite-sample variation. Compare it with three independent Gaussian sources using the identical mixing matrix. For each family, four declared data seeds and all three contrasts are fit with the same 500-update budget and 1e-8 tolerance. The plotted score is the mean absolute correlation after one-to-one source matching; the table also reports the worst individual match and numerical stopping.

    Gaussian nonidentifiability is a population statement, not a hypothesis test based on one low score. If \(s\sim N(0,I)\), every orthogonal \(Q\) gives \(Qs\sim N(0,I)\) because \(\|Qs\|^2=\|s\|^2\). The rotated Gaussian coordinates are also independent. No observation-only method can distinguish these alternative source bases. Finite samples can still produce a converged contrast direction and accidentally high matching correlations; neither contradicts the identification failure. These seed runs are descriptive sensitivity checks, not confidence intervals or significance tests.

    Iris: observed components without source truth

    Iris has 150 flowers, four numeric measurements in centimetres and three species. Fit all four measurement columns with logcosh, a 1000-update budget and 1e-7 angle tolerance. No species label participates in centring, whitening or component extraction; labels colour the scatter afterwards using the retained original-row map. Compare retaining two and four PCA dimensions before ICA, and report every component's numerical status and kurtosis.

    These measurements are not supplied with a validated instantaneous independent-source mixing model. Neither a visually separated species group nor non-Gaussian component kurtosis establishes biological latent causes or independent sources.     Conclusion

    The four-point example proves directly that whitening does not establish independence. The exact cubic update explains how non-Gaussian shape can select a direction after whitening, and the known-source benchmarks evaluate recovery with a one-to-one match. The Gaussian control illustrates an identification limit that numerical convergence cannot remove. The deterministic waveform recovery is useful evidence for that construction, rather than a proof that all signals sharing a time index are independent.

    Mixing-pattern geometry, reconstruction accuracy, contrast stopping and source recovery answer different questions. Iris has no source truth, so its fitted coordinates and kurtoses remain a descriptive illustration. In practice, source-model assumptions, finite-sample stability, component count and the downstream use all need assessment; a converged FastICA fit alone does not settle them.
