    Sparse Bayesian regression: actual precision updates and matched predictive variance

    Abstract

    Fit a Gaussian-kernel regression model with independent prior precisions that can suppress individual basis functions. Actual posterior and hyperparameter histories precede the original 41-point noiseless sine fit and 201-point prediction grid.

    Mechanism and mathematical scope

    For bias-first design Phi and diagonal prior precision A,

    \[\Sigma=(A+\beta\Phi^T\Phi)^{-1},\qquad\mu=\beta\Sigma\Phi^Tt.\]

    Re-estimate gamma_j=1-alpha_j*Sigma_jj, alpha_j=gamma_j/mu_j2 and beta=(n-sum gamma_j)/(SSE+1e-12), with the documented finite fallback rules. A positive finite threshold selects active nonbias bases; the bias remains. Crucially the final active model is refitted using the final reported alpha and beta, so mean and covariance match. Predictive variance is phiT*Sigma*phi+1/beta. This fixes the historical stale-weight/pruning mismatch. Fixed-point updates have no global evidence optimum guarantee; no monotone objective is asserted. Cholesky uses widened arithmetic and may reject numerically non-SPD systems.

    API and interpretation

    The original 300-update run reaches its budget without satisfying the log-alpha stopping criterion. Its close sine fit therefore does not establish hyperparameter convergence; the actual update plot shows the unresolved precision changes.

    Original targets contain no sampled noise. The plotted conditional Gaussian intervals are model quantities, not an empirical calibration certificate. Kernel scale remains a caller choice; sparse Bayesian regression does not remove all tuning choices. Final refitting intentionally changes historical finite-budget weights that had been paired with newer hyperparameters and then masked without refitting.
