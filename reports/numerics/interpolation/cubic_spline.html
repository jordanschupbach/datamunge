    Natural Cubic Splines: Moment Systems, Smoothness and Boundary Limits

    Abstract

    Given values at ordered knots, we want a smooth interpolating curve without forcing one high-degree polynomial across the whole domain. Natural cubic splines join local cubics with continuous values, slopes and second derivatives, using zero second derivative at the two endpoints. We solve a three-knot example by hand and expose its actual native moment system and coefficients, then retain the original eleven-node Runge comparison with its full polynomial overshoot visible. Independent refinement controls separate compatible and mismatched endpoint curvature: fourth-order behavior is not automatic for every smooth function under natural boundary conditions. Small counterexamples also show that C2 continuity does not guarantee monotonicity, positivity or linear extrapolation. Prepared native evaluation supplies derivatives and repeated queries without rescanning mutable coefficient arrays.

    Three knots, two cubics, one unknown moment

    Start with (0,0), (1,1), (2,0). On interval i let t=x−x_i and S_i(x)=a_i+b_i t+c_i t²+d_i t³. Write h_i=x_(i+1)−x_i>0 and m_i=S''(x_i). Since the second derivative of a cubic is linear,

    \[S_i''(x)=m_i+\frac{m_{i+1}-m_i}{h_i}t.\]

    Integrating twice and imposing both endpoint values gives

    \[a_i=y_i,\quad b_i=\frac{y_{i+1}-y_i}{h_i}-\frac{h_i(2m_i+m_{i+1})}{6},\quad c_i=\frac{m_i}{2},\quad d_i=\frac{m_{i+1}-m_i}{6h_i}.\]

    Matching the first derivative between neighboring pieces yields the interior tridiagonal system

    \[h_{i-1}m_{i-1}+2(h_{i-1}+h_i)m_i+h_i m_{i+1} =6\left(\frac{y_{i+1}-y_i}{h_i}-\frac{y_i-y_{i-1}}{h_{i-1}}\right).\]

    Natural endpoints set m_0=m_(n−1)=0. Here both widths are one and the secants are +1 and −1, so 4m_1=−12 and m_1=−3. The two pieces become S_0(t)=1.5t−0.5t³ and S_1(t)=1−1.5t²+0.5t³. In particular S(0.5)=0.6875, and both pieces meet at x=1 with slope zero and second derivative −3.

    Why the solution exists, and what natural means

    After fixing the endpoint moments, the matrix is symmetric. Each diagonal 2(h_(i−1)+h_i) exceeds the sum of its nonnegative off-diagonal entries, so it is positive definite. The moment system has a unique solution, and Thomas elimination has positive pivots in exact arithmetic. Assembly, factorization, back substitution and coefficient construction take O(n) work and storage. The traced and ordinary fit use one executing kernel, with owned array copies only when a trace is requested.

    Natural specifies endpoint second derivatives, not endpoint slopes. It also has a useful variational meaning. Among interpolants with a square-integrable second derivative on the knot span [x_0,x_(n−1)], the natural spline minimizes the bending functional E(g)=integral from the first to the last knot of (g''(x))² dx. To see why, write g=S+v, with v zero at every knot. Integrating integral S''v'' by parts on every interval gives zero: S''' is constant inside each piece, v vanishes at knots, S'' is continuous across interior knots, and its endpoint values are zero. Therefore

    \[E(S+v)=E(S)+E(v)\ge E(S).\]

    Equality requires v''=0, so v is a line; with at least two distinct knots it vanishes identically. This explains the natural boundary choice and uniqueness. It does not impose monotonicity or keep every piece inside its data range. Floating calculations approximate this ideal system; finite checks do not certify conditioning or an exact energy inequality.

    Retain the original Runge comparison, without clipping the polynomial

    Use f(x)=1/(1+25x²) at eleven equally spaced knots from −1 to 1. These are the original coordinates and values. The global interpolating polynomial has degree at most ten; the natural spline has ten cubic pieces. Both interpolate the same knot data, yet their behavior between knots can differ substantially.

    This is one useful interpolation comparison, not a theorem that every cubic spline is accurate for every knot choice. Global polynomial interpolation on unsuitable nodes can oscillate even in exact arithmetic; evaluating the same polynomial with a different formula does not change that interpolation problem. Likewise, natural boundary conditions can introduce their own approximation error.

    Boundary compatibility changes the refinement rate

    On a uniform mesh, the interior moment equation is m_(i−1)+4m_i+m_(i+1)=6(y_(i−1)−2y_i+y_(i+1))/h². For a smooth function, substituting its true second derivatives leaves an O(h²) residual. The strictly diagonally dominant interior matrix has an inverse bounded in maximum norm independently of the grid size. If the endpoint second derivatives also match the natural zeros, the moment errors are O(h²). They enter the local spline formula multiplied by h², giving O(h⁴) value error together with the local cubic interpolation remainder, under the stated smoothness and compatible boundary assumptions.

    For f(x)=x², however, the true second derivatives at both ends are two. Writing e_i=m_i−2 gives e_0=e_N=−2 and the homogeneous interior recurrence e_(i−1)+4e_i+e_(i+1)=0. Its decaying root is −2+sqrt(3), so an O(1) moment mismatch persists near each boundary. On an interval, with λ=(x−x_i)/h, the value error is

    \[S(x)-x^2=\frac{h^2}{6}\left[e_i((1-\lambda)^3-(1-\lambda)) +e_{i+1}(\lambda^3-\lambda)\right].\]

    Thus the maximum error near the ends is of order h², despite f being a smooth polynomial. Natural splines reproduce lines, but need not reproduce quadratics or arbitrary cubics whose endpoint curvature violates the chosen boundary data. The next controlled study contrasts x² with sin(πx), whose endpoint second derivatives vanish. Every displayed error is a native finite-grid census.

    C2 smoothness does not preserve shape, and natural extrapolation is still cubic

    Fit nondecreasing samples (0,0), (1,1), (2,1). The natural spline has S(1.5)=1.09375, above the largest sample. At x=2 its second derivative is zero, yet its last cubic coefficient is nonzero. Extending that piece to x=3 gives S(3)=1, while extending the endpoint tangent line would give 0.75. Natural imposes curvature at one endpoint, not a linear function everywhere outside it. Another nonnegative dataset, (0,0), (1,0), (2,1), gives the negative value S(0.5)=−0.09375. Neither monotonicity nor positivity follows from C2 continuity.

    Native contracts and verification scope

    The original CubicSpline five-vector layout, fitting/evaluation names and interpolation.hpp include path remain available. Finite increasing knots, matching finite ordinates and complete coefficient shapes now validate explicitly. Empty fitting input gives a zero model, a singleton gives a constant and two knots give a line. Those degenerate conventions are library choices, separate from the uniqueness statement for a natural interpolant through at least two knots.

    A mutable public CubicSpline can be changed by its caller. Consequently, cubic_spline_eval() checks its full record in O(n) time before binary lookup. PreparedCubicSpline owns and validates one snapshot, then handles repeated scalar/derivative queries in O(log n) and q-query batches in O(q log n). Values and derivative orders zero through three are supported; the third derivative can jump at a knot, where the right interval is selected. The last knot uses the last interval. Outside queries extrapolate the end cubic; their coordinates are not clamped. A structurally valid manually supplied record need not satisfy natural boundaries or C2 continuity.

    The fit trace defaults to a complete 100,000-knot cap, checked after full input validation and before allocation. The sampled error census defaults to a complete 1,000,000-query cap, with full finite-query/callback-presence checks before allocation or invocation. It calls each callback once per query, retains actual values and uses first-maximum ties and a scaled RMS. Empty error input has an explicit false existence flag and zero placeholders. Zero optional caps disable those caps only; allocation and container limits remain. Callback exceptions propagate. Input arrays, fitted records and diagnostics retain no borrowed views; the prepared coefficient accessor deliberately borrows its object's const record.

    Arithmetic must remain representable in the checked floating calculation. Unrepresentable widths, secants, system entries, coefficients, query offsets or evaluation results raise an overflow error. Ordinary rounding and extreme knot scales can still affect accuracy; validation is not a conditioning certificate. There is no shape-preserving modification, automatic boundary-condition choice or adaptive global-error guarantee hidden inside this natural spline API.

    No external dataset is required for these manufactured interpolation controls. Native tests retain the original Runge result, hand-solved moments, irregular knot continuity and actual factor replay, 1,000 random linearity controls, all derivative-side conventions, shape/finite/cap validation, empty/constant/line cases, both convergence families and stable large-error RMS calculations. A permanent optional Python Fraction oracle solves the full cubic value/C1/C2/ natural-boundary constraint matrix by exact dense elimination, independently of the native moment/Thomas formulation. It checks 102 fits, their coefficients, moments, extrapolated values and derivatives. The focused C++ tests and native oracle checker also run under address and undefined-behavior sanitizers.
