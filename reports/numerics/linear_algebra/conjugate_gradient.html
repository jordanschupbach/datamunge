    Conjugate Gradient: Directions, Residuals and Reliable Stopping

    Abstract

    Given a symmetric positive-definite matrix or linear operator A and a right-hand side b, find x with Ax=b without factoring the matrix. Large sparse systems make repeated matrix-vector products attractive, but a small recursively updated residual can conceal error in floating arithmetic. Conjugate gradient builds conjugate search directions and minimises the quadratic over growing Krylov spaces. This report works through two-dimensional steps, separates energy error from residual norm, reproduces the original twenty-unknown Laplacian comparison and checks the role of the spectrum. Native datamunge execution histories retain the actual operands and count both recurrence and residual-check products.

    The method: minimise a positive quadratic

    For a real symmetric positive-definite A, the unique minimiser of

    \[\phi(x)=\tfrac12 x^\top Ax-b^\top x\]

    solves Ax=b. Its gradient is Ax−b=−r, correcting the sign in the original report. For an iterate x and direction p, differentiating φ(x+αp) gives the exact line minimiser α=pᵀr/(pᵀAp). CG starts with x₀=0, r₀=p₀=b and uses

    \[\alpha_k=\frac{r_k^\top r_k}{p_k^\top Ap_k},\qquad x_{k+1}=x_k+\alpha_kp_k,\qquad r_{k+1}=r_k-\alpha_kAp_k,\]
    \[\beta_k=\frac{r_{k+1}^\top r_{k+1}}{r_k^\top r_k},\qquad p_{k+1}=r_{k+1}+\beta_kp_k.\]

    In exact arithmetic, the residuals are mutually orthogonal and the nonzero directions are A-conjugate: pᵢᵀApⱼ=0 for i≠j. Inductively, rₖ is orthogonal to the preceding Krylov space Kₖ(A,b)=span(b,Ab,…,Aᵏ⁻¹b), and xₖ minimises φ over that space. SPD curvature makes the minimiser unique. There are at most n independent directions, proving termination within n steps in exact arithmetic. This proof does not give a floating-point iteration guarantee.

    Two directions that can be checked by hand

    Take A=diag(1,4) and b=(1,1). The answer is x*=(1,1/4). The first α is 2/5, so x₁=(0.4,0.4), r₁=(0.6,−0.6), β₀=0.36 and p₁=(0.96,−0.24). Its curvature product with p₀ is 0.96−0.96=0; the second line minimisation reaches the answer in exact arithmetic.     Energy error decreases; the residual norm can rise

    Writing e=x−x*, subtracting the minimum quadratic value yields φ(x)−φ(x*)=½eᵀAe. Exact CG therefore decreases the energy norm ||e||A. It does not minimise the Euclidean residual norm ||b−Ax||₂ at every step. For A=diag(1,100), b=(1,0.1), the first α=1.01/2=0.505 makes r₁=(0.495,−4.95): the residual grows while the energy error falls.

    For SPD A, λmin||e||₂≤||r||₂≤λmax||e||₂ in exact arithmetic. A small residual alone therefore gives no scale-free small forward-error claim. The familiar exact-arithmetic energy bound, with κ=λmax/λmin, is

    \[\frac{\|e_k\|_A}{\|e_0\|_A} \le 2\left(\frac{\sqrt\kappa-1}{\sqrt\kappa+1}\right)^k.\]

    To see why, any Krylov iterate has error qₖ(A)e₀ for a polynomial of degree at most k with qₖ(0)=1. CG chooses the smallest energy error in that space. Bounding |qₖ(A)e₀||A by max over the spectrum of |qₖ times ||e₀||A and choosing a scaled Chebyshev polynomial on [λmin,λmax] gives this bound. It is a worst-case envelope; eigenvalue locations and active RHS components can make observed convergence much faster. Rounding can invalidate exact orthogonality, conjugacy and the finite-termination argument.

    Original example: twenty interior Dirichlet unknowns

    The unscaled matrix L=tridiag(−1,2,−1) with homogeneous boundary values is SPD: xᵀLx=Σ from i=0 to n of (xᵢ₊₁−xᵢ)² with x₀=xₙ₊₁=0; only the zero vector makes every term vanish. For unit RHS its exact discrete solution is xᵢ=i(n+1−i)/2, using one-based interior i. Substitution into the second difference proves the formula and supplies an independent answer.

    The original report printed a recursively updated CG residual near 2.3e−16. The current API reports a separately recomputed norm and uses widened sums and coefficients. It returns a computed zero for this exactly represented solution on this toolchain. This change in measured arithmetic is explicit: it is not a claim of universal machine-zero or ten-step convergence.

    Why ten rather than twenty? The Laplacian eigenvectors have components sin(ijπ/(n+1)) and eigenvalues λⱼ=2−2cos(jπ/(n+1)). For even n and a unit RHS, reflection symmetry removes all even-j modes: only n/2 distinct eigenvalues are active. A degree-n/2 polynomial can vanish on those eigenvalues, giving at most n/2 steps in exact arithmetic. An arbitrary RHS can activate all n.

    Checking active spectra and sparse work

    With m distinct active eigenvalues, the polynomial product over those roots proves an exact-arithmetic m-step upper bound. A repeated-eigenvalue diagonal control and a single-coordinate eigenvector make this mechanism hand-checkable. The matrix-free Laplacian uses three-point O(n) products and O(n) vector scratch; it never allocates a dense n-by-n matrix.

    The general implementation performs one Ap for the recurrence and one Ax to check the resulting iterate at every completed step. Those diagnostic products are included in products. An unsuccessful curvature attempt adds one Ap without a completed step. Thus dense checked solving costs O(n²+kn²) after input validation, and a sparse operator with product cost T(n) costs O(k(T(n)+n)). The solve-only path retains O(n) vector scratch; a trace stores four n-vectors per step, O(kn) additional entries. The displayed work is not a wall-clock ranking or a complete operation-by-operation equality between CG and GS.

    For this Laplacian κ=cot²(π/(2(n+1))) grows quadratically in n. Preconditioning is often needed for larger problems; this API demonstrates unpreconditioned CG. No external dataset is needed: the analytic solution and controllable spectrum give stronger checks of the numerical claims.

    Input contracts, stopping and honest failure

    The tolerance is absolute and inclusive. A zero RHS or empty system can converge initially; a zero iteration budget returns x=0 with residual |b|, without claiming convergence unless that norm is within tolerance. All finite entries, dimensions, controls and exact stored symmetry are validated before dense work. SPD remains a precondition: detecting positive curvature on visited directions does not certify the entire matrix. A matrix-free callback must itself be a deterministic linear SPD map and return a finite matching vector.

    Negative curvature has explicit reason: true. A 15-entry trace cap rejects the 16-entry two-step history. Nonsymmetry is rejected even for zero RHS. Tiny RHS solves without squared-double underflow: true; x=5.0e-201.

    The recursive vector r is separately retained from the recomputed b−Ax norm. If the former reaches tolerance or collapses to zero while the latter does not, CGStop::residual_gap stops unconverged. This implementation does not silently restart, replace residuals or use a relative stopping rule. Nonfinite or unrepresentable arithmetic throws. Inner products and norms widen to long double; vector/operator output remains double. The recomputed norm is based on the rounded returned Ax and widened differences: it is a floating diagnostic, not an interval certificate of the mathematical residual.

    A positive max_trace_values caps actual stored scalar entries, checked after positive curvature and before updating the next iterate. A cap failure returns no partial result; callback effects already performed are not rolled back. Zero means unlimited. Solve-only calls ignore this optional storage cap. Histories are actual execution records and never obtain their states by rerunning prefixes.

    Conclusion

    Conjugate directions explain the small worked solution and the ten-step unit-RHS Laplacian result. The energy interpretation and active-spectrum polynomial explain more than an assertion that every residual must decrease. Native histories now show the actual recurrence and recomputed residual, with complete operator costs, validated contracts and explicit unconverged stops. Finite termination belongs to exact arithmetic; the residual check and analytic controls establish what these particular floating runs achieved.
