    Native vsim robot sorting and RL comparison

    Robot sorting with in-house articulated physics

    The sorting environment now runs on datamunge's native C++ simulation engine. No MuJoCo or Jolt runtime is required. Both training and evaluation explicitly select ArticulatedBackend::Native; there is no fallback to another engine. The previous MuJoCo experiment retains its original results. Those scores, classifier adaptation and videos are historical results from that engine, not measurements of this port.

    What the native engine integrates

    The reusable UrdfModel loader reads link masses, centers of mass, full rotated inertia tensors, joint origins/axes, limits, effort/rate limits, fixed joints and mimic gearing. The core supports compound colliders, convex STL proxies, ellipsoids, sliders, hinges, passive damping/dry friction, reflected drive inertia, force-limited implicit velocity servos and sliding/rolling/spinning friction. A coupled bilateral solve transmits forces throughout the robot. Fruit remains a free rigid body: opposing finger contact forces establish a grasp, releases retain velocity, and bin scoring requires actual support forces and settling. Conveyor traction comes from contact slip against a moving surface.

    Torque-free rotation uses an implicit midpoint Euler/Cayley update. Analytic tests check both energy and world angular momentum, including rapid asymmetric tumbling. Other checks cover motor saturation, reflected inertia, coupled loads, mimic constraints, collision witnesses, bin support, snapshots and import rollback.

    This is a distinct maximal-coordinate constraint solver, with discrete contact at 960 Hz for this example. It does not reproduce MuJoCo's soft-contact equations, full implicit integrator or elliptic friction model. Meshes use sampled convex hulls; finger concavities use explicit clipped pieces. Nominal finger mass, friction, damping and motor parameters still need hardware calibration. Continuous-joint coordinates wrap into one revolution; this robot uses bounded joints.

    Native port

    The wrist view draws the live MobileNet class and probability beside the foreground box. Overlay inference uses the current clean crop and does not change controller decisions.

    The native port report runs the real Seeed meshes, actual wrist RGB classification, recorded joint/body poses and CUDA presentation rendering. It diagnoses why the transferred MuJoCo scheduler sorted nothing, adds two opt-in grasp corrections (grasp_alignment_tolerance, hold_grasp_target) and compares schedulers on held-out streams. Natively trained schedulers did not beat a greedy rule, so its 60-second video uses the greedy rule with the corrected grasp at a dense 0.4/s arrival stream.

    Fresh native comparison and training

    The complete program below retains the four-method comparison: Q(lambda), Double DQN, dueling Double DQN and PPO. It trains on native physics, screens checkpoints, selects using independent actual-RGB validation, freezes that selection and reports held-out streams before rendering the default 20-second excerpt. The prior proxy-trained scheduler remains an explicitly historical transfer candidate with a different training budget. The scheduler selects fixed IK skills; it does not learn raw joint torques. Observation masks and elapsed-time discounts are shared across the methods. Apples go to red and bananas to yellow.

    just playground starts a fresh native experiment in build/fruit_stream_native/; its 256 windows per fresh method are substantial work. This port does not yet have completed native comparison results. DATAMUNGE_STREAM_LEARN_ONLY=1 just playground stops after comparison. After that completes, just playground-gpu validates the native source/checkpoint fingerprints and renders the frozen result. DATAMUNGE_VIDEO_SECONDS=5 shortens rendering checks. Old MuJoCo caches are kept in their separate directory and are never accepted as native training evidence.
