    Ramer-Douglas-Peucker: actual finite-segment splits and retained vertices

    Abstract

    A densely sampled polyline often uses more vertices than its geometry requires. Ramer-Douglas-Peucker retains original endpoints and splits only where an intermediate vertex lies beyond a distance tolerance from the finite replacement segment. The actual iterative native split history now supports the method diagram. The original201-point wiggly curve remains, and an overshooting collinear control exposes why infinite-line distance was insufficient.

    Mechanism and mathematical scope

    For a chord from a to b, project p onto its direction and clamp the projection parameter to the finite segment:

    \[t=\operatorname{clamp}_{[0,1]}\frac{(p-a)\cdot(b-a)}{\|b-a\|^2},\qquad d(p,[a,b])=\|p-a-t(b-a)\|.\]

    Coincident endpoints use ordinary point distance. The implementation evaluates equivalent normalized-direction arithmetic in extended precision to avoid squared double overflow. If the first farthest interior vertex exceeds epsilon, retain it and process both child ranges; otherwise replace that range by its chord.

    The epsilon-neighborhood of a segment is convex. When all original vertices of an accepted range lie in it, every original connecting edge does too. Thus the resulting retained chords give a directed original-to-simplified distance bound (up to arithmetic roundoff). This does not prove minimum vertex count, topology preservation or that every retained farthest vertex is necessary in all possible simplifications. Worst-case distance work is quadratic; an iterative pending-range stack uses linear working storage.

    API and interpretation

    The native maximum is the largest original-vertex distance to its assigned retained chord. It upper-bounds distance to the union of all retained chords and is not necessarily that union nearest-distance maximum. Endpoint clamping intentionally corrects the old infinite-supporting-line behavior: the collinear path0 to2 to1 retains its overshoot at epsilon0.5. Every coordinate and epsilon is checked even on short input; arithmetic beyond finite double range throws. The waveform is a native synthetic structural input, not a geographic dataset or a topology guarantee.

    Reusable producers, fixtures and histories live in the canonical C++ library. Captured records own their values and do not come from a separate report replay. Selected counters and record/scalar policies are specified at their API boundaries; they are not peak-byte or wall-time budgets. Synthetic structural inputs are controlled fixtures for these mechanisms.
