    Push-relabel: actual preflow, excess and height changes

    Abstract

    A preflow may leave positive excess at intermediate vertices. The FIFO producer pushes only on admissible residual arcs and relabels a blocked vertex from actual neighboring heights. Its optional history now records the real residual-pair, excess and height updates. The original two-vertex capacity9 example remains, followed by the complete six-vertex CLRS control. Small exact-capacity networks allow independent flow/cut checks; tiny floating capacities expose the old absolute-epsilon failure.

    Mechanism and mathematical scope

    \[h(u)=h(v)+1\text{ permits a residual push};\qquad h(u)\gets1+\min_{(u,v)\text{ residual}}h(v).\]

    API and interpretation

    The solver uses strict positive residuals/excesses rather than silently discarding values below1e-12. Self-loops own valid residual pairs but keep zero flow; parallel and antiparallel inputs keep separate identities. Every positive residual/excess update must make finite representable progress, otherwise the call fails explicitly. This is checked floating arithmetic, so a numerical flow/cut certificate remains appropriate. Counts include final cut scans, and exclude validation, allocation and callback-free container internals; they are not wall-clock measurements.

    Reusable producers, fixtures and histories live in the canonical C++ library. Captured records own their values and do not come from a separate report replay. Selected counters and record/scalar policies are specified at their API boundaries; they are not peak-byte or wall-time budgets. Synthetic structural inputs are controlled fixtures for these mechanisms.
