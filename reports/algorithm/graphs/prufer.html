    Prüfer codes: actual smallest leaves, decoding and exact enumeration

    Abstract

    Deleting the smallest available leaf and recording its neighbor gives one code entry. The native encoder and decoder now retain their actual ordered leaf sets at each removal. Complete tree validation prevents malformed graphs from reaching an empty-set dereference. The original six-vertex tree and its code 3,3,3,4 remain, together with exact enumeration of all 16 four-vertex codes. These discrete fixtures make every removal and the finite bijection check inspectable.

    Mechanism and mathematical scope

    \[\text{code length}=n-2,\qquad \#\text{labeled trees}=n^{n-2}\quad(n\ge2).\]

    API and interpretation

    The ordinary encoder validates connectedness as well as n-1 edges and all endpoints; a disconnected cycle or parallel-edge graph can no longer drive an invalid leaf lookup. Ordered-set coding takes O(n log n) ordinary work, while copied leaf-set histories may grow quadratically. Exhaustive enumeration is a separate exponential exercise with checked integer powers and a sequence-count cap; the mathematical Cayley count is labelled separately from the actually decoded/distinct counts.

    Reusable producers, fixtures and histories live in the canonical C++ library. Captured records own their values and do not come from a separate report replay. Selected counters and record/scalar policies are specified at their API boundaries; they are not peak-byte or wall-time budgets. Synthetic structural inputs are controlled fixtures for these mechanisms.
