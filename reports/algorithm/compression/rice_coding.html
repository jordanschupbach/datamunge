    Rice coding: actual quotients, remainders and conditional lengths

    Abstract

    Encode nonnegative integers as prefix-decodable bit words when small values are common. Rice specializes Golomb coding to a power-of-two model. Actual native emission and parse histories replace the report-local encoder. The original eight integers and finite geometric-weight study remain.

    Mechanism and mathematical scope

    For M=2^k, n=qM+r with 0<=r<M. The producer emits q ones, zero, then k remainder bits:

    \[L_k(n)=\lfloor n/2^k\rfloor+1+k.\]

    The original weights are w_n=2^(-n/8) on n=0,...,299 only. The plotted conditional expectation is sum(w_n L_k(n))/sum(w_n), obtained from actual emitted spans. It is neither the untruncated geometric expectation nor a random compression experiment. Model k and decoded count are external metadata.

    API and interpretation

    Trace decoding requires exactly the requested complete word count and checks the entire input bit bound, including unread tails. Partial attempted words and missing complete words both reject.

    Boolean vectors are logical streams, not packed-byte files. Complete model/count metadata are external. Checked shared Golomb parsing rejects truncation and count overflows and reports only the consumed prefix. The expected length study explicitly normalizes the finite original support.

    Reusable producers, fixtures and histories live in the canonical C++ library. Captured records own their values and do not come from a separate report replay. Selected counters and record/scalar policies are specified at their API boundaries; they are not peak-byte or wall-time budgets. Synthetic structural inputs are controlled fixtures for these mechanisms.
