    QFT: actual gate states, comb phases and simulated storage

    Abstract

    Hadamards, controlled phase rotations and final bit swaps produce a unitary Fourier transform. The native producer now captures the actual state after each simulator gate and validates the complete finite register before it touches caller storage. The original 16-state comb supported at 1,5,9,13 is retained. Its frequency probabilities and phases are directly inspectable; a state-vector simulation is a classical exponential-storage calculation, not a quantum speedup.

    Mechanism and mathematical scope

    \[y_k=\frac1{\sqrt N}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_j e^{2\pi i j k/N},\qquad N=2^n.\]

    API and interpretation

    The sign convention is the positive-exponent unitary DFT; the inverse uses the negative sign. Exact equally spaced peaks are a property of a compatible periodic comb, not every truncated/nondividing support. Offset changes can be invisible to the probability histogram while remaining visible in complex phases. The abstract circuit has quadratic gate count, but each simulated gate touches an exponentially large vector. Actual history stores complete post-gate states and is capped independently of ordinary basis storage.

    Reusable producers, fixtures and histories live in the canonical C++ library. Captured records own their values and do not come from a separate report replay. Selected counters and record/scalar policies are specified at their API boundaries; they are not peak-byte or wall-time budgets. Synthetic structural inputs are controlled fixtures for these mechanisms.
