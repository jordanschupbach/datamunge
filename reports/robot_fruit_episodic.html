    Robot-arm fruit-sorting playground

    Robot-arm fruit-sorting playground

    A conveyor workcell

    This first experiment creates a headless physics environment and imports the real Seeed DevArm CAD model. One randomly selected apple or banana travels down the conveyor. An optical pick sensor stops the belt in front of the arm, and the fruit waits there. The learning task below uses the red bin on one side as the target for either fruit; the yellow bin is a distractor. This first video holds the stopped scene for several seconds.

    The reusable dm::sim::ConveyorEnvironment owns the physical belt, fruit, floor, pedestal and open sorting bins. Its belt uses tangential contact friction, not prescribed fruit positions. Physics runs at 240 Hz; the video samples it at 24 frames per second. A seed controls the fruit sequence independently of rendering. Every run records the seed and trajectory for replay.

    Seed 15675708447074113772 selected *banana* as the first fruit. The pick sensor stopped the belt at *4.642 s*. The fruit is waiting for the robot; no picking policy has run.

    Learning to put every fruit in the red bin

    The objective now ignores fruit identity: both apples and bananas belong in the red bin. The yellow bin is a distractor. Each training episode starts with one fruit already waiting at the sensor, with independently seeded position jitter of up to 8 mm. This separates manipulation learning from the belt's automatic arrival controller shown above.

    The agent chooses among nine actions: move the grasp point by 30 mm along one of the six Cartesian directions, close the gripper, open it, or wait. Numerical inverse kinematics converts each motion into the real URDF joint coordinates; position servos respect the joint limits and a 1.5 rad/s rate limit. The gripper moves at at most 0.08 m/s. There are no automatic reach, carry or deposit actions: the policy must choose its motions and decide when to grasp and release.

    We use tabular Q-learning with learning rate 0.3, discount 0.98 and epsilon-greedy exploration at 0.25. The state table records the Cartesian command cell and whether the fruit is held. Continuous joint/tool/fruit observations remain available through the environment API. Fruit identity is deliberately absent from the policy input. The table is a compact state abstraction for this workcell, rather than a vision policy or a claim of general manipulation capability.

    Each decision costs 0.05; a blocked move costs another 0.15. A released fruit that settles on the red-bin floor earns 10; a failed release or lost fruit earns -3. Potential-based shaping adds 0.98 * Phi(next) - Phi(current), using distance to the fruit before a grasp and distance to a point over the red bin while holding it. Absorbing states have zero potential. Shaping supplies feedback without choosing actions. Episodes end at release/loss or after 80 decisions, with termination and time limits reported separately.

    The next block trains from scratch, saves and reloads the final checkpoint, and evaluates its frozen greedy policy on 128 separate seeds in an independent environment. It also measures an untrained random-action baseline. The video replays the first successful apple and banana from that evaluation, in that order. Those are selected successful examples; the printed evaluation counts include every failure.

    After *2000 training episodes*, the frozen policy placed *67/67 apples* and *61/61 bananas* into the red bin on separate evaluation seeds. The random-action baseline completed *0/64* episodes.

    The selected successful replays use apple seed 9000000001 and banana seed 9000000000. Both execute the reloaded policy without exploration or scripted corrections.

    What this first learning environment models

    The policy controls the real DevArm's joint poses through a kinematic Cartesian position servo. Fruit motion, gravity, conveyor/bin contacts and post-release settling use the native rigid-body engine at 240 Hz. A close command near the fruit creates an ideal stabilized grasp with a size-dependent finger aperture; a missed grasp reopens. Payload orientation stays stabilized during transport. Opening restores a dynamic body, and success requires a released fruit to settle inside the red bin with low speed and physical floor support. Merely closing the gripper, hovering above the bin, or moving the fruit while held cannot succeed.

    This model uses conservative tool/payload collision guards. It does not yet model full arm self-collision, motor torques, slip, deformable fruit, noisy vision or hardware timing. The camera rendering includes the actual CAD arm and depth occlusion; cached static scenery receives no moving-object shadows. The reported success rate describes this simplified workcell and its two fruit proxies, not a hardware transfer result.

    Source model: Seeed reBot DevArm, revision 922c5fa3d8c8919db1847b49166f02fc26c8fdad, B601-DM. dm::datasets::fetch downloads missing assets and verifies the pinned hashes. The hardware assets are © 2026 Seeed Studio Co., Ltd., CERN-OHL-W-2.0; the downloaded license remains alongside the cached model.

    Wrist-camera vision with varied fruit and visible localization

    We assign *apples to red* and *bananas to yellow* using a camera rigidly mounted to the real DevArm's end_link. The classifier receives only RGB pixels; its prediction chooses the destination before the first arm action.

    Each class has *six geometry prototypes*: apples vary in girth, taper and lobing; bananas vary in length, curvature and thickness. Each new fruit receives an independent scale from 0.85 to 1.15, mottled skin texture, material roughness, optional apple leaf, full-turn random yaw and small pitch/roll tilts. Both classes draw from the same green, yellow, red/russet and brown palette. A fruit enters at a random position along and across the belt, then travels under friction until the optical sensor stops it. Rendered orientation and size follow the physical proxy. Rotation remains stabilized after spawning, as in the manipulation baseline; this is not a rolling/deformable-fruit model.

    The blue wrist camera follows actual joint kinematics and never aims using fruit coordinates or labels. Its fixed local mount is (-0.09,-0.075,0.045) m, viewing local (0.13,0,0) with local +Z up. We use a 95-degree field of view and 128 by 128 RGB pixels to accommodate off-centre arrivals and long bananas.

    A *calibrated RGB background-subtraction localizer* compares the camera view with a registered empty workcell at the same arm pose, including its moving tread marks. It uses RGB differences, without reading object IDs, depth, fruit coordinates or labels. The green box bounds the visible foreground. Its square, padded crop is resized to 64 by 64 and supplied to MobileNet. Boxes and prediction borders are drawn afterward for display. This localizer assumes known synthetic scenery; it is not a trained object detector and does not establish robustness to arbitrary backgrounds.

    The full MobileNet-v1 uses width 0.125, all thirteen depthwise/pointwise blocks, ReLU6, BN and global average pooling. All 57,738 parameters are trained from scratch with native Adam/autograd. Training includes brightness jitter and 25% grayscale augmentation. We also report grayscale test accuracy to check reliance on color.

    We render *1,024 training scenes from prototypes 0–3*, *256 validation scenes from prototype 4*, and *256 test scenes from prototype 5*, balanced by class and training prototype. Seed ranges are disjoint; validation loss selects the checkpoint and test prototypes never supply classifier gradients. This is a stronger check than changing the seed of the same geometry. The manifest records each seed, inspection position, sampled appearance parameters and visible bounding box.

    Both placement policies are retrained for 8,000 randomized episodes each. Motor training uses all six prototypes with independent seeds; the prototype holdout applies to the image classifier. The motor state adds five observed across-belt position bins before grasp; carrying states are shared. The servo aligns the jaws to the banana proxy's short axis and sizes its aperture to the payload. These motor controls still use simulator geometry and the stabilized proximity grasp; fruit pose is not estimated by the image classifier. There is no ground-truth class routing or corrective fallback. End-to-end evaluation includes every held-out test scene; the demonstration video then selects two successful examples of each class.

    On *256 held-out prototype-5 scenes*, MobileNet classified *241/256* RGB crops correctly and *236/256* grayscale crops correctly. Validation selected epoch *38*.

    Using only those class predictions for routing, the robot correctly sorted *126/128 apples into red* and *115/128 bananas into yellow*. All 256 attempts appear in the evaluation CSV.

    The video shows four successful held-out episodes: seeds 14000000016, 14000000001, 14000000023, 14000000005. Feeding is shown at 4x speed; arm manipulation is real time. Green rectangles are live RGB localization boxes; red/yellow inset borders show the latched MobileNet prediction.
