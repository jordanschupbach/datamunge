    Least-Squares GAN: Raw Scores and Explicit Targets

    Abstract

    A compact adversarial game fits 1000 balanced MNIST digits, with 200 validation and 200 held-out test images. The generator maps 16 Gaussian coordinates through 32 tanh units to 784 sigmoid pixels; the discriminator produces one raw source score. Digit labels do not enter either network or its objective. Three fixed epochs perform 96 discriminator writes and 96 generator writes, always recomputing the generator loss through the updated discriminator. Canonical C++ maps and objectives supply the model; the complete editable program exposes every update. The short run produces weak grey images. Game losses and coarse-pixel MMD remain diagnostics, and the fitting-mean baseline scores better on MMD. Exact source parity, independent numerical checks, checkpoint restore and noise controls preserve this result.

    Follow the supplied inputs through the game

    The architecture and objective follow the repository's compact teaching experiment. The method reference is [Mao et al., Least Squares Generative Adversarial Networks (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04076). The discriminator receives an image alone. A source target enters its loss, not its forward input. No sigmoid turns the discriminator score into a probability.

    Start with source scores whose losses can be checked by hand

    For separate real and fake pools, this experiment uses a=0,b=c=1:

    \[L_D=\tfrac12(\operatorname{mean}(r-1)^2+\operatorname{mean}f^2),\quad L_G=\tfrac12\operatorname{mean}(f-1)^2.\]

    The half multiplies the sum of the two means, preserving the source Float32 grouping. Derivatives are(r-1)/N,f/M for D, and(f-1)/M for G. For real scores(0,1,2) and fake scores(-2,-1,0,1), D=13/12 and G=7/4. A confident wrong score keeps a nonzero quadratic derivative. Scores remain unbounded. The cited paper also analyses different labels a=-1,b=1,c=0 for its Pearson-divergence relation; that relation is not asserted for this experiment's 0/1 targets. The explicit .5 reporting threshold below is a source accuracy convention, not a fitted decision boundary or calibrated probability.

    Hand-check: D=0.500000000,G=0.500000000; D derivatives=-1,0; G derivative=-1.

    Native preparation and separated digit pools

    The program prepares the native mnist-assets recipe and loads its saliency pools. Fitting/validation are disjoint subsets of original MNIST training data; test IDs remain in the original test range. Every class contributes 100 fitting and 20 validation/test images. The loader checks shape, IDs, balance and pixel scaling to Float32[0,1]. The preview takes the first two fitting observations per class, without model selection. Labels describe the dataset balance and preview only; fitting ignores them. The initial test forward is archived as an explicit diagnostic control; test outcomes never choose weights, noise banks, hyperparameters or epochs. Validation only logs diagnostics.

The gallery retains the first 40 fixed-bank outputs, without choosing attractive samples. Most remain grey fields rather than convincing digits. A low D loss or finite G loss does not turn this short game into a successful image model.

    The coarse diagnostic has a deliberately visible blind spot

    The MMD diagnostic first averages each 7×7 pixel block, giving16 coordinates per image, and divides those coordinates by4. It then averages IMQ kernels with constants1,4,16 and excludes within-pool diagonals in the unbiased two-sample estimate. This statistic can be negative at finite sample size and discards fine digit structure. The baseline repeats the fitting-only mean image200 times; it uses no held-out pixels to construct it. Its much smaller value shows why coarse MMD alone cannot certify sample quality or coverage. Neither the mean nor MMD adds a fitting objective or selects the checkpoint.

    Repeated fitting mean: MMD²=0.0001357794; generator: 0.1181927919. Both use the same200 held-out images.

    Changed noise is a control, not evidence of realistic samples

    Seed4877 supplies the changed bank; the zero bank has the same shape. The restored G responds to both changes, while standalone generation on the fixed bank matches the full forward bit for bit. The first sample agrees between single and batch inference (maximum error0). Every saved weight restores exactly, and all diagnostic calls leave both stores unchanged. Responsiveness proves dependence on the input, not useful mode coverage, diversity of recognizable digits or convergence.

    Rows: fixed seed4831; changed seed4877; zero input. All control weights unchanged.

    Verification and limits

    The unchanged original delegated to the new core, and this complete report, each match all 7 archives/762721 values exactly in dtype, shape and value bytes, the history and batch TSVs, all 2 source PNGs and every non-timing JSON value. Duration is remeasured. Native source snapshots and checksums retain the frozen baseline.

    The independent NumPy checker imports no datamunge and performs no fitting. It checks 653161 forward/control values, every one of the51569 initial parameter derivatives, 22 scalar finite differences and all 3000 fitting-row visits. It includes the explicit source-score algebra and its derivatives. Component checks separately exercise owned records, malformed inputs, live continuous paths and D-then-fresh-G ownership in native C++, Python and R. Those component checks do not complete independent Python/R experiment loops.

    This is one fixed short run, with no seed sweep, uncertainty estimate or paper-scale image benchmark. The training schedule and width are intentionally preserved. The result illustrates leastsquares source objectives; it supplies no evidence that this small game has converged. The weak samples and strong constant-baseline MMD remain part of the explanation.

    Method reference: [Mao et al., Least Squares Generative Adversarial Networks (2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1611.04076). Seeds, dimensions, source IDs, update order and artifacts follow the original repository experiment. Canonical native tensor maps, stable losses, seeded Gaussian generation and IMQ diagnostics provide the reusable computations.
