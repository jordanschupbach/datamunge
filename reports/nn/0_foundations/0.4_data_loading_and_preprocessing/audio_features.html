    Audio Features: From Samples to Log-Mel Bands

    Abstract

    A sampled waveform is too long and phase-sensitive to use directly as a compact audio feature. We turn local windows into frequency powers and then into log-mel bands. Tiny exact frames expose the FFT convention; a generated 1 kHz tone tests window gain, framing and the silence floor. WAV round trips quantify the separate PCM storage error.

    The method

    For a frame of length \(L\), hop \(H\) and FFT length \(N\ge L\), the periodic Hann window is \(w_n=(1-\cos(2\pi n/L))/2\). The unnormalised transform and power are

    \[X_{t,k}=\sum_{n=0}^{L-1}x_{tH+n}w_n e^{-2\pi i kn/N},\qquad P_{t,k}=|X_{t,k}|^2.\]

    Without padding or centring, a signal of length S has max(0,1+floor((S-L)/H)) frames. A real transform keeps bins 0 through floor(N/2). For even N, Parseval requires doubling interior one-sided powers but keeping DC and Nyquist once; divide their sum by N. The mel coordinate is \(1127\log(1+f/700)\). Unit-height triangles are equally spaced in mel, their weighted powers are summed, and a natural-log floor prevents log(0). These are powers, not amplitudes or decibels; no sample-rate conversion occurs. FFT cost is O(frames N log N); multiplying a dense B-band filter bank costs O(frames N B).

    A periodic 1 kHz sine sampled at 8 kHz lies at bin 32 of a 256-point FFT. The Hann peak amplitude is A L/4 = 24 for A=.375. Doubling N moves the same peak to bin 64.

    Cepstral coefficients, storage and the silence floor

    A type-II discrete cosine transform of the log-mel bands produces mel-frequency cepstral coefficients (MFCCs). Keeping the first 13 coefficients summarizes the smooth spectral envelope. The example first checks the cosine sum on a single frame, then checks that a signal too short for a complete analysis window yields zero MFCC frames.

    MFCC shape [15, 13], independent DCT error 5.3e-15. Short signals preserve zero frames.

    PCM16 maximum error: 0.000006102; sample rate: 8000 Hz.

    Silence log-mel mean: -13.815511; short-signal frames: 0.

    Conclusion

    The impulse makes the window's gain explicit. The tone checks both the bin-frequency mapping and the one-sided energy convention, while silence remains finite. These features discard phase and depend on window, hop, band edges and log floor; they do not by themselves recognise sound.
