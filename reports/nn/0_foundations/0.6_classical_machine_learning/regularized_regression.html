    Regularized Regression: Shrinkage and Exact Zeros

    Abstract

    An unpenalised fit can use large unstable coefficients or retain weak predictors. We compare ridge, lasso and elastic-net proximal updates on an orthogonal design with a closed-form optimum. A fixed penalty path makes shrinkage and exact threshold zeros visible without claiming a validation-selected penalty.

    The method

    With nonnegative mask m (zero for an unpenalised intercept), the objective is

    \[J(w)=\frac{\|Xw-y\|^2}{2N}+\lambda_1\sum_j m_j|w_j|+ \frac{\lambda_2}{2}\sum_j m_jw_j^2,\quad w_j^*=\frac{\mathrm{sign}(a_j)\max(|a_j|-\lambda_1,0)}{1+\lambda_2} \quad(X^TX/N=I,m_j=1).\]

    For the smooth part, g=Xᵀ(Xw-y)/N+lambda2 m*w. A step first forms z=w-rate*g and then soft-thresholds z by rate*lambda1*m. A sufficiently small step (at most the reciprocal smooth Lipschitz constant) gives descent. Lasso's proximal step produces exact zeros; taking an ordinary gradient of w generally does not. Penalty strength depends on loss normalisation and predictor scales. The visible loop uses tensor SGD for the smooth objective, then applies the L1 proximal map under NoGradGuard. copy_ preserves the learnable leaf captured by the optimizer; rebinding w would leave that optimizer attached to the old tensor. Adam with the same scalar threshold would not implement this Euclidean proximal-SGD objective.

    Optimality with correlated predictors

    On correlated columns, a coordinate-wise closed form no longer solves the whole problem. Check the KKT conditions instead. A nonzero coefficient requires the smooth gradient to cancel the signed penalty. At zero, the absolute smooth gradient must be bounded by the penalty. The intercept stays unpenalized.

    designruleobjectiveKKT residualpenalized zeros
    orthogonalridge0.8363581.11e-160
    orthogonallasso0.5678252.50e-162
    orthogonalelastic net1.2144921.11e-162
    correlatedridge0.4222806.71e-170
    correlatedlasso0.4078251.39e-163
    correlatedelastic net0.5744921.09e-163

    Conclusion

    The orthogonal solution explains both ridge shrinkage and lasso zeros under the exact loss normalisation used here. The fixed penalty path verifies implementation rather than choosing a model. Correlated predictors and training-only scaling require separate validation and KKT checks.
