    Log-Link GLMs: Means, Likelihoods and Scores

    Abstract

    Positive means can describe integer counts or strictly positive measurements, but one mean function does not determine a response distribution. We compare three log-link likelihoods at hand-checkable observations and verify autograd against their analytic scores. Shape and size are fixed here, keeping mean estimation separate from dispersion estimation.

    The method

    All three families use eta=Xw and mu=exp(eta). Poisson has variance mu; gamma with known shape a has variance mu²/a; negative binomial with known size r has variance mu+mu²/r. Their derivatives of per-observation negative log likelihood with respect to eta are

    \[\partial_\eta\ell_{\rm Pois}=\mu-y,\quad \partial_\eta\ell_{\rm Gamma}=a(1-y/\mu),\quad \partial_\eta\ell_{\rm NB}=\frac{r(\mu-y)}{r+\mu}.\]

    These follow by differentiating the distribution log densities after substituting mu=exp(eta). The coefficient gradient is Xᵀ times these scores. GlmNll retains response normalising constants in the reported likelihood while stopping their response gradients. Gamma requires y>0; count families use nonnegative integer y. Callers supply valid response support and finite predictors. The helper is a log-link likelihood, including for gamma; it differs from stats::GLM's inverse-link gamma family. NB uses a softplus expression for stability. Shape/size are supplied, not estimated.

    Simple examples

    At eta=0 and y=2, mu=1. Scores are -1 for Poisson, -3 for gamma shape 3 and -2/3 for negative-binomial size 2.

    Checking the theory

    15 analytic score probes; maximum discrepancy 0.000000000000.

    Fitting means with a tensor optimizer

    The intercept-only training responses 1,2,3,2 have mean 2. For each fixed dispersion, the score sums to zero at eta=log(2). The optimizer fits eta directly; this analytic answer checks the fit rather than supplying its parameters.

    familyfitted_meananalytic_mean_errormean_NLL
    Poisson201.408219096
    gamma201.378773338
    negative binomial201.703422192

    Held-out log-link fits

    Use the same coefficient vector to generate conditional means for all three families. Fit each likelihood to 2,400 observations. Compare with a constant predictor on 1,000 new observations, using only the training response mean for the constant prediction.

    Conclusion

    Analytic scores agree with differentiation across all three fixed-dispersion likelihoods. A positive mean function does not make the distributions interchangeable. Response support, dispersion choice and held-out likelihood should be assessed before interpreting fitted coefficients.
