    Class weighting, focal loss and resampling for rare events

    Abstract

    Fit four copies of a binary classifier on a 5% positive training set. Compare their precision and recall on a common held-out set at the original threshold of logit zero.

    The positive class shifts two of four Gaussian features. Each run uses the same 12-unit tanh network and starts from the same initialization. The plain fit uses binary cross-entropy. The weighted fit balances the expected contribution of the two classes to that loss. The focal fit reduces each easy example's contribution by the square of its error probability. The resampled fit uses the plain loss on batches drawn with replacement, half from each class.

    The held-out set keeps the original 5% prevalence. Precision and recall describe the shared decision threshold, while average precision describes the ordering over all thresholds. Reweighting or resampling may change the scale of the scores, so the report treats the sigmoid output as a score for classification.

    MethodPrecisionRecallBalanced accuracyAverage precision
    plain0.67740.32810.66000.4868
    weighted0.17820.84380.81950.4834
    focal0.40210.60940.78080.4839
    resampled0.19230.85940.83470.4547

    The weighted and resampled fits detect more positives at logit zero, with lower precision. The plain fit has the highest average precision in this run. These results distinguish the choice of operating threshold from the quality of the ranking.

    Reference

    Lin et al. (2017). Focal Loss for Dense Object Detection.
