    Selective Kernels: Normalized Branch Choice and a Complete SKNet Fit

    Abstract

    Tensor attention modules expose how global context changes channel or branch weights. We check the small module, complete network counts and gradients, then show every update of the source-shaped compact CIFAR classifier with tensor Adam.

    Source example: examples/cpp/source/nn/1_convolutional_neural_networks/1.5_attention_and_modern_convnets/sknet.cpp.

    The method

    A selective-kernel unit has two grouped 3×3/BN/ReLU branches, with dilation one and two. Their SUM is globally averaged and reduced to max(C/r,L) through bias-free 1×1/BN/ReLU. Two bias-free expansions give branch logits; a softmax across the TWO BRANCHES normalizes weights per sample and channel. The output is the weighted branch sum. Independent sigmoids would change that definition. Mathematically positive weights can underflow to zero in floating arithmetic; the small support fixture uses .01 filters to avoid saturation. The dilated branch samples nine taps over 5×5 rather than using a dense 5×5 kernel. Controlled positive inputs/filters expose branch-only derivative supports at strides one and two; complete attention derivatives also flow through pooled selection.

    SKNet-50 uses a ResNeXt 32×4d backbone, replacing each spatial filter with an SK unit, r=16 and L=32. Its full learned count is 27479784, adding 2450880 over ResNeXt-50. Grouped dilated core kernels replace the source release's split/zero-expanded implementation, preserving counts and connectivity. The quarter-width CIFAR network retains cardinality 32, group widths 1/2/4/8, and all sixteen blocks. Its conv2 callback captures normalization options by value; diagnostic state freezing must also freeze that callback's own update_stats setting.

    The backbone retains all sixteen ResNet-50 bottlenecks (3/4/6/3). Each unit ends with a linear 1×1/BN, its attention gate, shortcut addition and ReLU. He-uniform bias-free convolution filters use symmetric padding, and BN learns scale/shift with epsilon 1e-5. A global mean and biased classifier finish the model. In grouped variants, the width is floor(planes*base_width/64)*cardinality. Stage stride belongs either to the first 1×1 or to the spatial filter; grouped layers and replacement hooks receive the actual spatial stride, preventing double downsampling when it belongs to the first filter.

    Full-width count/forward and backward probes below use 64-pixel inputs to bound computation; they retain every block and channel but are not full-resolution ImageNet accuracy evaluations. The backward probe freezes moving updates, shifts BN betas away from inactive ReLUs, and checks every parameter gradient is finite. Its printed norm range does not guarantee every derivative is nonzero for arbitrary data or gates. All numerical model/gradient computations use tensors.

    The explicit fit uses the source's quarter-width CIFAR backbone: a 3×3 stride-one stem without pooling, all sixteen blocks, stage stride on the 3×3 SK branches and original 32×32 images. It shows 8 batch-eight epochs (128 updates, kept short so the report builds quickly) on 128 fit/64 validation/64 official-test rows, tensor Adam(.001), explicit kernel L2=.0001/2, padded crops/flips and paired learned/BN checkpoint selection by validation NLL. All attention parameters participate in the optimizer. This bounded single-seed run differs from the paper's ImageNet schedule and cannot establish attention accuracy superiority.
