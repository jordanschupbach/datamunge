    Transposed Convolution: Adjoint Geometry and Checkerboards

    Abstract

    Upsampling a feature map must place its coarse information on a finer grid. Transposed convolution spreads filter-weighted contributions over that grid. We verify its adjoint identity and show how overlap counts create checkerboards even on constant inputs.

    Source example: examples/cpp/source/nn/1_convolutional_neural_networks/1.1_fundamentals/transposed_convolutions.cpp.

    The method

    For a fixed forward kernel, flatten convolution into y=Mx. Its input derivative is Mᵀg, which defines transposed convolution. The filter layout is the original forward HWIO layout: coarse channels equal its output channels. Specifying the fine output shape fixes stride and padding ambiguity. The adjoint identity is

    \[\langle Mx,g\rangle=\langle x,M^Tg\rangle.\]

    An adjoint is not generally an inverse: downsampling loses information, and MᵀM is usually not identity. At stride s, an all-one kernel of width k covers each interior phase either floor(k/s) or ceil(k/s) times per axis. Counts are uniform when s divides k, but unequal learned weights can still imbalance phases. Nearest-neighbour resize followed by ordinary convolution preserves constants in the interior with a fixed filter. Edges must be analysed separately. Each transposed operation has the same multiply-count order as the corresponding forward map.

    Conclusion

    The fine output agrees with the forward map's adjoint, including input/filter finite differences. Checkerboards are visible on constant inputs, and the interior phase check separates uneven overlap from padding boundaries and learned phase weights.
