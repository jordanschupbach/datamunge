    Stochastic Depth: Survival, Scaling and Visible Optimization

    Abstract

    Stochastic depth randomly removes a residual contribution while preserving its shortcut. We check both scaling conventions, whole-batch and per-row masks, endpoint behavior and exact derivatives, then explicitly compare constant-depth and masked CIFAR-20 fits using tensor Adam.

    Source example: examples/cpp/source/nn/1_convolutional_neural_networks/1.3_residual_and_dense_families/stochastic_depth.cpp.

    The method

    Let b be Bernoulli(p). The original convention uses bF in training and pF in inference; the inverted convention uses bF/p in training and F in inference. These match expected branch outputs for p>0, but their fluctuations differ. At p=0 both training conventions give zero; inverted inference still returns F. Deterministic endpoints avoid division by zero.

    \[y=x+mF(x),\qquad J_y=I+mJ_F,\qquad p_l=1-\frac{l}{L}(1-p_{\rm last}),\quad l=1,\ldots,L.\]

    A whole-batch gate is one scalar shared by every image; per-example gates have shape [N,1,...,1], shared across each image's features. They are nondifferentiable sampled constants. The visible fixture checks binary gates, outputs, input and learned-weight derivatives for both conventions and mask granularities at p=0,.6,1. Independent mask counts are 512 batches or 8192 rows for the interior-probability rate checks; spatial pixels do not count as separate samples. Inference and endpoint calls do not consume random masks.

    The linear schedule has expected active masks L-(1-p_last)(L+1)/2. With L=54 and p_last=.5 this is 40.25, not 40.5: block indices begin at one. This implementation computes EVERY branch before masking and updates its BN moments even when dropped. It demonstrates regularization and does not implement conditional-execution speedup or memory skipping. A caller-owned generator supplies one stream rather than the source's per-block seeds.

    The complete CIFAR 6n+2 classifier uses a 3×3 conv/BN/ReLU stem, three n-unit stages, two bias-free filters per unit, option-A average-pool/zero-pad shortcuts and post-add ReLU. Global mean and a dense classifier finish the model. BN learns scale/shift, eps 1e-5; He-uniform fan-in initialization and this optimizer schedule are adaptations. CIFAR-110 has 1727962 learned/8096 moving scalars and 54 residual blocks; its backward probe retains all blocks/widths at 16-pixel synthetic inputs. CIFAR-20 has 269722/1376 scalars and nine units.

    Two visible fits reset parameter, minibatch, augmentation and mask seeds, comparing final survival one against .5 with whole-batch inverted DropPath. Both retain all CIFAR-20 layers, use 128 fit/64 validation/64 official-test images, ten batch-eight epochs (160 updates, kept short for build time) of Adam(.001), explicit kernel L2=.0001/2 and padded crops/flips. Each selects paired parameters/moving state by validation NLL before testing. Equal seeds alone do not establish a general accuracy advantage; this tiny experiment differs from both the original example's larger fit and the paper schedule.

    Conclusion

    Stochastic masks alter residual contributions and their derivatives according to an explicit scaling convention. Boundary, schedule and topology checks explain those choices; the paired optimizer loop makes the regularization experiment and its limited evidence reproducible.
