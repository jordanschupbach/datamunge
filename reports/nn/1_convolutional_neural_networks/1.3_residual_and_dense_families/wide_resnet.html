    Wide Residual Networks: Width and Branch Dropout

    Abstract

    Wide residual networks trade very deep thin stages for wider preactivated filters. We check where dropout acts and how its derivatives scale, verify full WRN parameter budgets and geometry, then explicitly train three CIFAR configurations with tensor Adam and validation checkpoints.

    Source example: examples/cpp/source/nn/1_convolutional_neural_networks/1.3_residual_and_dense_families/wide_resnet.cpp.

    The method

    A CIFAR WRN of depth 6n+4 uses a fixed 16-channel 3×3 stem and n two-filter units in each of three stages, whose widths are 16k/32k/64k. Stage sides are 32/16/8 for 32×32 inputs. Each branch applies BN/ReLU, 3×3, BN/ReLU, optional inverted elementwise dropout, then 3×3. Equal-width shortcuts carry raw inputs; projections consume the first preactivation. No ReLU follows the sum. Final BN/ReLU, global mean and a dense classifier finish the model.

    \[y=S(x)+W_2*D_p(\operatorname{ReLU}(\operatorname{BN}_2(W_1*a))),\quad a=\operatorname{ReLU}(\operatorname{BN}_1(x)),\qquad D_p(z)=m z/(1-p).\]

    Retained dropout values and derivatives scale by 1/(1-p), and inference bypasses masks. A zero final filter gives exact signed identity and unit input derivatives for matching-width units even with training dropout. This identity fixture does not claim arbitrary trained branches cannot cancel shortcut gradients. The sampled removed fraction is checked on positive values; zero preactivations alone cannot identify a dropped mask. Fresh masks must differ and inference must repeat. A caller-owned generator supplies one stream across the whole model, replacing the source's per-block seeds. BN learns gamma/beta with epsilon 1e-5; eager moving moments are saved separately. He-uniform fan-in replaces the release's normal initialization.

    WRN-16-1/16-2/28-10 have 175066/691674/36479194 learned scalars and 928/1824/17952 moving scalars. Depth labels exclude projection shortcuts. Width growth makes the dominant convolution budget approximately quadratic in k; MAC estimates below count multiply-accumulate pairs for kernels and classifier, excluding BN, ReLU, addition and pooling. They do not measure runtime. Full-size forward checks are separate from complete WRN-16 backward graphs at k=1/2; no blocks are omitted in those derivative checks.

    Three explicit fits compare WRN-16 at (k,p)=(1,0),(2,0),(2,.3). Each resets the parameter, augmentation and minibatch seeds, uses the same 128/64/64 fit/validation/official-test images, average-pooled to 16x16 so that the fits stay short (global pooling makes the network size-agnostic, so the parameter counts are unchanged), 8 epochs of batch-eight Adam(.001), pad-2 random crops with flips, and explicit kernel L2=.0001/2. Validation selects each paired parameter/moment checkpoint before test evaluation. Width changes random tensor shapes, so equal seeds do not produce identical kernels. These tiny single-seed fits illustrate the comparison procedure; they do not establish a width/dropout ranking or reproduce the paper.

    Conclusion

    Widening raises representational and arithmetic budgets, while dropout regularizes only the residual branch. Exact mask and signed-shortcut checks explain that distinction; the visible three-fit loop makes the comparison and its small-data limitations reproducible.
