    Bayesian data analysis examples

    Abstract

    Executable C++ examples of Bayesian modelling in datamunge, from exact conjugate updates through generalized linear models, hierarchical models and marginalization.

    Start here

    • Two samples and k samples derive posterior contrasts for a continuous response grouped by a categorical predictor.
    • Simple regression and multiple regression compare Gaussian posterior coefficient estimates with ordinary least squares.
    • Beta-binomial and Gamma-Poisson derive exact posterior updates and predictive probabilities.
    • Eight schools compares noncentered NUTS and hierarchical Normal quadrature. Hierarchical logistic regression shows partial pooling for binary outcomes.
    • Bayesian additive regression trees fits a nonlinear interaction and compares mean and observation intervals.

    Generalized linear and count models

    • Grouped binomial fits a logit link to counts with known numbers of trials.
    • Poisson and negative binomial fit log means under different variance assumptions.
    • Gamma fits positive continuous outcomes with a log mean.
    • Zero-inflated Poisson and Poisson hurdle model excess zeros in different ways.
    • Categorical links works through binary probit, ordered logit and multinomial logit.
    • Poisson replication uses posterior predictive checks to assess excess zeros and an extreme maximum.

    Stan 2.19 chapter coverage

    The chapter numbers follow Part 1 of the Stan User’s Guide 2.19. Each chapter has a worked datamunge example. Several chapters contain variants beyond the examples linked here.

    1. Regression. Gaussian linear regression, Student-t errors categorical links, hierarchical logistic regression and posterior predictive checking.
    2. Time series. AR(1) and a hidden Markov model.
    3. Missing data. Ignorable missing responses.
    4. Truncated or censored data. Left truncation and right censoring.
    5. Finite mixtures. Gaussian mixtures, zero inflation and hurdles.
    6. Measurement error and meta-analysis. Predictor measurement error and eight schools.
    7. Latent discrete parameters. Poisson change point and mark–recapture.
    8. Sparse and ragged data. Ragged groups.
    9. Clustering. Gaussian mixture assignments.
    10. Gaussian processes. Posterior smoothing.
    11. Directions and rotations. Circular location.
    12. Algebraic equations. Implicit equilibrium.
    13. Ordinary differential equations. Numerical decay model.
    14. One-dimensional integration. Integrated Poisson exposure.

    Data and fitted values

    Eight schools uses published treatment effect estimates and reported standard errors. The conjugate reports generate their own observations with fixed seeds and check the exact updates against the likelihood evaluated on those observations. The other introductory analyses use small synthetic datasets embedded in the programs. Fit panels show posterior expected responses, predictive distributions, or latent quantities alongside the observations. Nested sampling also fits a set of synthetic calibration readings.

    Run the reports

    Use a report toolchain that provides C++23, mep-mepml and the datamunge pkg-config entry. From the repository root, run an individual report with just docs-mepml-run docs/mepml/bayes/glm/poisson.mepml. Each program writes an SVG into its report’s local build/ directory. The runner copies that figure to the path referenced by the report.
