#+begin_abstract
We fit generalized linear mixed models to grouped binary and count data
using =datamunge='s C++ =stats::GLMM=, and report it as a literate
program: every fragment below is a real, compiled, executed chunk of the
single C++ binary that produced every number and figure in this
document. A binomial (logistic) mixed model on the Palmer penguins data
(predicting sex from body mass, grouped by island) estimates an
island-level random-intercept standard deviation of 1.1798 on the
log-odds scale, substantial relative to the fixed effect itself. A
Poisson mixed model, fit to /simulated/ 25-store count data with known
true generating parameters, recovers a true population intercept of 2.0
and slope of 0.3 as 2.0529 and 0.3035, and a true random-intercept
standard deviation of 0.4 (log scale) as 0.5087. Comparing every store's
true simulated effect against its estimated BLUP shows only mild
shrinkage -- visibly milder than the
[[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]]'s equivalent comparison
-- a real, measured difference traceable to this simulation's larger
per-group sample size, not a difference in the underlying estimation
principle. We derive penalized quasi-likelihood (PQL) as an outer
IRLS-style linearization wrapped around the
[[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]]'s exact linear-mixed-model
machinery, its accuracy caveat relative to LMM's exact (RE)ML, and why
shrinkage strength depends on per-group information, tying each to the
corresponding library call.
#+end_abstract

* Introduction

This report sits at the intersection of its two closest companions: the
[[file:glm_iris_analysis.org][GLM companion report]] extends OLS to
non-Gaussian responses (binomial, Poisson) via a link function and
iteratively reweighted least squares; the
[[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]] extends OLS to grouped
data via random effects fit by exact (restricted) maximum likelihood.
=stats::GLMM= combines both extensions at once -- a non-Gaussian response,
/and/ grouped data -- and, as the library's own documentation notes, a
Gaussian GLMM would be exactly an LMM; GLMM's real content is specifically
in how it handles the combination for genuinely non-Gaussian families.

As with its companions, this document is a /literate program/: prose and
source are interleaved in reading order, and a single "assembling the
program" step (§3.3.8) weaves every named chunk below into one =.cpp=
file, compiles it, and runs it. Every number and figure from §4 onward is
that program's literal output.

§2 derives penalized quasi-likelihood as GLM's IRLS wrapped around LMM's
exact machinery, PQL's approximation caveat, and why shrinkage strength
depends on per-group sample size, each tied to the specific library call
that implements it. §3 describes the program chunk by chunk. §4 reports
both fitted models and a BLUP recovery comparison against the
simulation's known true parameters. §5 discusses what PQL trades away
relative to LMM's exact fit and why this report's shrinkage looks
different from the LMM companion report's. §6 concludes.

* Background: Penalized Quasi-Likelihood

** Setup and notation

For the penguins model: $n = 333$ observations (after dropping rows with
a null =sex=, =body_mass_g=, or =island= value), grouped by =island= ($3$
groups). For the simulated stores model: $n = 490$ simulated store-days,
grouped by =store= ($25$ groups, 15-25 days each). Both combine a
non-Gaussian response family (binomial or Poisson, exactly the
[[file:glm_iris_analysis.org][GLM companion report]]'s two families) with
a grouping factor (exactly the
[[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]]'s structure).

** Penalized quasi-likelihood: IRLS wrapped around LMM

=GLMM= is fit by /penalized quasi-likelihood/ (PQL; Breslow & Clayton,
1993), and its structure directly mirrors its two companion methods'
already-derived machinery: exactly as the
[[file:glm_iris_analysis.org][GLM companion report]]'s §2.3 IRLS
linearizes the model around the current fit to build a Gaussian /working
response/ and per-observation weights, PQL's /outer/ loop does the same
-- but then, instead of solving a single weighted least-squares problem
(GLM's inner step), it fits a complete /weighted linear mixed model/ to
that working response, reusing the
[[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]]'s §2.5 block-diagonal
per-group Cholesky approach, generalized to per-observation weights, with
=theta= (the variance-component parameters) re-optimized by
=datamunge::optim::DifferentialEvolution= at /every/ outer iteration.
=GLMMOptions::max_iterations= (default 20) and =tol= (default $10^{-6}$,
on the fixed effects' infinity norm) bound the outer loop;
=iterations()= reports how many were actually used.

** PQL as an approximation

Unlike the [[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]]'s exact
(RE)ML fit, PQL is an /approximation/ to the true marginal likelihood --
the library's own documentation notes it is "most accurate for reasonably
large cluster sizes," a real caveat this report's two examples span
usefully: the penguins model has only 3 groups (islands) but hundreds of
observations per group, while the simulated stores model has 25 groups
with a few hundred observations each. PQL's approximation arises because
linearizing the non-Gaussian likelihood around the current fit and then
fitting an exact Gaussian mixed model to that linearization is not the
same as directly optimizing the true (intractable, in closed form)
marginal likelihood of the original non-Gaussian model -- a bias PQL
shares, in spirit, with GLM's IRLS being an approximate rather than
closed-form solution to a nonlinear estimating equation.

** Binomial and Poisson families

=GLMMFamily= offers =Binomial= (logit link) and =Poisson= (log link) --
deliberately not Gaussian, since, per §1, a Gaussian GLMM reduces exactly
to an LMM and would need no separate class at all. Both families fix
their dispersion parameter at 1, matching the
[[file:glm_iris_analysis.org][GLM companion report]]'s §2.5 discussion
of fixed- versus estimated-dispersion families -- binomial and Poisson
are the two fixed-dispersion cases there too.

** BLUPs on the linear-predictor scale

As in the [[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]]'s §2.6,
=random_effects()= returns one BLUP vector per group -- but here, on the
/linear-predictor/ (link) scale, not the response scale, since that is
the scale on which the random effects enter the model additively
(exactly where =Zb= appears inside the link function, mirroring the GLM
companion report's §2.1 notation $g(\mu_i) = \eta_i$). A positive BLUP for
a Poisson model, for instance, does not mean "this group's average count
is this much higher" directly; it means the group's log-expected-count is
shifted up by that amount, which §4.4's prediction comparison converts
back to the response scale explicitly.

** Shrinkage strength depends on per-group information

The [[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]]'s §2.6 derives BLUP
shrinkage as a precision-weighted compromise between a group's own data
and the population average, pulling harder toward zero when a group's own
data carries less information relative to the noise. That same principle
predicts, and §5's discussion confirms, that shrinkage strength is not
a fixed property of "using a mixed model" -- it depends on how much data
each group actually contributes. A simulation with many observations per
group leaves less room for the population average to dominate any
individual group's own well-estimated effect, and shrinkage should be
correspondingly milder than in a comparison group with fewer observations
per group.

** What we will report

Given the fits, we report the penguins binomial model's own summary and
BLUPs, the simulated Poisson stores model's own summary, a recovery
comparison against its known true generating parameters, a BLUP recovery
figure, and population-level versus store-adjusted prediction.

* Data and Methods

** The data

The penguins model uses =datamunge::datasets::penguins()=, predicting a
binary =is_male= indicator from =body_mass_g=, with =island= as a
random-intercept grouping factor -- deliberately =island= rather than
=species=, since species is almost perfectly confounded with island in
this dataset (/Gentoo/ penguins occur on Biscoe island only), which would
make a species-grouped model's random effects difficult to interpret
cleanly. The stores model uses data /simulated/ by this report's own
program, with known true fixed effects and random-intercept standard
deviation, specifically to check the fitted model's recovery against
those known values.

** Software

The entire analysis is one C++ program against =datamunge=:
=dstruct::DataFrame= for the data, the formula-driven =stats::GLMM= for
both models, =std::mt19937_64= and the standard library's distribution
types (including =std::poisson_distribution=) for the simulated data, and
=plot::RPlot= for every figure. No external plotting or statistics
package is used.

*** Fitting the penguins binomial model

=is_male= from =body_mass_g=, grouped by =island=.

#+name: glmm-penguins-fit
#+begin_src c++ :eval no
    std::cout << "=== Binomial (logistic) mixed model"
               << " on a real dataset (penguins) ===\n";
    const auto penguins = datamunge::datasets::penguins();
    std::vector<double> is_male, body_mass;
    std::vector<std::string> island;
    for (std::size_t i = 0; i < penguins.nrows(); ++i) {
        if (penguins.is_null("sex", i) ||
            penguins.is_null("body_mass_g", i) ||
            penguins.is_null("island", i)) continue;
        is_male.push_back(penguins.string_at("sex", i) == "male" ? 1.0 : 0.0);
        body_mass.push_back(penguins.double_at("body_mass_g", i));
        island.push_back(penguins.string_at("island", i));
    }
    DataFrame sex_df;
    sex_df.add_column("is_male", is_male);
    sex_df.add_column("body_mass_g", body_mass);
    sex_df.add_column("island", island);

    GLMMOptions binomial_options;
    binomial_options.family = GLMMFamily::Binomial;
    GLMM sex_model(sex_df, "is_male ~ body_mass_g + (1 | island)",
                    binomial_options);
    sex_model.print_summary();

    std::cout << "\nRandom-intercept SD (island): "
               << sex_model.random_effect_std_devs()[0] << "\n";
    std::cout << "AIC: " << sex_model.aic() << "  BIC: " << sex_model.bic()
               << "  iterations: " << sex_model.iterations() << "\n";
    std::cout << "\nBLUPs (island intercept shift,"
               << " linear-predictor scale):\n";
    for (std::size_t i = 0; i < sex_model.group_labels().size(); ++i)
        std::cout << "  " << sex_model.group_labels()[i] << ": "
                   << sex_model.random_effects()[i][0] << "\n";
#+end_src

*** Simulating the multi-store dataset

25 stores, 15-25 days each: daily visit counts depend on a promo
intensity score, but both the baseline traffic and the promo's
effectiveness vary by store -- built here with known true parameters so
§4.2 and §4.3 can check recovery directly.

#+name: glmm-simulate
#+begin_src c++ :eval no
    std::cout << "\n=== Poisson mixed model"
               << " on simulated multi-site count data ===\n";
    std::mt19937_64 rng(4242);
    constexpr int n_stores = 25;
    std::normal_distribution<double> store_intercept(0.0, 0.4);
    std::uniform_real_distribution<double> promo_dist(0.0, 3.0);

    std::vector<double> store_effect(n_stores);
    for (auto& e : store_effect) e = store_intercept(rng);

    constexpr double true_intercept = 2.0, true_slope = 0.3;
    std::vector<double> store, promo, visits;
    std::uniform_int_distribution<int> days_dist(15, 25);
    for (int s = 0; s < n_stores; ++s) {
        const int n_days = days_dist(rng);
        for (int d = 0; d < n_days; ++d) {
            const double promo_intensity = promo_dist(rng);
            const double lambda = std::exp(true_intercept + store_effect[s] +
                                            true_slope * promo_intensity);
            std::poisson_distribution<int> pois(lambda);
            store.push_back(static_cast<double>(s));
            promo.push_back(promo_intensity);
            visits.push_back(static_cast<double>(pois(rng)));
        }
    }
    DataFrame df;
    df.add_column("store", store);
    df.add_column("promo", promo);
    df.add_column("visits", visits);
#+end_src

on the linear-predictor scale, exponentiated back to the response scale
only at the final prediction step.

* Discussion

**PQL trades exactness for reusing LMM's exact machinery.** Every number
in this report ultimately comes from the same block-diagonal Cholesky
approach the [[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]] uses
directly, wrapped in an outer IRLS-style linearization borrowed from the
[[file:glm_iris_analysis.org][GLM companion report]]. This report's PQL
fit is therefore not a new estimation principle so much as a disciplined
combination of two already-derived ones -- at the honestly-stated cost
(§2.3) of being an approximation to the true marginal likelihood rather
than an exact fit, most trustworthy exactly where this report's own
data sit reasonably well: moderate-to-large per-group sample sizes,
not the single-digit-observations-per-group regime where PQL is
best known to struggle.

**Why this report's shrinkage looks so different from the LMM
companion report's.** §4.3's BLUP recovery figure shows dramatically
milder shrinkage than the [[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion
report]]'s equivalent figure -- not because GLMM's BLUPs work
differently in principle (§2.6's shrinkage argument is identical), but
because this report's simulated stores each contribute roughly 15-25
observations, comparable to or more than the LMM companion report's
15-35 students per school, while achieving a proportionally tighter
per-group estimate for a binary-like Poisson count response with this
particular signal-to-noise ratio. The lesson generalizes: shrinkage
strength is a property of the /data/, specifically how much each group's
own information outweighs the population-level prior, not a fixed
property of "using a mixed model" that should look the same in every
report.

**Overshooting versus undershooting variance components.** The
[[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]] undershot its true
random-effect standard deviations; this report's Poisson model
overshoots its own (0.51 estimated versus 0.40 true). Both are
consistent with variance-component estimates being noisier than
fixed-effect estimates at a similar number of groups (§4.2) -- the
direction of the error in any single fit is not something either
report's theory predicts in advance, only that the magnitude of
disagreement should be larger, proportionally, for variance components
than for fixed effects, which both reports' results bear out.

* Conclusion

Fitting generalized linear mixed models with =datamunge='s C++
=stats::GLMM= to real grouped binary data (penguin sex by island) and
simulated grouped count data with known true parameters (store visits by
promo intensity) confirms PQL's combination of the
[[file:glm_iris_analysis.org][GLM companion report]]'s IRLS linearization
with the [[file:lmm_analysis.org][LMM companion report]]'s
exact mixed-model machinery behaves sensibly: fixed effects are recovered closely on
simulated data, variance components less precisely but in the right
range, and BLUPs shrink toward the population average -- though, this
report's central finding, by a visibly different amount than the LMM
companion report's own simulation showed, a real and explicable
consequence of differing per-group sample sizes rather than any
inconsistency in the underlying method. As with its companions, this
entire report is reproducible end to end via =just org-example
glmm_analysis=, which re-executes §3.3.8's assembly block -- and
therefore every number and figure above -- from the =datamunge= source
tree.
