#+title: Karger's Randomized Minimum Cut
#+options: toc:t num:t
#+property: header-args:c++ :eval no

#+begin_abstract
Karger's algorithm repeatedly contracts uniformly random edges until two
supervertices remain.  We derive its success probability, give pseudocode, and
find a bridge cut with =karger_min_cut=.
#+end_abstract

* Mathematics and algorithm

If the minimum cut has size \(k\), a graph with \(r\) remaining vertices has
at least \(rk/2\) edges, so contraction avoids that cut with probability at
least \(1-2/r\).  Multiplication from \(n\) down to 2 gives success probability
at least \(2/(n(n-1))\); independent trials amplify confidence.

#+begin_example
repeat trials:
  start one disjoint-set component per vertex
  while more than two components:
    choose a uniformly random crossing edge and contract it
  count original edges crossing the two remaining sides
return the smallest cut and its sides
#+end_example

* C++ example
