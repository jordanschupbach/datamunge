#+begin_abstract
We fit \(t\)-distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding (van der Maaten &
Hinton, 2008) to Fisher's (1936) iris measurements using =datamunge='s
C++ =stats::TSNE=, and report it as a literate program: every fragment
below is a real, compiled, executed chunk of the single C++ binary that
produced every number and figure in this document. =Species= is
withheld entirely from the fit. The per-point binary search that
calibrates each point's Gaussian bandwidth to a target perplexity of
30.0 converges tightly -- achieved perplexity ranges from 29.9998 to
30.0002 across all 150 points, mean 30.0000. The resulting 2-dimensional
embedding separates the three species dramatically: mean within-species
embedded distance is 3.2820 against a mean between-species distance of
46.4803, a ratio of 14.16, and the scatter itself resolves into three
tight, visually disjoint blobs even though =Species= is never given to
the fit. Refitting at perplexity 5 and 50 shows the well-documented
sensitivity concretely: perplexity 5 fragments each species into several
small sub-clusters (each achieving perplexity 5.0000 almost exactly),
while perplexity 50 spreads and partially blends /versicolor/ and
/virginica/ back together, both while still calibrating essentially
perfectly (49.9997-50.0003). On a synthetic Swiss roll (\(n=400\),
seeded per the shared batch formula), \(t\)-SNE does /not/ unroll the
manifold into a smooth rectangle -- consistent with its design goal of
preserving local neighborhoods rather than global geometry, it instead
shatters the roll into roughly half a dozen disjoint, locally-coherent
fragments scattered with no consistent global arrangement. Quantitatively
this is a genuinely mixed result: \(t\)-SNE's best-axis correlation with
the true \(t\) parametrization (0.4105) is actually /higher/ than plain
PCA's or classical MDS's (0.2426 each, identical to four decimal places,
as expected since Euclidean MDS and unscaled PCA are close cousins), even
though \(t\)-SNE's picture looks far less like the true manifold than
PCA/MDS's recognizable (if self-intersecting) spiral projection -- a
concrete illustration that "looks unrolled" and "correlates linearly
with ground truth" are not the same question. We derive the full
pipeline -- perplexity-calibrated conditional similarities, symmetrization,
the Student-\(t\) low-dimensional kernel and the crowding problem it
solves, the KL-divergence gradient, and the early-exaggeration/momentum
schedule -- tying each step to the exact formula =stats::TSNE= computes.

* Introduction

Every other unsupervised report in this project -- the [[file:pca_iris_analysis.org][companion PCA
report]] and the [[file:mds_iris_analysis.org][companion MDS report]] chief among them -- asks a
/geometry-preserving/ question: find a low-dimensional coordinate system
whose distances (PCA: variance-maximizing directions; MDS: pairwise
Euclidean distances) reproduce the high-dimensional data's global
structure as faithfully as possible. \(t\)-SNE asks a different kind of
question entirely: forget reproducing distances faithfully everywhere,
and instead find a low-dimensional layout in which points that were
/close neighbors/ in the original space remain close neighbors, at the
cost of systematically distorting (or simply not caring about) distances
between points that were already far apart. This makes it, by design, a
visualization tool rather than a distance- or variance-preserving
embedding, and the trade-off has real, measurable consequences this
report demonstrates directly: dramatically better class separation on
iris than PCA or MDS produce, and a genuinely different (and, on one
concrete metric, worse-looking) picture on a manifold -- the Swiss roll
-- whose whole structure is about preserving smooth global geometry, the
opposite of what \(t\)-SNE optimizes for.

As with its companion reports, this document is a /literate program/:
prose and source are interleaved in reading order, and a single
"assembling the program" step (§3.3.13) weaves every named chunk below
into one =.cpp= file, compiles it, and runs it. Every number and figure
from §4 onward is that program's literal output.

§2 derives the full \(t\)-SNE pipeline -- perplexity-calibrated
conditional similarities and the binary search that finds them,
symmetrization into a joint distribution, the Student-\(t\)
low-dimensional kernel and the "crowding problem" it exists to solve, the
Kullback-Leibler objective and its gradient, and the
early-exaggeration/momentum optimization schedule -- each tied to the specific
=stats::TSNE= computation that implements it. §3 describes the program
chunk by chunk. §4 reports the fitted iris embedding, its
achieved-perplexity convergence, the within-/between-species separation,

does not buy over PCA/MDS. §6 concludes.

* Background: \(t\)-Distributed Stochastic Neighbor Embedding

** Setup and notation

\(n\) observations \(x_1, \ldots, x_n \in \mathbb{R}^p\) -- for iris,
\(n=150\), \(p=4\) (=Sepal.Length=, =Sepal.Width=, =Petal.Length=,
=Petal.Width=); for the Swiss-roll experiment in §4.5-4.7, \(n=400\),
\(p=3\) (the observed 3D coordinates =x=, =y=, =z=, with the true 1D
manifold position \(t\) and the true "height" coordinate withheld). As
in every companion report, no response/label column is ever passed to
the =TSNE= constructor; it is retained separately for coloring figures
and computing separation statistics afterward. =stats::TSNE= maps each
\(x_i\) to a point \(y_i \in \mathbb{R}^k\) in a low-dimensional space
(=TSNEOptions::n_components=, \(k=2\) throughout this report), chosen so
that a probability distribution over neighbor pairs in the /embedded/
space matches, as closely as possible, the corresponding distribution
computed in the /original/ space.

** Conditional similarities and perplexity calibration

For each point \(i\), =TSNE::fit= first computes squared pairwise
distances \(D^2_{ij}\) (Euclidean by default, =DistanceMetric::Manhattan=
also supported) to every other point, then converts them into a
conditional probability that \(i\) would pick \(j\) as its neighbor,

\[
p_{j \mid i} = \frac{\exp(-\beta_i D^2_{ij})}{\sum_{k \neq i} \exp(-\beta_i D^2_{ik})}, \qquad p_{i \mid i} = 0,
\]

a Gaussian kernel centered on \(x_i\) with precision (inverse variance)
\(\beta_i = 1/(2\sigma_i^2)\) -- the exact quantity the implementation's
binary search solves for, one \(\beta_i\) per point, so that nearby
points in dense regions get a narrower Gaussian (small \(\sigma_i\)) and
points in sparse regions get a wider one, equalizing the /effective/
number of neighbors each point sees regardless of local density. That
effective count is formalized as /perplexity/: the Shannon entropy (in
bits) of the conditional distribution \(P_i = (p_{1|i}, \ldots,
p_{n|i})\),

\[
H(P_i) = -\sum_{j \neq i} p_{j \mid i} \log_2 p_{j \mid i}, \qquad
\mathrm{Perp}(P_i) = 2^{H(P_i)} .
\]

A distribution concentrated on a single neighbor has \(H=0\) bits and
perplexity 1; a distribution spread uniformly over \(m\) neighbors has
\(H = \log_2 m\) bits and perplexity exactly \(m\) -- so perplexity is
literally interpretable as "the effective number of neighbors" \(i\)
attends to, smoothly interpolating between integer neighbor counts for
non-uniform distributions. =TSNEOptions::perplexity= (default 30.0) is
the /target/; for each point =TSNE::fit= runs a binary search over
\(\beta_i\) (implemented directly on \(\log_2\)-perplexity, i.e. entropy,
since entropy is monotonic in \(\beta_i\) while perplexity is its
exponential): start at \(\beta_i = 1\), and after each trial compute
\(H(P_i)\) via the formula above; if it exceeds the target
\(\log_2(\text{perplexity})\) (distribution too spread out) increase
\(\beta_i\) (narrow the Gaussian), otherwise decrease it, halving the
search interval each of up to 50 steps or stopping early once
\(|H(P_i) - \log_2(\text{perplexity})| < 10^{-5}\). §4.2 and §4.6 report
=achieved_perplexity()= -- \(2^{H(P_i)}\) at the search's stopping point
for every point -- as the direct empirical check that this calibration
actually converged.

** Symmetrization into the joint distribution \(P\)

\(p_{j|i}\) is not symmetric in general (\(i\)'s bandwidth \(\beta_i\)
differs from \(j\)'s), so =TSNE::fit= symmetrizes the two conditional
distributions into one joint distribution over unordered pairs,

\[
P_{ij} = \frac{p_{j \mid i} + p_{i \mid j}}{2n} ,
\]

dividing by \(2n\) (rather than just averaging) so that
\(\sum_{i \neq j} P_{ij} = 1\) and every point contributes a reasonable
amount of probability mass to the objective even if it is an outlier
under one of the two conditional distributions alone.

** The Student-\(t\) low-dimensional kernel and the crowding problem

The low-dimensional similarities \(q_{ij}\) are /not/ computed with the
same Gaussian form as \(p_{j|i}\) -- =TSNE::fit= instead uses a Student-\(t\)
distribution with one degree of freedom (equivalently, a Cauchy
distribution),

\[
q_{ij} = \frac{(1 + \lVert y_i - y_j \rVert^2)^{-1}}{\sum_{k \neq l} (1 + \lVert y_k - y_l \rVert^2)^{-1}} .
\]

The reason is what van der Maaten & Hinton (2008) call the /crowding
problem/: in a high-dimensional space there is enough volume for a point
to have many /moderately/ dissimilar neighbors simultaneously, all at
roughly comparable distance; a 2-dimensional map simply does not have
enough area to place that many points at moderate distance from a common
center without either overlapping them or pushing the genuinely close
neighbors unrealistically far apart to make room. A Gaussian
low-dimensional kernel, whose tails decay as \(\exp(-d^2)\), would
therefore be forced into a bad compromise: to keep near neighbors
looking near, it would have to also keep moderately-dissimilar points
crowded uncomfortably close, since its probability mass falls off too
fast to distinguish "moderately far" from "very far." The Student-\(t\)
kernel's heavy tail, falling off only as \(d^{-2}\), lets =TSNE::fit=
assign moderately-dissimilar points a low \(q_{ij}\) at a much /larger/
embedded distance than a Gaussian would require -- points that are
moderately far apart can genuinely spread out in the map, relieving the
crowding, while pairs with large \(p_{ij}\) (genuine near neighbors) are
still pulled tightly together because the kernel's peak near \(d=0\) is
even sharper than a Gaussian's.

** The Kullback-Leibler objective and its gradient

=TSNE::fit= optimizes the embedding \(Y = \{y_i\}\) to minimize the
Kullback-Leibler divergence of \(Q\) (the low-dimensional joint
distribution) from \(P\) (the high-dimensional joint distribution, held
fixed once computed in §2.3),

\[
C(Y) = \mathrm{KL}(P \parallel Q) = \sum_{i \neq j} P_{ij} \log \frac{P_{ij}}{q_{ij}} .
\]

Differentiating with respect to a single embedded point \(y_i\) (van der
Maaten & Hinton, 2008, eq. 5) gives a gradient with a clean physical
reading -- a sum of pairwise "spring forces" along each \(y_i - y_j\)
direction, attractive when \(P_{ij} > q_{ij}\) and repulsive when the
reverse holds:

\[
\frac{\partial C}{\partial y_i} = 4 \sum_{j \neq i} (P_{ij} - q_{ij}) \, (1 + \lVert y_i - y_j \rVert^2)^{-1} (y_i - y_j) .
\]

This is exactly what the implementation's innermost loop computes: with
=num(i,j)= holding the unnormalized Student-\(t\) affinity
\((1+\lVert y_i - y_j \rVert^2)^{-1}\) and =q_ij= its normalized form,
each pair contributes =coeff = 4.0 * (p_ij - q_ij) * num(i,j)= to
=grad(i,c) += coeff * (Y(i,c) - Y(j,c))= for every embedded coordinate
\(c\) -- the code is a direct, unsimplified transcription of the formula
above (using \(q_{ij} \cdot Z = \) =num(i,j)=, where \(Z\) is the
normalizer, to avoid recomputing the Student-\(t\) numerator twice).

** Early exaggeration and the momentum schedule

Two optimization devices, both tied directly to =TSNEOptions= fields,
make the gradient descent above converge to well-separated clusters
rather than a locally-optimal tangle:

/Early exaggeration/ (=TSNEOptions::early_exaggeration=, default 12.0,
applied for the first =TSNEOptions::early_exaggeration_iterations=
iterations, default 250) multiplies every \(P_{ij}\) by 12 before
computing the gradient, so \(P\) itself is not the objective's true
target during this phase -- the optimizer instead pursues an artificially
peaked distribution. Since the low-dimensional Student-\(t\) kernel
cannot represent a target that concentrated without pulling genuinely
similar points very tightly together and pushing dissimilar ones
correspondingly far apart, this phase forces well-separated clusters to
form early, before the algorithm spends its remaining iterations
refining fine-grained structure /within/ those clusters -- without it,
points can settle into a diffuse, moderately-separated configuration
that is difficult to untangle later. /Momentum/ (=initial_momentum= 0.5
before =momentum_switch_iteration= 250, =final_momentum= 0.8 after) adds
a fraction of the previous step's update to the current one,
\(\Delta y_i^{(t)} = \mu \, \Delta y_i^{(t-1)} - \eta \, \partial C /
\partial y_i\), the standard classical-momentum device for accelerating
gradient descent through shallow regions and damping oscillation; the
switch from low to high momentum is timed to coincide with the end of
early exaggeration (both default to iteration 250), matching the
intuition that once clusters have formed, larger, more confident steps
are safe. =TSNE::fit= re-centers each embedded coordinate to zero mean
after every iteration -- a numerical bookkeeping detail (the objective
is translation-invariant) rather than part of the derivation above.

#+name: tsne-separation
#+begin_src c++ :eval no
    std::cout << "\n=== Within- vs. between-species embedded"
               << " distance ===\n";
    {
        auto avg_dist = [&](const bool within) {
            double sum = 0.0;
            std::size_t cnt = 0;
            for (std::size_t i = 0; i < tsne.observations(); ++i) {
                for (std::size_t j = i + 1; j < tsne.observations(); ++j) {
                    const bool same =
                        species[tsne.kept_row_indices()[i]] ==
                        species[tsne.kept_row_indices()[j]];
                    if (same != within) continue;
                    double d2 = 0.0;
                    for (std::size_t c = 0; c < tsne.n_components(); ++c) {
                        const double d =
                            tsne.embedding()(i, c) - tsne.embedding()(j, c);
                        d2 += d * d;
                    }
                    sum += std::sqrt(d2);
                    ++cnt;
                }
            }
            return sum / static_cast<double>(cnt);
        };
        const double within = avg_dist(true);
        const double between = avg_dist(false);
        std::cout << "Within-species mean distance:  " << within << "\n";
        std::cout << "Between-species mean distance: " << between << "\n";
        std::cout << "Ratio (between / within):      "
                   << between / within << "\n";
    }

#+name: tsne-swissroll-data
#+begin_src c++ :eval no
    std::cout << "\n=== Swiss roll: construction ===\n";
    const std::size_t n = 400;
    std::mt19937_64 rng(42);
    std::uniform_real_distribution<double> u(0.0, 1.0);
    std::vector<double> t_true(n), height(n), x(n), y(n), z(n);
    for (std::size_t i = 0; i < n; ++i) {
        t_true[i] = 1.5 * M_PI * (1.0 + 2.0 * u(rng));
        height[i] = 21.0 * u(rng);
        x[i] = t_true[i] * std::cos(t_true[i]);
        y[i] = height[i];
        z[i] = t_true[i] * std::sin(t_true[i]);
    }
    DataFrame roll;
    roll.add_column("x", x);
    roll.add_column("y", y);
    roll.add_column("z", z);
    const std::vector<std::string> roll_features{"x", "y", "z"};
    std::cout << "n = " << n << " points generated\n";

    constexpr int kBins = 6;
    const std::vector<RGB> bin_colors = {
        {37, 99, 235}, {8, 145, 178}, {22, 163, 74},
        {217, 119, 6}, {220, 38, 38}, {124, 58, 237}};
    const double tmin = *std::min_element(t_true.begin(), t_true.end());
    const double tmax = *std::max_element(t_true.begin(), t_true.end());
    auto t_bin = [&](const double tv) {
        int bin = static_cast<int>((tv - tmin) / (tmax - tmin + 1e-12) *
                                    kBins);
        return std::min(bin, kBins - 1);
    };

*** Recovering the true parametrization

Pearson correlation between each embedded dimension and the withheld
true \((t, \text{height})\) pair, allowing for the sign flips and
axis swaps every one of these methods is invariant to (an eigenvector or
a converged embedding is only ever recovered up to such symmetries).

#+name: tsne-swissroll-correlation
#+begin_src c++ :eval no
    auto pearson = [](const std::vector<double>& a,
                       const std::vector<double>& b) {
        const std::size_t m = a.size();
        double ma = 0.0, mb = 0.0;
        for (std::size_t i = 0; i < m; ++i) {
            ma += a[i];
            mb += b[i];
        }
        ma /= static_cast<double>(m);
        mb /= static_cast<double>(m);
        double num = 0.0, na = 0.0, nb = 0.0;
        for (std::size_t i = 0; i < m; ++i) {
            const double da = a[i] - ma, db = b[i] - mb;
            num += da * db;
            na += da * da;
            nb += db * db;
        }
        return num / std::sqrt(na * nb);
    };

    std::cout << "\n=== Recovery of the true (t, height) parametrization"
               << " ===\n";
    std::cout << std::setw(8) << "method" << std::setw(12) << "dim0_vs_t"
               << std::setw(14) << "dim0_vs_hgt" << std::setw(12)
               << "dim1_vs_t" << std::setw(14) << "dim1_vs_hgt"
               << std::setw(12) << "best_t" << std::setw(12)
               << "best_hgt\n";
    auto report_corr = [&](const std::string& name,
                            const datamunge::linalg::DenseMatrix<double>& emb) {
        std::vector<double> d0(n), d1(n);
        for (std::size_t i = 0; i < n; ++i) {
            d0[i] = emb(i, 0);
            d1[i] = emb(i, 1);
        }
        const double c_t0 = pearson(d0, t_true), c_h0 = pearson(d0, height);
        const double c_t1 = pearson(d1, t_true), c_h1 = pearson(d1, height);
        const double best_t = std::max(std::abs(c_t0), std::abs(c_t1));
        const double best_h = std::max(std::abs(c_h0), std::abs(c_h1));
        std::cout << std::setw(8) << name << std::setw(12) << c_t0
                   << std::setw(14) << c_h0 << std::setw(12) << c_t1
                   << std::setw(14) << c_h1 << std::setw(12) << best_t
                   << std::setw(12) << best_h << "\n";
    };
    report_corr("t-SNE", tsne_roll.embedding());
    report_corr("PCA", pca_roll.scores());
    report_corr("MDS", mds_roll.embedding());

** Species separation

#+begin_example
Within-species mean distance:  3.2820
Between-species mean distance: 46.4803
Ratio (between / within):      14.1623
#+end_example

A between-to-within distance ratio of 14.16 is a genuinely dramatic
number, not an exaggerated one -- the average pair of flowers from
/different/ species ends up over fourteen times farther apart in the
2-dimensional embedding than the average pair from the /same/ species.
This is \(t\)-SNE's signature real-world behavior: because the objective
in §2.5 only rewards preserving /local/ neighbor relationships (a point's
handful of nearest neighbors in feature space, weighted through the
perplexity-calibrated \(P_{ij}\)) and pays no direct cost for how far
apart already-dissimilar points end up, points from different,
well-separated classes are free to be pushed arbitrarily far apart by
the repulsive term in §2.5's gradient once their local neighborhoods no
longer overlap -- a much stronger effect than PCA or MDS's global
distance-preservation ever produces on this same data.

** Perplexity sensitivity

#+begin_example
  perplexity   achieved_mean  achieved_min achieved_max
      5.0000          5.0000        5.0000        5.0000
     30.0000         30.0000       29.9998       30.0002
     50.0000         50.0000       49.9997       50.0003
#+end_example

Calibration is essentially perfect at all three target perplexities --
the binary search in §2.2 does not get harder or less accurate at the
extremes tested here. What changes is the /embedding/, not the fit
quality:

#+ATTR_HTML: :alt Scatter of t-SNE embedding at perplexity 5 colored by true species :width 100%
[[file:../../build/org/tsne_iris_perplexity_5.svg]]

At perplexity 5 -- each point's Gaussian bandwidth calibrated to an
effective neighborhood of only about 5 other points -- every species
fragments into several small, tight sub-clusters rather than one blob
per species: with such a narrow neighborhood, points that are close but
not each other's very nearest neighbors are no longer guaranteed to end
up in the same fragment, so the embedding reveals more fine-grained
local sub-structure within each species at the cost of a messier overall
picture.

#+ATTR_HTML: :alt Scatter of t-SNE embedding at perplexity 50 colored by true species :width 100%
[[file:../../build/org/tsne_iris_perplexity_50.svg]]

At perplexity 50 -- roughly a third of the entire dataset treated as
each point's effective neighborhood -- /versicolor/ and /virginica/
partially blend back together along a shared boundary (mirroring §4.1's
true-species figure, where these same two species overlap in the raw
feature space), and a handful of points end up visibly misplaced (e.g. a
few /setosa/ points pulled down near the other two species). Larger
perplexity values push \(t\)-SNE's notion of "local" outward until it
approaches global structure, at which point it necessarily converges
toward something closer to what a global method like PCA or MDS would
show, overlap included.

#+ATTR_HTML: :alt t-SNE embedding of the Swiss roll colored by binned t :width 100%

This is the headline qualitative result: \(t\)-SNE does /not/ unroll the
manifold into anything resembling a smooth rectangle. Instead it shatters
the roll into roughly half a dozen small, elongated, internally-coherent
fragments (each individual fragment shows a clean color gradient,
meaning /local/ \(t\)-order is preserved within it) scattered around the
embedding with no consistent global arrangement -- bin colors that are
adjacent along the true manifold (e.g. bin 4/orange and bin 5/red) land
in entirely different regions of the plot in some fragments and directly
next to each other in others. This is exactly the qualitative failure
mode the companion [[file:isomap_iris_analysis.org][Isomap report]] and [[file:lle_iris_analysis.org][LLE report]] describe
avoiding for methods purpose-built to preserve global manifold geometry:
\(t\)-SNE was never optimizing for a smooth global unrolling in the first
place (§2.4-2.5), so this is confirmation of its design trade-off rather
than a bug.

PCA (and, visually indistinguishably, classical Euclidean MDS -- see
§4.7 and the [[file:mds_iris_analysis.org][MDS companion report]]'s own derivation of why the two

failure: a recognizable, continuous spiral -- the roll viewed edge-on --
rather than \(t\)-SNE's disconnected fragments. It is a smoother, more
interpretable picture than \(t\)-SNE's, but it is still not an unrolling:
the true \(t\)-order runs /around/ the spiral, not along a single
straight axis, so no single embedded dimension tracks \(t\) monotonically
either. §4.7 quantifies both pictures on the same footing.

** Recovery of the true parametrization

#+begin_example
  method   dim0_vs_t   dim0_vs_hgt   dim1_vs_t   dim1_vs_hgt      best_t   best_hgt
   t-SNE      0.4105        0.0673     -0.2218       -0.1454      0.4105      0.1454
     PCA      0.2426        0.0864      0.0254        0.0022      0.2426      0.0864
     MDS      0.2426        0.0864     -0.0254       -0.0022      0.2426      0.0864
#+end_example

A genuinely mixed, and worth-reporting-honestly, result. On this single
linear-correlation metric, \(t\)-SNE's best embedded axis (0.4105
against \(t\)) correlates with the true manifold parametrization
/more/ strongly than either PCA's or classical MDS's best axis (0.2426
each -- identical to four decimal places, precisely the
Euclidean-MDS/PCA equivalence the [[file:mds_iris_analysis.org][MDS companion report]] derives and
verifies on iris). None of the three methods recovers =height= well (best
correlations of 0.1454, 0.0864, and 0.0864 respectively) -- unsurprising
given =height= is the coordinate every one of these methods has the
least structural reason to isolate on its own embedded axes. The
apparent tension with §4.6's qualitative figures -- \(t\)-SNE's picture
"looks" far more broken than PCA's recognizable spiral, yet scores higher
numerically -- resolves once the two figures are read carefully together:
PCA's global spiral, while visually coherent, wraps \(t\) /around/ a
closed curve, so no straight line through the 2D plane tracks \(t\)
monotonically over its full range (correlation is fundamentally a
linear-trend statistic, and a spiral has none along either axis alone);
\(t\)-SNE's fragments, though scattered with no consistent global
arrangement, are individually elongated /along/ locally-monotonic
stretches of \(t\), and by chance enough of that local ordering survives
in aggregate to produce a stronger net linear trend along one embedded
axis than PCA's globally coherent but circular one does. Neither number
should be read as "\(t\)-SNE recovered the manifold better than PCA" in
any structural sense -- §4.6's figures make clear it did not -- only that
a single linear-correlation summary statistic can be misleading exactly
when the true relationship (a spiral, or a shattered set of locally
monotonic fragments) is not itself linear.

* Discussion

**A genuinely different objective, not a worse one.** §4.2-4.4's iris
results and §4.6-4.7's Swiss-roll results are, read together, a direct
empirical demonstration that \(t\)-SNE is solving a different problem
than PCA or MDS, not a strictly better or worse version of the same
problem. On iris -- a dataset whose classes are already reasonably
separable in the raw feature space -- optimizing purely for local
neighborhood preservation (§2.5) produces a dramatically cleaner
visualization (14.16x separation ratio, §4.3) than either global method
achieves. On the Swiss roll -- a dataset whose entire interesting
structure /is/ its global, smoothly-varying geometry -- the same
local-only objective actively destroys the thing a reader would want to
see (§4.6), even while, by one narrow numeric measure, doing no worse
than PCA/MDS (§4.7).

**Perplexity is a real, consequential hyperparameter, not a
technicality.** §4.4's three embeddings of the /same/ 150 flowers, /same/
random seed, differing only in the target neighborhood size, produce
visibly different pictures -- more fragmented sub-structure at
perplexity 5, cleaner separation at 30, partial re-blending at 50 -- a
concrete, first-hand version of the well-documented (van der Maaten &
Hinton, 2008) sensitivity, not merely an assertion repeated from the
literature.

**A correlation number alone can mislead about "did this unroll the
manifold."** §4.7's finding that \(t\)-SNE's best-axis correlation with
\(t\) numerically exceeds PCA's/MDS's, despite \(t\)-SNE's embedding
looking obviously more broken in §4.6's figure, is this report's most
important methodological caution: whenever the true underlying
relationship is nonlinear (a spiral, or a shattered set of locally-linear
fragments), a single Pearson correlation is not a substitute for looking
at the actual picture. The [[file:isomap_iris_analysis.org][Isomap]] and [[file:lle_iris_analysis.org][LLE]] companion reports,
purpose-built to preserve exactly the geodesic/local-linear structure the
Swiss roll needs, are the fairer comparison for "did a manifold-learning
method recover this dataset's true structure" -- this report's honest
answer for \(t\)-SNE specifically is no, not in the way those methods
are designed to.

* Conclusion

Fitting \(t\)-SNE with =datamunge='s C++ =stats::TSNE= to Fisher's iris
measurements -- with =Species= withheld throughout -- shows near-perfect
perplexity calibration (achieved perplexity within 0.0002 of the 30.0
target for every point) and a dramatic, quantified separation between
species (a 14.16x between-to-within embedded distance ratio) that
visibly exceeds what either companion PCA or MDS report produces on the
same data, exactly matching \(t\)-SNE's design goal of preserving local
neighborhood structure for visualization. A perplexity sweep (5, 30, 50)
confirms this behavior is a real, tunable trade-off rather than a fixed
property of the method. On a synthetic Swiss roll built to this report
batch's shared formula, \(t\)-SNE honestly fails to unroll the manifold
into a smooth rectangle -- shattering it into locally-coherent but
globally scattered fragments -- while still, on one narrow
linear-correlation metric, scoring numerically better than PCA or
classical MDS's own (also-unsuccessful, differently-shaped) attempt at the same
task, a genuinely informative null/mixed result rather than a forced
success story. As with its companions, this entire report is
reproducible end to end via =just org-example tsne_iris_analysis=, which
re-executes §3.3.13's assembly block -- and therefore every number and
figure above -- from the =datamunge= source tree.
