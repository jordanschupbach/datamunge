#+begin_abstract
The area of *any* simple polygon -- convex or not -- falls out of its corner coordinates
alone, with no triangulation and no trigonometry. The *shoelace formula* (Gauss's area
formula, the surveyor's formula) sums cross products of consecutive vertices; the pattern
of multiplications, drawn out, laces like a shoe. The *sign* of the result even encodes the
winding direction. This report drives =datamunge='s =geometry::signed_polygon_area= and
=polygon_area=, verified on shapes with known areas, and shades an irregular polygon by its
computed area.
#+end_abstract

* Lacing the coordinates together

For a polygon with vertices \((x_0,y_0),\dots,(x_{n-1},y_{n-1})\) in order, the signed
area is

\[ A = \frac12 \sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \bigl(x_{j}\,y_{i} - x_{i}\,y_{j}\bigr), \qquad j = i-1 \ (\text{mod } n). \]

Each term \(x_j y_i - x_i y_j\) is the cross product of two consecutive vertices -- twice
the signed area of the triangle they form with the origin. Summed around the boundary, the
triangle contributions *outside* the polygon cancel and only the enclosed area remains, no
matter where the origin sits or how non-convex the shape is. Two facts make it especially
useful:

- *Sign = winding.* \(A>0\) for counterclockwise vertices, \(A<0\) for clockwise; the
  unsigned area is \(|A|\).
- *Exactness.* For integer or exactly-represented coordinates the result is exact -- which is
  why it is the surveyor's and GIS standard for parcel areas.

It runs in a single \(O(n)\) pass over the vertices.

* Algorithm

#+begin_example
SIGNED-AREA(vertices):
  sum <- 0
  for i in 0..n-1, with j = i-1 (mod n):
      sum <- sum + (x[j]*y[i] - x[i]*y[j])
  return sum / 2                         # sign encodes winding; |.| is the area
#+end_example

* Worked example: known shapes, then an irregular polygon

We check the formula on a unit square (area 1, and \(-1\) when wound backwards) and a
right triangle (area 6), then measure a non-convex polygon and draw it.

#+name: sl-run
#+begin_src c++ :eval no
    const std::vector<Point2D> square{{0, 0}, {1, 0}, {1, 1}, {0, 1}};
    std::vector<Point2D> square_cw{{0, 0}, {0, 1}, {1, 1}, {1, 0}};
    const std::vector<Point2D> tri{{0, 0}, {4, 0}, {0, 3}};

    std::cout << "=== known areas ===\n";
    std::cout << "unit square (CCW): signed " << signed_polygon_area(square) << ", area " << polygon_area(square) << "\n";
    std::cout << "unit square (CW):  signed " << signed_polygon_area(square_cw) << ", area " << polygon_area(square_cw) << "\n";
    std::cout << "right triangle 4x3: area " << polygon_area(tri) << " (exact 6)\n\n";

    // A non-convex ("arrow"/chevron) polygon.
    const std::vector<Point2D> poly{{0, 0}, {6, 0}, {6, 4}, {3, 2}, {0, 4}};
    std::cout << "chevron polygon: area = " << polygon_area(poly) << "\n";

    std::vector<double> x, y;
    for (const Point2D& p : poly) { x.push_back(p.x); y.push_back(p.y); }

    auto plot = RPlot::create();
    plot.polygon(x, y, RGB{147, 197, 253}, true);   // shaded interior
    plot.polygon(x, y, RGB{37, 99, 235}, false);    // outline
    plot.points(x, y, "vertices", RGB{220, 38, 38}, 8.0);
    plot.legend({"polygon (area by shoelace)"}, {{37, 99, 235}});
    plot.title("Shoelace formula: area of a non-convex polygon from its corners")
        .x_label("x").y_label("y").background({250, 250, 252});
    plot.save_svg("build/org/shoelace.svg");
    std::cout << "wrote shoelace.svg\n";
#+end_src

* Results

#+begin_example
=== known areas ===

chevron polygon: area = 18

The unit square returns \(+1\) counterclockwise and \(-1\) clockwise -- the sign
flipping with the winding while the unsigned area stays \(1\) -- and the right triangle
gives exactly \(6\). The non-convex chevron, which no single triangle covers, is measured
correctly in one pass: the inward notch is subtracted automatically by the cancelling cross
products.
